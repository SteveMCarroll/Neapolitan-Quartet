Capitolo 46 / 53

Splendid and dark friendship

Summary

Lila's idea that Tina may have been taken by mistake, in place of Elena's daughter, upsets Elena deeply — not because she believes it, but because of the obscure feelings that must have produced it. Trying to make sense of them, she even remembers, after many years, the strange chain of accidents by which Lila named her daughter after Elena's beloved childhood doll, the same doll Lila had thrown into a cellar. Elena begins to fantasize about that connection, but quickly pulls back from the dark well it opens.

She concludes that every intense human bond is full of tagliole — traps — and that, if a relationship is to last, one must learn to avoid them. She treats this moment as another sign of how splendid and tenebrous her friendship with Lila has been, and how long, complicated, and still unfinished Lila's suffering is.

Yet Elena leaves for Turin convinced that Enzo was right: Lila is far from a quiet old age within the boundaries she has chosen. The last image Lila gives her is painfully physical. At fifty-one she looks ten years older, and while talking she is sometimes seized by hot flashes; she turns red, her neck blotches, her eyes go lost, and she fans herself with the hem of her dress, showing Elena and Imma her underwear.
