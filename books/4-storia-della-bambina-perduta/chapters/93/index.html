Capitolo 93 / 110

Public life accelerates

Summary

Lila's hostility sometimes hides behind helpfulness. She keeps taking care of Elena's daughters, but Elena hears in her tone a reminder that Elena's public life depends on Lila's sacrifice. If Elena proposes a babysitter, both Lila and Enzo are offended. Elena finally admits, sincerely, that she could not manage without Lila, then gives herself over to her commitments.

Thanks to the press office, Elena appears constantly in newspapers and sometimes on television. She is excited but tense, afraid of saying the wrong thing. She asks Lila what to do if journalists mention the Solaras, and Lila tells her that books are written per farsi sentire — "to make oneself heard." Elena remains cautious: she avoids naming Michele and Marcello or entering party politics, but speaks about neighborhood degradation, post-earthquake misery, false legality, institutional complicity, her education, misogyny at the Normale, her mother, her daughters, and women's thought. Soon she has newspaper work, a column, invitations, calls from famous writers, film interest, and foreign publishers reading the book.

Two unexpected calls please her especially. Adele warmly praises the improved book and asks Elena to bring the girls to Genoa, though Elena knows she will not. Then Nino calls, praises the novel extravagantly, and visits for lunch. He pays attention to Dede, Elsa, and Imma, then talks mostly about himself and his work with Pietro's father. Suddenly he asks to get back together. Elena laughs, says he may call to see Imma, but she and Nino have nothing left. He leaves sulking and forgets them again.
