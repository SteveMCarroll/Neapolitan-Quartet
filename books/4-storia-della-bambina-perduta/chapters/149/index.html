Capitolo 39 / 53

Innocent and guilty

Summary

Guido Airota never actually appears before the magistrates, but for days newspapers and magazines place him inside maps of corruption. Elena is relieved that Pietro, Dede, and Elsa are in America, away from the scandal, but she worries about Adele. Instead of calling Adele directly, she phones Mariarosa, thinking it will be easier.

It is not. Mariarosa, whom Elena has not heard from in years, is cold and sarcastic about Elena's public career. Then, unusually, she talks at length about herself: books, articles, travel, and the fact that she has left the university because it disgusted her. She says, almost bitterly, that she now lives on family wealth. When she turns to her father, she invokes Franco, who had understood early that without radical change worse times would come. Guido, she says, tried to change a little here and there, but anyone who changes little becomes trapped in the system of lies. Is Guido guilty? Yes — and utterly innocent, since he never pocketed an illicit lira.

That double judgment proves accurate. The scandal around Guido fades, but he remains, in Elena's mind, an innocent guilty man and a guilty innocent man. Adele later thanks Elena with irony and blames neoalfabetizzati — newly literate climbers — around Guido. That same evening television shows Nino, now named among the corrupt and the corrupters.
