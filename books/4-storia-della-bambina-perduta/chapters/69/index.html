Capitolo 69 / 110

A promise for autumn

Summary

Elena struggles to accept Immacolata's death. She does not cry, but the pain lasts. For weeks she sees and hears her mother everywhere, regrets that the tenderness discovered during illness has ended, and tries to prolong Immacolata through small objects: a hairpin, a handkerchief, scissors, the old bracelet. Even the hip pain revived by pregnancy becomes a relic she refuses to treat, a lascito ("legacy") kept in her own body.

Her mother's final words — "you are you, I trust you" — begin to help. Elena decides to prove Immacolata right. She returns to reading and writing in scraps of time, loses interest in petty party politics, follows Italy's corruption and violence, reads feminist work, and pitches articles to women's magazines. Yet her main energy goes into convincing her publisher that her new novel is nearly ready.

In truth, it is not. She has spent most of a large advance and still has no story. Then an article in the Corriere della Sera names her among the unfulfilled promises of young Italian literature. Soon the editorial director comes to Naples and asks to meet. Elena anxiously hosts him at via Tasso, partly because Nino hopes to propose a book of essays.

The dinner is strained. The director offers condolences, praises Imma, gives books to Dede and Elsa, then asks whether he can schedule Elena's novel for autumn 1982. He warns that readers must be cultivated or lost. Elena imagines Adele's hostility behind his severity. After he dismisses Nino's proposal, Elena yields: she will deliver the book in time.
