Capitolo 17 / 53

Who would you surrender to?

Summary

The good days do not last. Lila's body seems increasingly out of order after the operation: she is hot, cold, yellowish, flushed, sweaty, shouting, demanding. She quarrels with Carmen, finds Elsa unbearable, scolds Dede, treats Imma badly, and abruptly turns her back on Elena. When she cannot stay at home or in the office, she takes a bus or the metro and wanders Naples. If Elena asks where, Lila snaps that she owes her no account.

Lila especially fights with Gennaro/Rino, blaming his disagreements on Dede and Elsa. In fact Elena admits she is partly right: the girls are filling him with a permanent insubordination, which for them is clever verbal play but for him becomes confused, self-indulgent talk. Lila cannot bear slogans, sentimentality, or borrowed rebellion — though she herself still produces a theatrical anarchism Elena now finds misplaced.

The issue explodes when they read, during the 1987 election campaign, that Nadia Galiani has been arrested in Chiasso. Carmen panics that the police will also take Pasquale and kill him. Lila argues that Nadia likely surrendered to negotiate a lighter sentence. Elena suggests Pasquale should also surrender to the state, lo stato ("the state"). Lila erupts, listing decades of thefts and criminal complicities by ministers, police, magistrates, and secret services. Why, she demands, give Pasquale to that? Carmen, torn between them, asks whether Nadia will blame Pasquale to save herself and whether prison is still better than death. Lila insults them both and storms out.
