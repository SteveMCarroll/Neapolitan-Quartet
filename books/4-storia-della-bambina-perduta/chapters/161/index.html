Capitolo 51 / 53

Wanting Lila to last

Summary

Elena lets herself go for a while. She works very little, and no one at the publishing house or elsewhere asks more of her. She sees almost no one. Her main contact with the world is long phone calls to her daughters, during which she insists on speaking to the grandchildren in a childish voice. Elsa now has a son named Conrad, while Dede has given Hamid a little sister named Elena.

Those precise children's voices bring back Tina. In Elena's darkest moods, she becomes certain that Lila has written the detailed story of her lost daughter and mingled it with the story of Naples, perhaps with the ingenuità proterva — "stubborn naivety" — of an uneducated person who can achieve prodigious results. Then Elena recognizes the thought as fantasy, a mixture of anxiety, envy, hostility, and love.

She tells herself Lila has no such ambition, because ambition requires self-love, and Lila has always said she does not love herself. Elena even imagines that Lila wanted to erase herself, beginning with her refusal to leave the neighborhood while the world was losing its borders. Yet after pitying and mocking Lila's narrow experience, Elena writes again: come visit me. She concludes there will be no manuscript, and feels both relieved and sorry. She loves Lila and wants her to endure — but wants to be the one who makes her endure.
