Capitolo 3 / 53

Searching for Tina

Summary

No one in the rione trusts the police or journalists. For days and weeks everyone searches for Tina on their own. Nino phones only a couple of times, anxiously insisting he bears no responsibility because he had just handed the child to Lila and Enzo. Lila and Enzo rely especially on Antonio, who postpones leaving for Germany because, he surprisingly says, Michele Solara ordered him to help.

The Solaras make their involvement very visible. Marcello and Michele visit Lila and promise to do everything possible, but Enzo screams that they themselves took Tina because he and Lila refused them a share of Basic Sight's profits. The brothers do not contradict him. Their wives, Gigliola and Elisa, visit the next day, and the Solaras seem to organize searches of street vendors and Roma people, public outrage against police questioning Stefano, Rino, and Gennaro, and even vigilantes outside the elementary school.

When the rione tires, the theory of a hit-and-run truck gains force. Elena then meets Armando Galiani, now an aggressive private-TV journalist. He claims to be investigating post-earthquake building sites and a hastily scrapped truck. Lila agrees to see him, then tells him he is inventing everything and throws him out as a failed doctor, revolutionary, and journalist. Armando later tells Elena that vanity is a resource: Lila has none, and so lost her daughter. His truck story briefly makes national news, then collapses. Lila says Tina is alive and never wants to see that pezzo di merda — "piece of shit" — again.
