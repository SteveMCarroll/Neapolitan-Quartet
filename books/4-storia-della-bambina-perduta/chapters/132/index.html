Capitolo 22 / 53

The goodbye bed

Summary

Once Elena and Pietro are alone, she sees that he is deeply worried. Dede, he says, "functions by theorems": after her exams she will declare herself to Rino, lose her virginity, leave with him, and live by begging to challenge the work ethic. Elena accuses Pietro of fleeing and leaving her the role of bad mother, but he says Dede is counting on him too — she plans to bring Rino to Boston.

They talk until late, first about Dede, then Elsa and Imma, then politics, literature, Elena's books, and Pietro's work. Pietro teases her for always taking half-positions — half feminist, half Marxist, half Freudian, half Foucauldian — except with him. He recalls how harsh she was, how perfect Nino seemed, and how Nino has now ended in the Socialist gang. Elena lets him speak and admits he was right about Nino. When she turns back to Lila, Pietro says Lila is trying to occupy her mind and escape pain: she has told him she spends whole days in the National Library learning everything about Naples. Elena is unsettled that Lila hid this from her. Pietro suggests Lila may resent seeing Elena free while she remains trapped in her own unsatisfied head.

Elena denies that Lila hates her. Then, instead of preparing Pietro's usual cot, she lets him sleep with her. After twelve years without touching, they have a gentle farewell embrace, knowing it will not happen again. At dawn he leaves, again urging her to watch over the girls, especially Dede. Soon after, Lila phones from downstairs: the radio has announced that Pasquale has been arrested.
