Capitolo 11 / 110

Private life, public words

Summary

Back in Florence, Elena and Pietro discuss separation and quickly fight so violently that Adele shuts herself in with Dede and Elsa. The quarrel spills into the street because the girls' presence prevents them from going as far as they want. When Elena later complains, Adele turns on her for the first time, asking whether Elena sees how she has treated Pietro.

Elena leaves for France, guilty and loaded with books. On the exhausting train ride, her reading mixes with Nino, Pietro, her daughters, Carmen's defence of Pasquale, Antonio's warning, and Alfonso's transformation. Once in France, she rediscovers expansion. The tour is poor and tiring — constant trains, debates, private apartments, borrowed beds — but she succeeds.

Night after night, in French, she turns private experience into public reflection: poverty, family degradation, motherhood, women's subjection to men, and how love can drive a woman to wrong other women and children. She begins from the idea that she has been inventata dai maschi — invented by men — and uses Alfonso as an example of a man extracting a woman from himself. Only later does she notice how much she has drawn from the Solara shop, yet she never mentions Lila. Lila seems sunk in the neighborhood's lota — muck — while Elena can read chaos and its laws.

Before Christmas, her publishers keep her longer to arrange a reissue of her first novel. Nino sounds jealous of her work, and they arrange a rushed meeting in Rome. Their stolen night makes Elena miss her train home on Christmas Eve; Pietro laughs when she calls. She reaches Florence late, freezing and guilty, and finds the apartment dark, silent, perfectly ordered, and empty.
