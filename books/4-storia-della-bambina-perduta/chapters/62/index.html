Capitolo 62 / 110

Blood on the floor

Summary

Elena instinctively snatches Imma back from her mother as Immacolata faints. Nino catches the older woman, Lila orders an ambulance, but Nino insists it will be faster to take her by car. Elena, terrified by the blood and by her newborn's crying, lets Nino and Lila leave with her mother. Only after the door closes does she feel the wound: they are doing what she should be doing.

Alone, Elena nurses Imma and stares at the blood, imagining the car racing through cold Naples. She phones Elisa, minimizing the scene and mentioning Lila but not Nino. Elisa panics, insults her, and blames her for sending their mother off with "a stranger." Elena realizes Elisa is partly right: she yielded to Nino's male decisiveness, his urge to seem salvifico ("saving, heroic").

Lila later calls: Immacolata has been admitted, Nino knows the doctors, and everything is under control. Her practical warning that Elena must stay calm or se ne va il latte — "the milk will go away" — steadies Elena. Then Lila calls again: Marcello has arrived and is raging. He wants to move Immacolata to a private clinic in Capodimonte, threatens violence if anyone stops him, and demands to know who is in charge. Elena orders Nino home and leaves Lila to handle Marcello.
