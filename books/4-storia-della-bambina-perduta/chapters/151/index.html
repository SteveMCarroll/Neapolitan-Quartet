Capitolo 41 / 53

Zia Lina guides Imma

Summary

Lila shows no real interest in Nino's legal troubles. She treats the news almost as a joke, remembering that whenever he needed money he got it from Bruno Soccavo and surely never paid it back. She imagines his downfall as the result of vanity: smiling, shaking hands, needing to seem brilliant, wanting to rise higher and higher. After that she behaves as if the former deputy Sarratore no longer exists. She had worked intensely for Pasquale and Enzo, but Nino's fate leaves her indifferent.

What revives instead, for no clear reason, is Lila's interest in Imma. While Lila's own son Rino has attached himself to Elena like a dog devoted to a new mistress, Lila again binds herself to Elena's daughter, and Imma, always hungry for affection, returns the feeling. Lila takes her out: the Botanical Garden, the Museum, Capodimonte.

During Elena's last period in Naples, these walks give Imma a lasting curiosity about the city. Imma says admiringly that zia Lina — Aunt Lina — knows countless things. Elena is grateful because Lila's outings soften Imma's anguish over Nino, her anger at schoolmates' cruel insults, and her loss of special status among teachers. But Elena also learns from Imma that Lila's secret writing is not about one monument or another: it is about Naples as a whole, a vast project Lila has never mentioned to Elena.
