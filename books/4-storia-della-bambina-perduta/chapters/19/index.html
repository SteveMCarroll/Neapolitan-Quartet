Capitolo 19 / 110

The wider world

Summary

Elena notices for the first time how rigidly Lila has limited her own perimeter. Lila cares less and less about what happens outside the neighborhood unless it touches people she has known since childhood. Even work seems to interest her only within a narrow radius. Enzo sometimes travels to Milan or Turin, but Lila never moves. Elena understands this more sharply as she herself begins to relish travel.

She takes every chance to go abroad, especially with Nino. During a promotional tour through West Germany and Austria, he happily drives her from event to event. Their landscapes, hotels, food, fear, and politics become material for their happiness. One night German police stop them at gunpoint, separate them, interrogate them, and release them only at dawn because they are Italians with Italian plates. In the station Elena looks anxiously at mug shots for Pasquale and Nadia and thinks of Pasquale as a rocket shot from Lila's tiny world into Elena's larger one.

At readings, foreign audiences ask about Italy's political violence. Elena relies on old revolutionary vocabulary, centering on reprimere ("to repress"), and sometimes tells Pasquale's story concretely. Yet she has learned caution from Nino, avoiding the word revolution and speaking instead of rebellion, consensus, and governing. With women audiences she still sharpens her tone, then Nino teases her for exaggerating.

At night Elena admits to Nino that she likes subversive words but is unsure what she truly believes. In Bologna, repeated police checks frighten her into dialect insults, while Nino stays calm and charms the officers. He tells her that only the two of them matter; the rest is background and will change.
