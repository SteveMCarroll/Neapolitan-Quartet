Capitolo 70 / 110

No one must stop me

Summary

Elena's promise calms the editorial director. He stays another hour, tries to be warmer to Nino, and leaves whispering to Elena that he is sure she is writing a beautiful story. Once the door closes, Elena bursts out that Adele is still waging war on her and that she is in trouble.

Nino disagrees. The faint possibility of his own book being published has soothed him, and he has recently met Guido and Adele in Palermo at a Socialist Party congress. Professor Airota seemed to appreciate Nino's latest work, so Nino advises Elena not to exaggerate Airota conspiracies. They quarrel. Elena has promised a novel, but how can she write it with any real continuity? She lists the recent burdens: Immacolata's illness and death, Dede and Elsa, housework, pregnancy, Imma's birth, Nino's lack of interest in the baby, his trips to conferences and congresses, and the disgust of still sharing him with Eleonora. Elena is nearing divorce from Pietro, while Nino has not even separated.

The scene changes nothing. Nino grows gloomy, says she is unfair, swears he loves her and the girls, and offers to pay for a domestic worker. This time Elena immediately accepts. She hears in herself not her mother's weak dying voice but her old combative one. She must think of her future: a very good novel, written in a few months, and nothing — not even Nino — must keep her from doing her work.
