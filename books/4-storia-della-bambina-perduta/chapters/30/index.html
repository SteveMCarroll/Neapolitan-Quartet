Capitolo 30 / 110

Life on Via Tasso

Summary

Nino has truly rented the apartment on via Tasso. Elena moves in immediately, though it is full of ants and barely furnished. She tells him that Franco largely determined her decision, then brings one good piece of news and one bad one: her publisher will consider Nino's essays if he rewrites them less dryly, but she does not want him to touch her.

That resolve soon weakens. They spend evenings side by side revising his work, and proximity dissolves anger. Eleonora is still pregnant when Elena and Nino become lovers again; by the time Eleonora gives birth to Lidia, they have resumed the habits of a couple with a house, Dede, Elsa, and an active public life. Elena tells herself she is only taking what she needs, as Franco advised, until Nino's attraction is exhausted. But she knows this is a messinscena — a staged performance — and that she is hiding darker truths from herself.

Naples quickly strains her. Via Tasso is inconvenient, traffic defeats the used white R4 Nino finds her, daily life is harder than in Florence, Genoa, or Milan, and both girls hate school. They begin idealizing Adele and Mariarosa, resisting Elena's attempts at affection. Elena wonders whether she has sacrificed her work and her daughters to Nino while publicly pretending to be a free, lucid woman.

Still, whenever Nino knocks, regret vanishes. The bright house, the sea view, dialect in the streets, and Nino's intellectual guests cheer her. Pietro visits the girls, and Elena tries to keep their separated family civil.
