Capitolo 2 / 2

Tina and Nu return

Summary

Elena describes her ordinary old-age routine in Turin: breakfast at seven, a walk to the newsstand with her new Labrador, and mornings in the Valentino park with the dog and the newspapers. Yesterday, coming home, she found a badly wrapped packet of newspaper above her mailbox. There was no note and no name, so nothing proved it was meant for her.

When she opened one side of the package, Tina and Nu sprang out of memory before they were fully free of the paper. They were the dolls that, almost sixty years earlier, Lila and Elena had thrown into the neighborhood cellar, the dolls they never found. They were the dolls Lila had pushed Elena to seek at Don Achille's house; he had denied taking them and perhaps suspected Alfonso, compensating the girls with money. Instead of buying new dolls, they bought Piccole donne — Little Women — the book that led Lila to write La fata blu and Elena to become the author of many books, especially Un'amicizia.

Elena longs for Lila to appear and say mockingly, ti piace questo regalo? — "do you like this gift?" No one comes, and Elena cries. Maybe Lila has deceived and used her all along; maybe the dolls simply say she is alive and loves her. Elena takes them upstairs, sets them against her books, and accepts that now Lila has shown herself clearly, Elena must resign herself to never seeing her again.
