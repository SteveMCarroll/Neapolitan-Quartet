Capitolo 5 / 110

A book in France

Summary

On the drive to Paris, Elena clings to Nino and again feels herself expanding beyond her old borders. She is traveling toward a book she wrote in Italian but that, thanks to Mariarosa, is appearing first in another language. She imagines it as a stone of her own launched far beyond the childhood stones she and Lila once threw at boys.

The journey also sours. Nino seems too intimate with Colombe, touching her shoulder and speaking in a tone he does not use with Augustin. In their hosts' Canal Saint-Martin apartment, Elena becomes tired, jealous, and frightened that Nino is slipping away. He answers sharply that Colombe cannot please him because he loves Elena. During the night Elena half-wakes, sensing him absent or standing in the dark, but sinks back into sleep.

At Nanterre, Nino's seductiveness extends to the two women publishers, which oddly reassures Elena: it is not special to Colombe, he behaves this way with women. The publishers celebrate Elena. Her volume is already in bookshops, with two enthusiastic reviews by women critics and plans for promotion. Elena should be ecstatic, but love has made success feel secondary, merely a effetto secondario — a pleasant side effect. News of her break with Pietro has reached France; she decides that this may help make the separation final, and tells herself that Nino, Dede, and Elsa will all somehow be set right.
