Capitolo 26 / 110

Lessons in Sant&#39;Ambrogio

Summary

Elena lives those days in self-disgust, unable to tear Nino out of her head. She works, travels, returns, and suffers mechanically, while feeling that Lila had been right: she is neglecting Dede and Elsa, leaving them without proper care or school.

The girls, however, are enchanted by Mariarosa's apartment in Sant'Ambrogio, a chaotic porto di mare ("seaport," a place of constant comings and goings). Mariarosa welcomes everyone — troubled women, wanderers, people marked by crime or drugs — and worries less about bodies than about minds. She plays with the girls, cleans with them, and organizes evening lessons full of wit and information, often involving her nieces directly.

One evening the police arrive after neighbours complain. Mariarosa calmly invites the two officers inside and resumes the lesson. Dede, fascinated by seeing a policeman up close, explains with great seriousness that her aunt is in realtà ("actually") a professor of art history, impressing the young officer with how well she speaks.

Later Elena herself gives one of these lessons. Her daughters sit in front and listen obediently, perhaps for the first time seeing their mother as someone other people admire. But Mariarosa unexpectedly attacks Elena's ideas, recounting Elena's clash with her mother and arguing that a woman without love for her matrice — her source, her mother — is lost.
