Capitolo 24 / 110

A heap of debris

Summary

The atmosphere in the Airota home grows unbearable. Nino keeps calling; Elena either hangs up or fights loudly with him. Lila calls too, asking how things are, and Elena snaps that everything is fine before cutting her off. She becomes irritable with Dede and Elsa, and especially with Adele.

One morning Elena accuses Adele of trying to block her book. Adele does not deny it; she calls the work an opuscolo ("pamphlet"), unworthy of being a real book. Elena answers that Adele has never managed even pamphlets and has no right to authority. Soon after, during another dialect quarrel with Nino, Adele reproaches Elena contemptuously. Elena retaliates with what Pietro once told her: Adele had lovers. Unlike Elena, who has taken public responsibility and pays the consequences, Adele is a hypocritical little bourgeois hiding her dirt under the carpet.

Adele turns pale. Quietly she calls Elena a bad woman, incapable of understanding love or renunciation, and says that under Elena's apparent meekness lies a vulgar urge to grab everything. She orders Elena and the girls to leave the next day. Elena knows she has gone too far but does not apologize. The next morning she packs alone and leaves without greeting Guido.

At the station, exhausted and watched suspiciously by her daughters, Elena first thinks of going to Naples and invading Nino and Eleonora's home with her chaos. Then an announcement makes her change direction for Milan, where she can beg the publisher for work. On the train she realizes why: despite everything, she still loves Nino more than her own daughters. Without Lila and without Nino as a model, she feels like un mucchio di detriti — a heap of debris. She arrives, terrified, at Mariarosa's.
