Capitolo 48 / 53

The wish to erase

Summary

In Elena and Lila's phone calls, the dead become a way to speak of the living. Fernando dies, then Nunzia, and Lila moves with Rino into the old apartment where she was born, which she had bought with her own money; now her siblings claim it belonged to their parents and demand their shares. Stefano dies of another heart attack, and Marisa leaves the neighborhood with her children. Nino, finally helpful, finds her secretarial work and sends money for the boys' university. Elena also learns from Lila, not from Elisa, her father, or her brothers, that Elisa has left for Caserta and remarried.

Their conversations exchange news: Elena's daughters, Pietro's new marriage, Elena's writing and publishing job, Lila's neighborhood updates. Only twice does Elena press the question that obsesses her. If Lila ever wrote something, would she let Elena read and publish it? Lila denies writing: she only uses the computer to go online and learn about electronics. Since she never even answers Elena's emails, she says, that proves it.

Then Lila explains that writing means wanting something to survive you. She has no such desire; if she could cancellarmi — erase herself — she would. From the late 1990s on, this wish to erase becomes a mocking refrain, not suicide but an aesthetic project. She hates the traces electronics leave everywhere. Even a famous name, she says of Elena Greco, is only a ribbon around a random bag of flesh, blood, words, and waste. Yet sometimes, denying the manuscript, Lila sounds vividly creative, and this secret verbal power begins to sadden Elena.
