Guido Airota

Pietro's father, a socialist professor whose moral authority erodes.

Family: Airota

Guido Airota enters book 4 less as the generous intellectual father Elena once admired than as one of the aging men whose authority no longer convinces her. He is cold during the family crisis, dismissive when Elena leaves Genoa, and sharply critical of Nino, whom he sees as wavering, unrooted, and politically unreliable.

Late in the novel Guido is caught in the newspapers' maps of corruption around the Socialist world. Mariarosa's judgment gives Elena the right formula: he has not pocketed illicit money, yet he has benefited from a system of lies. Guido ends the quartet as an "innocent guilty man," a patriarch whose clean hands no longer mean clean power.

This page reflects what's known to a reader who has finished book 4. Earlier knowledge from book 3 is summarized briefly; no events from later books appear here.
