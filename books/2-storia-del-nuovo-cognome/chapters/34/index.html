Capitolo 34 / 125

Nadia is Nino&#39;s girlfriend

Summary

Stefano drives them to the building and out of the convertible — overdressed, doused in shaving soap, hoping in vain to be invited up. The girls don't invite him. They go upstairs (avoiding the unfamiliar elevator), looking for the brass plate "Dott. Prof. Frigerio", locating it instead by the volume of music behind the door. Elena's heart hammers; both of them smooth their skirts and hair, afraid of "escaping themselves."

The door is opened by a dark, short, lively young man with a beautiful, alert face: Armando, Galiani's son. Before Elena can introduce herself he laughs: Elena? In this house we all know you. Our mother won't stop torturing us by reading your essays aloud. That sentence is decisive. Mi diede un'improvvisa sensazione di potenza. The fear evaporates: she has been preceded by her good reputation, she doesn't have to earn the room, sono gli altri a dover faticare per entrare nelle tue grazie. She forgets even to introduce Lila, who closes the door behind them in silence.

The flat is enormous, every room open and lit, ceilings high with floral cornices, walls of books taller than the library in the rione. There's a terrace with vermouth, pasta reale, jasmine, the dark bay below the city's lights. Galiani greets Elena warmly, turns to the silent Lila — a friend of yours? — and Elena introduces her as "Lina, my elementary-school classmate." Galiani's eye drops to the wedding ring on Lila's finger; Lila covers it with the other hand. You're married? Same age as Elena? Older by two weeks.

Back in the dance room the lights come up between songs. A boy is lighting a cigarette and the match flame jumps into his face. It's Nino Sarratore — older, taller, more dishevelled, more beautiful, just back from England. And the girl he has just stopped dancing with is the bell ragazza fine, luminosa Elena had spotted last spring outside school. Eccola, says Armando. Nadia — Nino's girlfriend — is Galiani's daughter.
