Capitolo 39 / 125

An errand from Fernando

Summary

Before she can avoid him, Fernando Cerullo — Lila's father — appears at her elbow and asks her, formally, to dance. They turn a silent waltz, his sweating hand crushing hers. At the end he mutters, switching strangely to the formal voi: Se non vi porta troppo disturbo, parlate un poco con Lina, la madre è preoccupata. He follows it up with the bargain: When you need a pair of shoes, come to me, no charge. Elena leaves angrily — payment for time with Lila — and feels Nunzia's eyes on her all the way out.

Through the next weeks Elena's confidence sags. The bookshop owner turns out to be a sweating, leering man who keeps her on a stepladder and makes her unload boxes; Armando has stopped coming by; Nino has vanished. Without Galiani feeding her papers, the world becomes "a foggy moor" again. She broods about her stupid breakup with Antonio — l'unica persona che mi avesse veramente amato — and re-runs the smell of his skin, the pond evenings, the kissing in the old tomato cannery.

One evening Pasquale, Carmen, and Ada come to find her — Pasquale's hand is bandaged from a work accident. They get ice cream in the little public garden. Carmen and Ada lay into her: if you cared about somebody you'd find time. You're senza sentimenti, look how you treated my brother. Pasquale steers her past the blame: it's not whose fault, it's that we have to be there for Lina. He explains. Lila has been quietly paying for his dental work, for Carmen's family, for Antonio's expenses — and Antonio in the army la sta vedendo assai brutta, has had a kind of attacco di nervi. The family wants to take Lila to a doctor — Stefano, Pinuccia, all the in-laws — to find out why she got pregnant once and never again. Lila refuses, fa la pazza. Portacela tu, says Carmen.
