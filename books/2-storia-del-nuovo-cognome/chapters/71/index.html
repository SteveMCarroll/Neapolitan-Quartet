Capitolo 71 / 125

Sulla spiaggia dei Maronti

Summary

Donato has been sent by Lidia and Nella to find her. He sits beside Elena and murmurs in his lyrical-seducer register that he knows her as he knows himself, that they share la stessa sensibilità per le cose belle, la stessa necessità di cercare le parole giuste. Elena hears the ridicule of it — il liricume dietro cui celava la smania di mettermi le mani addosso — but a different calculation runs underneath: forse siamo fatti davvero della stessa pasta, forse siamo davvero condannati senza colpa alla stessa identica mediocrità. She leans her head on his shoulder and whispers ho un po' freddo. He puts an arm around her waist, lifts her chin between thumb and finger, kisses her lightly, asks così come va?, then keeps going, piccoli baci sempre meno leggeri. His mouth is warm and wet; she lets him in, con gratitudine crescente.

The cold thaws. Elena feels something she hasn't felt before — che le mani di lui toglievano via il freddo ma piano, come se fosse fatto di strati sottilissimi e Sarratore avesse l'abilità di tirarli via con precisione cauta, a uno a uno, senza lacerarli. There is, she discovers, una me nascosta — capii — che dita, bocca, denti, lingua sapevano scovare. Donato, a differenza di Antonio, demands nothing of her hands; he confines himself to proving how thoroughly he knows women. When Elena is travolta da una necessità di piacere così esigente e così egocentrica da cancellare even his own body and all his labels — padre di Nino, ferroviere-poeta-giornalista, Donato Sarratore — he notices, and he penetrates her. The first thrust is a strappo nella pancia at once erased by rhythm; then he pulls out abruptly, falls back onto the sand, and emits una sorta di ruggito strozzato.

The sea returns, the cielo tremendo returns. Donato tries to lead her back to herself with tender words; she tolerates two sentences, then sits up sharply, shakes sand from her hair, dresses, makes herself orderly. When he asks dove possiamo incontrarci domani? she answers in Italian, con una voce calma piena di me: he is mistaken, he is not to look for her again — not at Cetara, not in the rione. He gives a sceptical smile. She tells him plainly that what Antonio Cappuccio could do to him is nothing next to what Michele Solara would do — she knows Michele well, one word from her and se la vedesse brutta. In particular, she says, Michele has been waiting for an excuse to break Donato's face perché aveva preso soldi per scrivere del negozio di piazza dei Martiri ma non aveva fatto bene il suo lavoro. All the way home she keeps threatening him in Italian, half because he relapses into frasette mielose, half because she is genuinely surprised at how well the menacing register — a thing she had used only in dialect since childhood — sits on her tongue anche in lingua italiana.
