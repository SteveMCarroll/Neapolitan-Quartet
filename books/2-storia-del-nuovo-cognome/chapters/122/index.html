Capitolo 122 / 125

La fata blu: the secret heart of the book

Summary

Back home a bad piece of news is waiting. Her mother — who routinely opens Elena's post — has opened a parcel from Potenza containing some of Elena's elementary‑school notebooks and a note from maestra Oliviero's sister. The maestra has died serenamente twenty days ago; she had often spoken of Elena toward the end and left instructions that some of her preserved primary‑school quaderni be returned. Elena is moved more than Elisa, who weeps inconsolably for hours — which annoys her mother, who barks at Elisa and then comments aloud, for Elena's benefit, «quella cretina s'è sempre creduta più madre di me».

All day Elena thinks of Oliviero, of how proud she would have been to learn of the centodieci e lode and the book. After everyone's asleep she opens the notebooks in the silent kitchen and leafs through them. Che bella grafia mi aveva dato. She laughs at the misspellings struck through con tratti furiosi, at the buono, ottimo annotated cavillosamente a lato. Mi era stata davvero più madre di mia madre? Maybe; but she had imagined a road her mother couldn't and made her walk it, e per questo le ero grata.

She's about to put the bundle away when she notices a thin fascicoletto tucked inside one notebook: una decina di fogli a quadretti fissati con uno spillo e ripiegati. A sudden hollow in her chest. She recognises La fata blu — Lila's story, written tanti anni prima, quanti?, tredici, quattordici. She remembers the copertina colorata coi pastelli, the carefully drawn title, the awe and envy she felt seeing it. She opens it. The pin has rusted and stained the paper brown. Mi accorsi con meraviglia che la maestra aveva scritto a lato di una frase: bellissimo. So Oliviero had read it — and liked it. Every page is filled with brava, bene, benissimo. Vecchia strega, Elena thinks furiously, perché non ci hai detto che ti era piaciuto, perché hai negato a Lila quella soddisfazione? What were the scontenti of yours that you offloaded onto her?

She reads La fata blu from the beginning. By the end of the first page her stomach hurts; she breaks into a sweat. By the last she has admitted what she'd grasped after a few lines: le paginette infantili di Lila erano il cuore segreto del mio libro. Whoever wanted to know cosa gli dava calore e da dove nasceva il filo robusto ma invisibile che saldava le frasi would have to look at that little fascicolo di bambina, ten paginette di quaderno, lo spillo arrugginito, the brightly coloured cover, the title — and not even the signature.
