Capitolo 2 / 125

Marcello in Lila&#39;s shoes

Summary

We jump back to the wedding reception at the close of book 1. Elena cannot believe what she has just seen: Stefano — so gentle, so in love — has handed Marcello Solara the unmistakable proof of Lila's girlhood labour, the prototype shoes she invented. The music, the drunken laughter of her mother, the voices around her, Alfonso and Marisa flirting at her table, even the image of Nino Sarratore (who has just left the room "like an archangel without an annunciation") all fade out. Elena's whole field of vision narrows to Lila and Stefano at the head table.

Lila — pale, in her wedding gown — is hissing into Stefano's ear, both hands gripping his arm. Stefano's face is a Carnival-mask of unease, a chalky stain that runs from his forehead to his eyes. Watching her friend tug at the new husband as if she would wrench his arm out of its socket and use it as a club to smash Marcello's face, Elena has a brief, ecstatic vision of total destruction: the marriage exploding, the rione in pieces, herself and Lila running away together to live in unknown cities, descending the steps of degradation side by side. She realises, frightened, how much she wants it. And realises immediately afterwards that this kind of explosion is something only Lila could ever pull off — Elena herself can only sit still and quietly nurse her resentment.

Lila stands so abruptly that the table shakes and a wine glass tips over Signora Solara's dress. While Stefano scrambles to dam the spreading wine, Lila is already heading for a side door, gathering her skirt where it snags. Elena thinks of running after her, whispering away, away from here — but doesn't move. Stefano, after a moment's hesitation, follows his wife through the dancing couples.

The whole room has noticed that something is wrong with the bride. But Marcello goes on chatting with Rino as if it were perfectly normal that he should be wearing those shoes. The metal-trader's toasts grow filthier. The guests at the lowest-ranking tables doggedly keep smiling. Nobody, in short, except Elena, seems to understand that this marriage — which would otherwise drag on through children, grandchildren, silver and golden anniversaries — is already, for Lila, over.
