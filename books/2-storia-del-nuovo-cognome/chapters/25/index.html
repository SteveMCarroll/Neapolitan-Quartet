Capitolo 25 / 125

The photo defaced

Summary

Lila opens the new salumeria with deliberate provocation: she dresses deliberately badly, treats Stefano as a servant in front of guests, sends the priest packing without the blessing while pressing money into his hand. She hires Carmela Peluso at a wage well above what Carmela was getting at the haberdasher. She slices prosciutto and hands out free wine and panini — opening day is a riot of customers. That night Stefano explodes; she goads him into worse insults; he doesn't hit her (since she became pregnant, he won't). She tells Elena, ringing thumb against forefinger: capisce solo questi, he understands only this.

A few days before the Piazza dei Martiri opening she shows up at Elena's house with feverish eyes. I've found a solution. If they want the photo, they have to do it my way. That evening she drags the whole cast — Stefano, Pinuccia, Gigliola, Rino, Marcello, Michele — to the unfinished shop. She has Marcello and Michele lay the giant bridal photo flat on the floor. She finds a roll of black cardboard and a pair of large scissors, and, working with the same absolute focus Elena remembers from their childhood drawing games, cuts strips and pins them across the image until most of her own body is occluded. What survives: a single eye; the hand that holds the chin; the lit mouth; diagonal stripes of bodice; the line of crossed legs; the shoes.

The reactions split. Gigliola: I'm not putting that in my shop. Pinuccia: people will run away — it's a mamozio. Rino, scolding Stefano. Elena, recruited by Stefano, says it's beautiful — like the Brazzi house in Confidenze. Michele, who has been silent, suddenly takes the room: I like it, signó. You wiped yourself out on purpose to show the leg, to show how good a leg looks in those shoes. You're a pain in the ass but you do things to the last millimeter. He turns on his fiancée Gigliola to shut her up. Stefano, surprised, agrees. Lila announces it isn't finished: Ci devo mettere un poco di colore. Three days to opening. We'll wait, Michele says. Do whatever you want.
