Capitolo 51 / 125

Hiroshima vs Pearl Harbor

Summary

Monday morning, quasi un fantasma evocato dal mio desiderio, Nino appears not at four but at ten, alone. He kisses Elena on the cheek — un bacio forte, rumoroso — and then, after a beat, remembering himself, posso dare un bacio anche a te? to Lila, a lighter peck. He launches into the Beckett. He has read it. The buried-up-to-the-neck people, the line about life being more vita when stripped of sight, sound and speech: aboliamo tutti i filtri che ci impediscono di gustare appieno questo stare hic et nunc, veri. Lila answers, with a flicker of revulsion: la vita senza vedere e senza dire, senza una veste, senza un contenitore, è sformata. Nino repeats sformata under his breath come se il vocabolo fosse una mala parola. Then he yanks off his shirt, grabs both of them by the hand, and drags them shrieking into the water — ecco finalmente un altro giorno divino.

The week that follows is the high water mark of the summer. Da quel momento, the two boys arrive at ten not four; they stay till sunset; they merge into one group of five. The distances cede. Bruno calls Lila signora Carracci to tease her; she chases him with mock fists. Nino puts an arm round Elena's shoulder, then — without thinking — around Lila's, takes her indice, pollice. They play tamburelli, pegni, baci per scherzo; they let Pinuccia win because she gets so worked up she forgets her stato. Elena no longer minds that Lila is stealing her books. Non mi dispiacque nemmeno che, quando si accendevano le discussioni, lei dicesse sempre più spesso la sua e Nino l'ascoltasse con attenzione.

But there is a tic in Nino that Elena starts to read carefully: when Lila beats him in argument, he stops addressing her at all and turns to Elena come se questo lo aiutasse a ritrovare le sue convinzioni. It happens during the Hiroshima debate. Lila — fueled by the stolen book — calls the atomic bomb un crimine della superbia, peggio delle decimazioni naziste; she demands that the Americans be tried come i peggiori tra i criminali. Nino, who is sympathetic to American mass-culture and the modo di vita there, parries with ti ricordo Pearl Harbor. Elena doesn't even know what Pearl Harbor is, but Lila does and hits back: Pearl Harbor era un atto di guerra vile, Hiroshima era uno scervellato ferocissimo vendicativo orrore. Cornered, Nino turns to Elena and quietly continues — come se lei non ci fosse — that the bomb was the only way to end the worst of all wars, and (proper Nino move) all future wars. He looks her in the eye. It is unbearably beautiful. Mi salivano le lacrime agli occhi e faticavo a rimandarle giù. By Friday, walking home, Pinuccia without warning throws her bag down at the side of the road, sits in the dirt and begins to keen, piccole grida sottili, quasi un uggiolare: non sopporto questo costume bagnato. Lila narrows her eyes, registering qualcosa di brutto a cui non era preparata, and walks on without waiting.
