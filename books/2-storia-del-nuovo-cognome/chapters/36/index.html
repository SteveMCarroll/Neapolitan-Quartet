Capitolo 36 / 125

Parrots in the car

Summary

Years later Elena will read in Lila's notebooks how that night actually felt inside her — confessed: she had been the one who asked to come along, hoping to step out of the salumeria for an evening and "share the brusque widening of my world"; she'd been sure she would not look ridiculous, sure that, as always, the men would notice her. Instead she had felt senza voce, sgraziata, priva di bellezza. Everyone — even when they were standing side by side — spoke only to Elena. Armando spent fifteen minutes showing Elena a seventeenth-century family painting; Lila might as well not have been in the room. That night it had become clear, for the first time, that her life would always be Stefano, the delicatessens, the petty war with the Solaras.

But in the convertible going home she gives Elena none of that. She turns vicious. When Stefano sourly asks if they had fun she answers, in dialect, that she has never been so bored in her life. She caresses Stefano's hand on the gear stick as a small public reminder that she has a man and Elena has none. She attacks the Galiani household: nothing in that flat was earned by them — the furniture is a century old, the books are old law and medicine and politics, those people hanno letto e studiato for generations and yet non tengono nella testa nemmeno un pensiero che è loro. She mimics them as parrots saying cocoricò cocoricò: chimps that pee in toilets, who give themselves airs about Cuba and Algeria and the Katanga. Pure tu, Lena: stai diventando la pappagalla dei pappagalli. She mocks Elena's new italianate accent — ciuciù, ciuciù — mocks Nino — te lo ricordi come sapeva risolvere i problemi il figlio di Sarratore? — and asks whether Elena is now content to be a pupazza of the rione going on stage for that kind of people while the rest of them stay here in our shit.

Elena absorbs the venom in silence. Stefano, dropping her off, murmurs Lina scherza. As the car pulls away Lila shouts after her, mimicking the "fake voice" Elena supposedly used at Galiani's: Ciao, eh, ciao.
