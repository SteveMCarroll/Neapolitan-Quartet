Capitolo 92 / 125

Nino runs, and Antonio beats him in the rain

Summary

Elena warns that what follows she pieced together later, from different people in different times. She starts with Nino. He leaves the Campi Flegrei and runs home to his parents. Lidia receives him meglio del figliuol prodigo; Donato instantly picks a fight, shouting in dialect that he can come or stay but he cannot disappear for a month and then turn up only per fottersi soldi as if he'd earned them. Nino retreats to his old room and argues with himself for a day and a night. Part of him wants to run back to Lila that minute, kiss her, accept his responsibilities. Another part has decided he's been pulled into a trap — not her fault, not his, but the desire's. Lina non è adatta a me, Lina è incinta, mi fa paura quello che c'è nella sua pancia. He must, he decides, not go back; he should run to Bruno, borrow money, leave Naples like Elena, study elsewhere.

The argument keeps swinging — now the need for her, now icy notes on her ingenuità maleducate, her ignoranza troppo intelligente, the way she shoves him into thoughts that look like insights and turn out to be reckless lunges. That evening he calls Bruno and bolts out to the bus stop in the rain. He even catches the right bus, but somewhere between piazza Garibaldi and Campi Flegrei the want for Lila wins. He gets off, switches to the metro, decides he'll grab her against the entrance wall the moment he's inside the flat and worry about the rest later.

It's dark. He's striding through the rain, eyes ahead, and doesn't see the figure coming at him until he's slammed to the ground. What follows is a long calm beating — kicks and punches, kicks and punches — and a steady, dispassionate voice repeating in dialect: «Lasciala, non la vedere e non la toccare più. Ripeti: io la lascio. Ripeti: io non la vedo e non la tocco più. Omm' e mmerd: ti piace, eh, prenderti le femmine degli altri. Ripeti: ho sbagliato, la lascio». Nino, obedient, repeats every line. The attacker doesn't stop. He passes out — more from terror than from pain.
