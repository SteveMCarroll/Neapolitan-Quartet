Capitolo 11 / 125

The bath, the projector, the film

Summary

Elena starts spending nearly every afternoon at Lila's apartment. She eats prosciutto sandwiches, has her own room to work in, even takes baths — the first proper hot bath of her life, with foam, a mirror that doesn't distort, and a hair dryer. Maybe this is the wealth we wanted as children, she thinks: not gold coins in chests, but a bathtub, a fridge full of food, a telephone. That night at the ponds Antonio sees right through her exhilaration: you always want what Lila has. They fight; she runs off offended.

Studying with Lila reveals a strange new thing. Lila no longer dominates the books. She second-guesses herself — forse non ho capito, controlla tu. But Elena keeps noticing the old quickness: half a page of philosophy and Lila is already linking Anaxagoras to Mendeleev. When she catches herself doing this she retreats: basta, ho da cucinare. She has become a woman with nothing to do but order groceries by phone (pronto, sono la signora Carracci), buy hair dryers, buy albums, sign her new name on receipts as if practising penmanship.

The chapter's centerpiece is the wedding film. Lila buys a projector, hangs a sheet, and runs the reel for Elena. Elena is struck twice: once by herself (she looks elegant and content at Nino's table; gawky beside Antonio), and once by what the camera catches when Nino leaves the reception — he and Marcello brushing shoulders in opposite directions, Marcello swelling with menace, Nino walking through him without seeing him. He doesn't even know the hierarchy that puts the Solaras on top. Lila finally notices Nino: that's the one you kissed on Ischia? — yes — he's too thin, ugly, and full of himself. Let him go, Antonio is better. Inwardly Elena fights to keep herself from defending him out loud and says only: "I liked him at elementary school, not anymore."
