Capitolo 23 / 125

At the Sea Garden; Lila is pregnant

Summary

Elena's summer at the Sea Garden begins, with the cartolaia's three daughters in tow and a stack of philosophy from Galiani — Rousseau's Origine della disuguaglianza on top. One Sunday Marisa Sarratore sneaks up and covers Elena's eyes. Behind her is Alfonso, sun-pale in a black bathing suit; he and Marisa are now an item, finally public. Marisa reports on Nino's maturità: tutti otto e due nove, all 8s with two 9s; the moment results were posted he left, alone, broke, for England — to find work and learn the language, then maybe study economics in the autumn.

Then Alfonso says, awkwardly: Sta per arrivare pure Lina. Antonio drove them. Antonio. He has something he has to tell you, Marisa adds, clapping. Antonio appears unsmiling, dives in with the girls, won't talk to Elena. Lila sits down beside her, picks up the Rousseau, and asks bitterly Sai tu quello che mi interessa e che non mi interessa? — do you know what interests me and what doesn't? They tussle over books — Elena boasts that this is the kind of thing Galiani gives the bright kids, the kind Nino reads. Penso ai due di noi che potremmo fare le stesse cose: viaggiare, fare la cameriera, imparare l'inglese meglio degli inglesi. Perché lui sì e noi no?

Lila smooths the book's cover and finally says it: Sono incinta. I'm pregnant. Before Elena can respond she shouts che caldo, runs to the edge of the platform, and throws herself in calling Tonì, salvami! — she can't swim. Antonio catches her. The chapter ends mid-fall, mid-fear.
