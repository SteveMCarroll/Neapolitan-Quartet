Capitolo 15 / 125

At the Solara bar

Summary

The following Sunday, while Stefano sleeps in, Lila walks with Elena to the Solara bar. She has dressed and made herself up like a film star — somewhere between Jennifer Jones in Duello al sole and Ava Gardner in Il sole sorgerà ancora — and Elena, beside her, feels both like a faded escort dog and afraid. Heads turn; older women stare with a half-smile that recalls the way the neighbourhood looks at Melina the madwoman. But inside the bar there is only respectful admiration: Gigliola behind the counter, Michele at the till, Marcello stepping out from the back room, ashen-faced, when he hears Gigliola announce dodici paste per la signora Carracci.

What follows is a tour-de-force of Lila's new register. She flirts — but as a married woman who knows exactly what men are, not as a girl playing at women in novels — with both brothers at once, swinging from coquettish warmth to cool refusal and back. Marcello mentions that her bridal photo is already in the Rettifilo dressmaker's window. Then she steers them, almost casually, to Antonio's draft problem. Marcello, hypnotised, agrees to ask a friend whether something can be done — not for Antonio, who doesn't deserve it, for Lenuccia.

Just as they're leaving, Michele plays his card. He wants Lila's wedding portrait — enormous, inside the new shoe shop in Piazza dei Martiri, as advertising for the Cerullo brand. Lila laughs and says it isn't her decision; Stefano decides. The Solaras exchange a startled glance — they had assumed she would refuse outright. Outside the bar Marcello, very serious, tells her he wants no misunderstandings between them: the prototype shoes were given to me by your husband and your brother, not demanded by me. Lila listens, then drops the friendly mask: I believe you, Marcè. But what you say, what they say — it doesn't matter to me anymore.
