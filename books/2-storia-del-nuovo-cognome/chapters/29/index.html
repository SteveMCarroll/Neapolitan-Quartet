Capitolo 29 / 125

Sarratore reviews the panel

Summary

Elena's mother, who has a contemptuous word for everything that happens to her daughter, sees the unwrapped books and bursts into tears — then mutters something obscure and disappears back into the housework. Elena lines the dictionaries up on the worm-eaten table in the room she shares with her brothers, and the school year ignites. She returns Galiani's summer books, gets harder ones, reads them on Sundays understanding little.

Galiani asks one day che giornale leggi, Greco? and Elena, mortified, blurts il Roma — the paper Sarratore writes for, the only one she's ever heard named. From then on Galiani hands her a daily paper after class. Elena hides them in her room (her father keeps stealing them), reads at night, half-grasps articles on the death camps, the atom bomb, peace, religion. She tries to repeat the new phrases at Lila over a sandwich: the atrocious reality of the Nazi camps; we have bent the forces of nature with the instruments we invent; religion will disappear when we build a world without classes. Lila looks uncomfortable, says little; when she does speak it's to drag the talk back to where Stefano's money comes from: don Achille's black market, the Solaras' loan sharking, the shoe factory built on both. "Non mi piace più quello che ho fatto e quello che sto facendo."

One night Elena finds a paragraph in Il Mattino that lavishly praises the Piazza dei Martiri panel as an avant-garde mixing of photograph and colour that bends matter to the urgencies of an intimate, powerful pain. She runs to the shop. Lila already knows: Michele paid for it. She produces two other clippings she keeps in the till. One in Il Roma, signed "d.s.", praises the Solaras and ignores the panel. One in Napoli notte, three columns long, gushes about "the two seductive Nereids" (Gigliola and Pinuccia) and dismisses the panel as "a coarse mess, a wrong note in an ambience of stately elegance." The signature: Donato Sarratore, Nino's father. Tale padre tale figlio, Lila says drily.
