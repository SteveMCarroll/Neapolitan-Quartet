Capitolo 119 / 125

Bruno Soccavo on the road

Summary

Lila tells Enzo everything about Stefano's visits. He listens with great attention and almost never comments — continuava a trattenersi in ogni sua manifestazione. He doesn't tell her what work he does or whether he likes it. He leaves at six in the morning and comes back at seven in the evening. He eats, plays a little with the boy, listens to her, and as soon as she names anything urgent — a thing for Rinuccio — he brings the money the next day. He never tells her to ask Stefano for child support, never tells her to look for work. He looks at her come se vivesse solo per arrivare a quelle ore serali e sedere insieme a lei in cucina; then says buonanotte and shuts himself in the bedroom.

Then comes an encounter with major consequences. One afternoon she has left Rinuccio with the neighbour and gone out alone. She hears a horn behind her — a macchina di lusso, someone waving from the window. «Lina». She recognises il viso da lupo di Bruno Soccavo, Nino's friend from Ischia. «Che fai qui?» — «Ci vivo». She tells him almost nothing; all'epoca erano cose difficili da spiegare. She doesn't mention Nino, and neither does he. Has he graduated? «Ho deciso di non studiare più». Married? «Macché». Engaged? «Un giorno sì e uno no». What does he do? «Niente, c'è chi fatica per me».

Half playing, she asks if he'd give her a job. «A te? E a che ti serve?» — «A lavorare». — «Vuoi fare i salami e le mortadelle?» — «Perché no». — «E tuo marito?» — «Non ce l'ho più un marito. Ma ho un figlio». Bruno studies her, disoriented, dodges: «Non è un bel lavoro», then chats anomalously about his parents' fighting, a recent amore violentissimo with a married woman who left him. He takes her to a bar and keeps talking about himself. At the end he asks: «Veramente hai lasciato tuo marito? Veramente hai un bambino?» — «Sì». He frowns, writes something on a napkin: «Va' da questo signore, lo trovi la mattina a partire dalle otto. E fagli vedere questo». Lila smiles, embarrassed: «Il tovagliolo?» — «Sì». «È sufficiente?» He nods, then says quietly, intimidated by her teasing tone: «Quella è stata un'estate bellissima». — «Anche per me».
