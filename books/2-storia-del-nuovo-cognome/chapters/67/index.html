Capitolo 67 / 125

Michele e Gigliola in spiaggia

Summary

Elena is called by name and turns to find Michele Solara, Gigliola, and Gigliola's twelve-year-old brother Lello on the beach. They were tipped off by Pinuccia: walk along the sand, you'll find them. Gigliola lays out her towel with care; Michele steps onto it with sandy feet — un po' d'attenzione, she snaps, uselessly. Elena performs grandi feste, doesn't invite them to sit, hopes they're in a hurry. They're not. Michele runs through his casual cover story — a heat-day off from Naples, vaporetto morning and evening, Alfonso and Pinuccia minding the piazza dei Martiri shop, anzi no, Alfonso e Pinuccia, because Pinuccia non faceva granché — and then the question Elena has been bracing for: Lina dov'è?

Fa il bagno. Con questo mare? They glance distractedly at the choppy water and settle back. Gigliola compliments Elena's tan and lightened hair. Elena, in tension-management mode, runs through a machine-gun of small talk — Spagnuolo il pasticciere, Marcello and his love life, the new shoe models, opinions from Michele's father, mother, grandfather. She walks to the shoreline and shouts Lina, torna, ci sono Michele e Gigliola — useless. Eventually Michele sees Lila for himself, uscendo dall'acqua, la mano intrecciata a quella di Nino, both of them tall and naturally elegant, un sorrisetto between them. Lila pulls her hand away the instant she sees him; era troppo tardi. Michele turns to read Elena's face for confirmation; he finds it in her fright. He says slowly: dieci minuti, il tempo di salutare, e ce ne andiamo.

They stay an hour. When Michele hears Nino's surname his face sharpens: sei il figlio di quello che scrive sul Roma e sul Napoli notte? From then on he addresses only Lila — a fast, perfidious exchange in dialect about the new shoe line (una chiavica, says Michele; si venderà ancora meglio della precedente, retorts Lila), about Gigliola's pasticceria role, about Michele's plan to install Lila at piazza dei Martiri con carta bianca. Lila parries everything: carta bianca, carta nera, toglitelo dalla testa, sto bene dove sto. Nino is stordito, half-admiring; Gigliola is furibonda at being discussed like furniture. As Michele leaves he gives Nino a final humiliation, come a un subalterno: tell your father that when one takes money from people one should write that everything is beautiful, otherwise soldi non se ne vedono mai più. Nino, impantanato nella sorpresa, says nothing.
