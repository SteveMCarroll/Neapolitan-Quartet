Capitolo 105 / 125

Reading the notebooks; the Arno

Summary

Before leaving she finds Enzo. When she delivers the line — Lina mi ha raccomandato di dirti che ha provato ma non c'è riuscita — non gli passò nemmeno l'ombra di un'emozione over his face, and she decides the message meant nothing to him. «Sta molto male», she adds. «D'altra parte non so proprio cosa si può fare». He tightens his lips, looks grave, and they say goodbye.

On the train she opens the metal box, anche se avevo giurato di non farlo. Eight notebooks. From the first lines she feels ill. Back in Pisa the sickness grows for days, then months. Every word of Lila's diminishes her. Every sentence — even the ones written when Lila was still a girl — seems to empty her own, not the old ones but the present ones. And at the same time every page lights up thoughts of her own, ideas, pages, as if until now she has lived in a torpore studioso ma inconcludente. She memorises them all, and at the end she finds that the Normale — friends and admirers, kind professors urging her on — feels troppo protetto, troppo prevedibile next to the stormy world Lila, within the conditions of the rione, has explored in her hurried lines and rumpled, stained pages.

All her past effort starts to feel meaningless. She is afraid; she studies badly for months. She is alone — Franco Mari aveva perso il posto in Normale — and cannot shake the sense of her own pochezza. At some point it becomes clear to her that she will fail something and be sent home like everyone else. So one evening in late autumn, without a precise plan, she takes the metal box out, walks to the ponte Solferino, and throws it into the Arno.
