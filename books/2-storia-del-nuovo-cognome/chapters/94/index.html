Capitolo 94 / 125

An interlude on Enzo

Summary

A short interlude on Enzo, the man who has just decided to fetch Lila. At this point Elena knows almost nothing about him except that nothing — niente, assolutamente niente — pleases him anymore: not his mother's whining, not the weight of his younger siblings, not the camorra at the fruit‑and‑veg market, not the carting work that earns less and less, not Pasquale's communist talk, not even being engaged to Carmen. He has the kind of closed character that makes him hard to read. From Carmen Elena has heard that he is studying in secret to take the perito industriale diploma as a private candidate, and — said with irritation around Christmas — that since coming back from the army the previous spring he has given her exactly four kisses. Forse non è uomo, Carmen huffs.

Elena steps back to gloss the phrase. When a man didn't seem to want them, the girls used to fall back on the formula non è uomo — because they understood nothing about certi fondi oscuri dei maschi, none of them did, and the verdict was a way to label any behaviour that wouldn't decode. She runs through her sample: some men, like the Solaras, Pasquale, Antonio, Donato Sarratore, even Franco Mari — il mio fidanzato della Normale — want women, in tones aggressive or sullen, attentive or careless, but unambiguously. Others, like Alfonso, like Enzo, like Nino, hold themselves at a distant compostezza, as if a wall stood between them and the women and the climb were the women's job.

After the army, Enzo's distance has only sharpened. He has stopped doing anything to please women, and in truth anything to please l'universo mondo. His body, never tall, looks as if it has compressed further, un blocco compatto di energia, the skin on his face stretched taut like an awning, his stride reduced to the pure compass of his legs, no other part of him moving — not arms, not neck, not head, not even the casco biondorossiccio of his hair. When he tells Pasquale and Antonio that he's going to Lila he doesn't open a discussion; he closes one. He sets off without confusion, finds the street, finds the portone, climbs the stairs and rings firmly at the right door.
