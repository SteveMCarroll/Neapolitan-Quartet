Capitolo 21 / 125

Lila wins the bet, sort of

Summary

Lila is in a mean mood. She makes Elena try on all her dresses, knowing perfectly well they will be ridiculous on her smaller, dowdier friend, then enjoys the discomfort. Then she abruptly stops and says: by the way, I went for ice cream the other night. With whom? With Pasquale and Ada. Alone. I asked. And another night: pizza with Enzo and Carmela. She tells these stories with relish, listing everyone in the old rione's bodies and ailments — Giuseppina Peluso's trembling legs, Melina's vomiting — in a tone of I know all of them intimately and you've stopped noticing. Then: I also got a gelato with Antonio. He's fine. He said nothing about you. He leaves in September. Marcello hasn't lifted a finger for him. Era scontato — it was a given.

Elena bristles silently — if it was a given the Solaras wouldn't help, why did you take me there? Why see the old friends alone? Why not tell me you saw Antonio? — and starts gathering her things. Lila stops her with the punchline she came to deliver: Stefano refused the Solaras in Piazza dei Martiri. Three of them — Marcello, Michele, Rino — pressed him; he held the line. Visto? — see? You won the bet. So Lila now has to go back to school. Aspettiamo, Lila says. Wait.

Elena is left thinking that what Lila really wanted was not for Stefano to refuse but for the Solaras to push him harder, force her photo onto that wall — for the men around her to fight to the death over her image. And underneath that, she watches Lila stroke her own flank like a farewell, the same look she had the day Melina was fished out of the ponds. Something is wrong inside her body, and she doesn't have words for it yet.
