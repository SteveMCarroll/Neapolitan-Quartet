Capitolo 73 / 125

Quello che diranno i quaderni

Summary

A retrospective. Elena says it's fortunate she only read Lila's quaderni later: there were pages and pages on that day and that night with Nino, e ciò che quelle pagine dicevano era esattamente ciò che io non avevo da dire. Lila didn't write a single word about sexual pleasure — nulla che potesse essere utile per accostare la sua esperienza alla mia. She wrote instead about love, and in a way that surprises Elena.

The notebooks describe Lila's state from the wedding day to those Ischia days as a slow approach to death she hadn't recognised — un calo di energia, sonnolenza, una forte pressione al centro della testa, come se tra cervello e ossa del cranio ci fosse una bolla d'aria in continua espansione. Everything around her moved too fast and grazed her, hurt her in the belly and behind the eyes. Her senses were ottundimento, as if she'd been wrapped in cotton wool, the wounds coming not from the world but from the gap between her body and the wadding. The certainty of imminent death had stripped her of respect for everything, including herself, so nothing mattered and tutto meritasse di essere guastato. There were bursts of need per esprimersi senza nessuna mediazione, one last time, before becoming Melina, before walking into a passing truck.

Nino had pulled her out of that state. Already at the Galiani party, when he'd asked her to dance and she'd refused, terrified of the offer of salvation. Then on Ischia, giorno dopo giorno, he had taken on la potenza del soccorritore, restored her capacity to feel, resuscitato il senso di sé. The notebooks circle obsessively around the word: resurrection, un estatico levarsi, la fine di ogni vincolo e tuttavia il piacere indicibile di un nuovo vincolo, un risorgere che era anche un insorgere. The two of them reimparavano la vita, ne esiliavano il veleno, la reinventavano come pura gioia del pensare e del vivere.

Elena adds: this is a paraphrase — le sue parole erano molto belle, il mio è solo un riassunto. If Lila had said any of this on the motocarrozzella, she would have suffered more, because she would have recognised in Lila's pienezza realizzata the inverse of her own emptiness. She would have understood it wasn't un gioco estivo but un sentimento violentissimo building inside Lila. Instead, on the way back to Nunzia after le nostre violazioni, she felt only the familiar lopsidedness — l'impressione – ricorrente nella nostra storia – che mi stessi perdendo qualcosa che invece lei stava acquistando. She thinks of telling Lila about the Maronti — leaving out Donato's name, inventing a sailor, an American-cigarette smuggler — to even the score; then realises she doesn't want to tell about her pleasure for its own sake, only to draw out Lila's confession and confrontarlo col mio e sentirmi — speravo —in vantaggio. Lila would never play that game. Me ne restai in silenzio come restò in silenzio lei.
