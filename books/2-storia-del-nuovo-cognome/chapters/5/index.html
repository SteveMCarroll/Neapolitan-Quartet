Capitolo 5 / 125

Skipping school; Lila returns veiled

Summary

A heavy exhaustion descends on Elena that no amount of rest will lift. For about two weeks she does something she has never done before: she plays hooky. She leaves the house every morning at the usual time, school satchel in hand, and wanders Naples on foot. She rummages through used books on the stalls of Port'Alba, drifts down to Toledo and the sea, climbs to the Vomero up via Salvator Rosa, comes back down by the Petraio, wanders the cemetery reading names off the stones. Unemployed young men, dirty old men, even respectable middle-aged signori accost her with obscene proposals. She hurries past with her eyes down, but she doesn't go home; the morning absences widen the tear in the net of school obligations that has contained her since she was six.

She gets back at the usual hour and no one suspects her — not her, of all people. Afternoons she reads novels. Evenings she goes to Antonio at the ponds; he asks no questions, terrified of starting a fight she could use as a pretext to leave him. She does not tell him she has decided to drop out.

What gnaws at her isn't Antonio. It's Nino. She thinks of him constantly, wanting to talk to him and dreading it. She is sure he will mention the article she wrote about her clash with the religion teacher — the one his magazine accepted in principle and then dropped "for lack of space." She is sure he will deliver the editors' verdicts and the verdicts will be cruel. Better to step aside before that can happen. Better to forget every notion crammed into her head, the living languages and the dead ones, even the Italian that now rises to her lips automatically, even with her siblings. It's Lila's fault, she thinks, that I went down this road; I have to forget her too. Lila always knew what she wanted and got it; I don't want anything; I'm made of nothing. She tells herself Antonio's affection will be enough.

Then, on her way home one day, she runs into Pinuccia. Lila is back from the honeymoon, Pinuccia announces, and has thrown a big lunch in her new apartment to celebrate Pinuccia's engagement to her brother Rino. (Pinuccia is now wearing his ring.) Elena pretends to be surprised, then learns to her stinging mortification that she herself was not invited. She tells herself it's just as well; she doesn't want to see Lila again. Only at the end does she carefully ask after her friend. Pinuccia answers with a sly smirk and a dialect phrase: si sta imparando. She's learning her place. Elena doesn't ask what that means. She goes home and sleeps the whole afternoon.

The next morning she leaves at seven again, on her way to "school." She has just crossed the stradone when she sees Lila step out of Stefano's convertible and slip into their old courtyard without so much as a wave to him. Lila is meticulously dressed and wearing big dark sunglasses though the sun isn't out. What strikes Elena most is a sky-blue gauze scarf knotted across the lower half of her face, covering her lips. Elena thinks bitterly that this is some new self-presentation, no longer Jacqueline Kennedy but a "lady of shadows" of the kind they had imagined for themselves as children, and walks on.

A few steps later, against every plan, she turns back. She doesn't know why; she only knows she can't help it. Her heart hammers. Maybe she wants to make Lila say to her face that the friendship is over. Maybe she wants to scream that she's quit school and is going to marry Antonio and end up like Melina the madwoman, washing stairs.

She finds Lila on the stairs of the building where Stefano's mother lives — the same stairs the two of them climbed as little girls to demand that Don Achille give back their dolls. "Sei tornata," Elena says. Yes. — And why didn't you come and find me? — I didn't want you to see me. — Other people can see you, but I can't? — The others I don't care about. You I do.

Elena climbs the rest of the stairs between them, gently pulls the scarf aside and lifts the dark glasses off her friend's face. The chapter cuts off there, on what she sees.
