Capitolo 9 / 125

On the stairs; an invitation to study

Summary

Picking up immediately from chapter 5: Elena lifts the scarf and sunglasses off Lila's face on Stefano's mother's staircase. The skin around the eye is yellowing, the lip is purple with red streaks. To everyone else, Lila has stuck to a story about slipping on the rocks at Amalfi; with Elena she doesn't bother. She offers a sparse, almost icy summary of the honeymoon — Stefano fooled me, he's just like his father. "Remember how, instead of the dolls, don Achille gave us money?" she asks. We bought Little Women. We shouldn't have. From that moment I've done everything wrong.

Elena's emotional response is mixed and the chapter is honest about it: she cries, hugs Lila, suffers for her — and quietly notices a secret pleasure that Lila now needs her. Before Maria calls Lila upstairs, Lila makes an offer: come study at my place — every day, a room of your own, just so I know you're there. Elena doesn't answer. She wanders the city instead, telling herself she's done with school, done with chasing Nino's level. She decides she'll marry Antonio and help him run a petrol station on the stradone.

Then she runs into Alfonso outside the liceo. He's waiting for Marisa. He tells Elena that Galiani has been asking after her; he's covered for her with a story about a fever. Elena, fishing: And what about what your brother did to Lina? He's evasive — you'd have to know what Lina did to him. Would he ever raise a hand to Marisa? No. Why? Because I know you, because we talk, because we go to school together. The clumsy formulation sticks in Elena's head all the way home. The next morning she forges her mother's signature on absence slips and goes back to school. At the ponds that evening she promises Antonio they'll marry as soon as she finishes the year.
