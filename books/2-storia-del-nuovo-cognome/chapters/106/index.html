Capitolo 106 / 125

The &#39;quasi&#39; year at the Normale

Summary

Elena's last Pisa year changes everything she thought she knew about the first three. Mi prese un disamore ingrato — she falls out of love with the city, the friends, the professors, the freezing days, the meetings under the Battistero, the cineforum, even the dependable little circuit (the Timpano, Lungarno Pacinotti, via XXIV maggio, piazza dei Cavalieri) that has become hers. Whether it's the notebooks she now can't unread or not, she has been disenchanted. The initial sense of being inside una battaglia intrepida has gone; so has the batticuore before exams and the pleasure of slowly re‑educating her voice, her clothes, her walk, come se gareggiassi per il premio del travestimento migliore, the mask worn so well it's almost a face.

It's the quasi that has caught up to her. Has she made it? Quasi. Has she really pulled herself away from Naples and the rione? Quasi. Has she really joined the cultured friends, often more cultured than the Galiani household? Quasi. Avevo paura come il primo giorno che ero arrivata a Pisa. Temevo chi sapeva essere colto senza il quasi, con disinvoltura. At the Normale she sees how many young men (and they are almost all men, like the famous names that have passed through) move easily because they already know the practical use of what they're learning: how a newspaper or a magazine is made, how a publisher works, the radio and television, the films, the university hierarchies, what lies beyond le Alpi and il mare. They know the names that matter, who to admire, who to despise. Per me chiunque stampasse il suo nome su un giornale o su un libro era un dio. She doesn't know the mappa del prestigio. She prepares perfectly for an exam, but if a professor suddenly asked her by which authority he teaches the subject, she would have nothing to say.

While she was Franco Mari's girl the fear had eased. She had learnt to move dentro la sua scia, to lean on his certainties, to be sharper in private and sometimes in public than he was — o almeno lo stavo diventando. His comfortable manners, his prestige as a well‑known left‑wing student militant, his sociability and even his courage when he stood up calibrated and confident against people in power — all of that radiated onto her, quasi che il puro e semplice fatto che mi amasse fosse la certificazione pubblica delle mie qualità. With his expulsion from the Normale the certificate has lapsed. The good‑family students stop inviting her to Sunday parties and trips; the old jokes about her Naples accent come back; the dresses he gave her go out of fashion on her. Franco only ever masked her real position, never changed it. Avrei avuto sempre paura: paura di dire la frase sbagliata, di usare un tono eccessivo, di essere vestita in modo inadeguato, di rivelare sentimenti meschini, di non avere pensieri interessanti.
