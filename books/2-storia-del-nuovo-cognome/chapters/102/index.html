Capitolo 102 / 125

Two betrayals, two retaliations

Summary

From that night Lila loses what little freedom she had. Stefano, now that there's nothing left to hide, drops every pretence: he often doesn't come home, alternates Sundays in the car with Ada, even takes her abroad — that August they drive a spider to Stockholm, with Ada officially "at a cousin's at Fiat in Turin". At the same time he develops a sick jealousy of his wife. She isn't to leave the flat, has to phone in the shopping, and is interrogated about every outing — chi hai visto, con chi hai parlato. Cheating has fired up his imagination and he now sees her doing more, with more men. He's especially terrified of Michele, who he senses is using Lila per sfregio to ruin him in business. But the same logic demands Lila stay at least cordial with Michele, so whatever she does is wrong. «Hai visto Michele, ti ha chiesto di disegnare nuove scarpe?» one day; «A quello stronzo non devi dire nemmeno ciao» the next. He starts opening her drawers in search of evidence of her natura di zoccola.

Then Pasquale finds out. He is, naturally, the last to know that his fidanzata is Stefano's lover; he learns it a Sunday in September when he sees them coming out arm in arm of a portone on the Rettifilo — Ada had said she was busy with Melina. The instinct is to butcher both, but his party formation forbids it: he is now segretario di sezione, reads l'Unità, runs debates, and however much, growing up, he and the rest of the boys would catalogue any inconvenient girl as a troia, he forces himself now to treat women as not inferior, con nostri sentimenti, nostre idee, nostre libertà. The next evening, still grimy from work, he goes to Ada's. She is relieved, confesses everything, cries, asks his pardon, says she loves Stefano — only she knows what a buona, generosa, garbata person he really is. Pasquale punches the kitchen wall and walks home weeping with bleeding knuckles. He sits up all night with Carmen — she nursing the wound of Enzo, he the wound of Ada. Then, fatefully, he decides he must defend the dignity not just of his fiancée but of Lila. He tells Stefano he must leave his wife and openly take Ada as un regolare concubinato. He tells Lila she shouldn't let Stefano calpestasse i suoi diritti di moglie. One morning at six‑thirty Stefano confronts him on his way to work and offers him money to stop annoying everyone. Pasquale counts the cash, throws it in the air, says «Lavoro da quando ero piccolo, non ho bisogno di te», and then, walking off, turns back and shouts: «Sei peggio di quel porco fascista di tuo padre». They fight terribly and have to be pulled apart.

Rino opens a parallel front. He won't tolerate that Lila has stopped working on Dino's intelligence, that Stefano has stopped paying him and just laid hands on him, that his sister's humiliation is now public. He retaliates by mirroring Stefano onto Pinuccia: if Stefano beats Lila, he beats Pinuccia; if Stefano has a lover, he takes one. Pinuccia weeps, begs, threatens; nothing. «La devo finire?» he yells at her and Nunzia. «E allora va' da tuo fratello e digli che deve lasciare Ada, che deve rispettare Lina, che dobbiamo essere una famiglia unita e che mi deve dare i soldi che lui e i Solara m'hanno fottuto». Pinuccia, bruised, runs sobbing to the salumeria where Ada and the customers can hear, then drags Stefano into the back room with the same shopping list of Rino demands, ending each time: «Non gli dare niente a quello stronzo, vieni a casa subito e ammazzalo».
