Capitolo 88 / 125

Lila leaves Stefano with a knife in her hand

Summary

Then she finds out she's pregnant and decides to end the whole charade. A Sunday in late autumn 1963 she skips the usual lunch at her mother‑in‑law's and cooks at home with care. While Stefano slips out to fetch pastries from the Solaras' bar and drop a few off at Maria and Pinuccia to make peace, Lila packs the suitcase from their honeymoon — underwear, a couple of dresses, a pair of winter shoes — and hides it behind the salotto door. She washes every pot, lays the kitchen table, and from a drawer takes out the meat knife and lays it in the sink under a rag. Then she opens the window onto the railway tracks and waits in the cold davanzale; the chill le dava energia.

Stefano comes home in a foul mood because he missed his mother's cooking. He doesn't praise the lunch, hammers his brother‑in‑law Rino at the table, makes a point of calling the baby il figlio di mia sorella — diminishing Rino's part to nothing — and eats three of the pastries while Lila eats none. «Andiamo a dormire un poco», he says. Lila answers: «Da domani non vado più al negozio». Step by step she dismantles every fallback he offers. Not Solara trouble, not the salumeria either, not even staying home — she wants to andarsene, leave him, leave the marriage. She tells him calmly she made a mistake from the start, never loved him, can no longer stand being kept by him, helping him make money, sharing his bed.

He slaps her off the chair. She bolts to the sink, grabs the knife from under the rag, and turns it on him as he lunges again: «Fallo e ti uccido come hanno ucciso tuo padre». The reference to don Achille stops him. He mutters «Ma sì, ammazzami, fa' quello che ti pare», lets out a long defeated yawn, eats another pastry and slumps off, throwing over his shoulder all he gave her, all he gave her famiglia di merda, then bellowing from the bedroom «Tu non ti puoi neanche immaginare quanto ti voglio bene». Lila lays the knife on the sink and realises he doesn't believe she's leaving — non crederebbe neanche che ho un altro. She goes in to tell him about Nino and the pregnancy. He has fallen asleep, dragging the sleep over himself come una cappa fatata. She puts on her coat, picks up the suitcase, and walks out of the apartment.
