Capitolo 43 / 125

Maronti; the address

Summary

They walk down to the Maronti. The Sarratores are exactly where they were years before — same umbrella, same swimsuits, same poses. Donato lies in the black sand on his elbows, gut out; Lidia sits on a towel with a magazine. Elena calls out for the children — Pino, Clelia, Ciro — and Lidia recognises her, leaps up, calls her name. Donato bounds over, arms wide, ready to embrace her; Elena ducks the hug and only says buongiorno. He instantly pivots to Lila and Pinuccia, calling them signora Carracci and signora Cerullo, gushing with his stock literary nostalgia about the running away of time.

Elena chats with Lidia — Marisa is in Naples having to make up four subjects, ben le sta. Lidia's face carries heavy traces of pain. She follows her husband with her eyes when Donato offers to take the two younger women into the water and teach Lila to swim. Lo ho fatto con tutti i miei figli, he says, lo farò anche con te.

Elena does not ask about Nino. But a dark point bobbing on the shimmering blue stops moving away from shore and starts coming back. It enlarges. Sì, è lui. He climbs out and watches his father holding Lila up under one arm, demonstrating strokes with the other. He registers Elena without surprise: Che ci fai qui? Quella non è Lina? He toils his hair dry, asks who Pinuccia is, half-listens to Elena's news. She mentions clumsily that they have a house near Forio and that on weekends she has nothing to do. He says: Fatti viva. Then to his mother — I have to go. Lidia: already? Ho da fare. She protests: but Elena is here. He looks at Elena as if just realising. He pulls a notebook from a hanging shirt, writes an address, tears the page, hands it over: Sto a questo indirizzo. Crisp, decisive, like a film actor. He pulls a towel around the waist, changes, walks off along the shore without saying goodbye to his father. Elena pockets the slip as if it were a relic.
