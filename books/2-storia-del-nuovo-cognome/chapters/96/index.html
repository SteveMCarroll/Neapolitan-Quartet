Capitolo 96 / 125

Back home: a Pisa story for the rione

Summary

Enzo really does wait under their windows that evening, and would have come up and killed Stefano if Stefano had hit her. But Stefano doesn't hit her. He welcomes her back into the clean, orderly flat as if she really had been at Elena's in Pisa — anche se non c'era nessuna prova. Lila doesn't bother to confirm or deny the Pisa story. The next morning, at breakfast, she tells him svogliatamente «Sono incinta»; he is so happy that when she adds «Il bambino non è tuo» he bursts out laughing con genuina allegria. She repeats it, hits him with closed fists, can't dent him; he kisses her, mutters «Basta, Lina, sono troppo contento». Lila has long known people lie to themselves to keep facts at bay; she's still astonished he can manage it con tanta gioiosa convinzione. Fine — let him postpone his pain. She lists her conditions: no more piazza dei Martiri, no salumeria, no visitors, no Solaras; she'll stay home and be wife and mother. He agrees, sure she'll change her mind in a few days.

She doesn't. She seals herself in. Pinuccia brings her baby Dino; Lila doesn't open. Rino comes whining about Solara fury and Stefano's stinginess; Lila listens, then says: «Rino, viviti la tua vita senza rivolgerti continuamente a me». Michele visits twice a day; she sits in the kitchen barely breathing until he shouts from the street «Chi cazzo ti credi di essere, troia». She accepts only Nunzia and Maria. The vomiting stops, but a grey tint settles on her skin and she feels grossa e gonfia dentro più che fuori — every organ enlarging inside the envelope of her body, her belly an inflating bubble that might rompersi, dilagare. Then she feels the thing growing inside her — quella modalità assurda della vita, the nodule that will one day come out come un pupazzetto a corda — and discovers she loves it. She buys nothing for herself, only newspapers from her mother and magazines from Alfonso, and reads everything she can find about pregnancy and birth. When Carmen comes by to ask for money she sends her to Stefano. The rione spits at her newly: Carmen calls her a zoccola getting corna e soldi and (without evidence) puts her in bed with Michele; she has lost her sodality at the salumeria, her closed purse, and now also Enzo, who's left her without explanation — Pasquale demanded a reason and got none.

That spring of 1964 Elena comes home for Easter. Antonio, in those very days, finally tells her about the beating and about sending Enzo to fetch Lila — she will later be sure he has never told anyone else. Alfonso, pressed hard, reports the Marisa version of Nino: gone to study in Milan. Armed with all of this, Elena bumps into Lila on the stradone on Holy Saturday and savours knowing more about Lila's life than Lila knows. Lila looks tiny under a sharp belly, imbruttita, verdognola, cheekbones taut. They make small talk — Pisa, Franco Mari ("una specie di fidanzato"), the glasses he gave her, the dress he gave her. Lila says the Ischia business is finita. The due date is July, in a clinic, with the doctor who once prescribed the sea — Ho paura per il bambino, non voglio partorire in casa. Then she lets slip: «Sto ancora qua solo per questo». «È bello sentirsi dentro il bambino?» — «No, mi fa ribrezzo ma lo tengo con piacere». Elena offers to back the Pisa story if asked; Lila shrugs: «Fa' come ti pare». They part promising to write, and don't. Inside Elena a feeling surges that she pushes down before it can become conscious: volevo che le accadesse qualcosa, che il bambino non nascesse.
