Capitolo 93 / 125

Antonio lies to Michele and sends Enzo

Summary

The attacker was Antonio. He tells Michele almost nothing. Yes, he found the Sarratore boy. No, the trail didn't lead to Lila. No, Nino has nothing to do with la signora Carracci. All of it a lie. He had found them quickly, by accident, the night he'd been sent to brawl with the communists at the Pasolini event; he'd peeled off from the fight to tail the two who ran, learnt where they lived, and then spent days watching them. The watching had bred in him two things at once — admiration and envy. Admiration for Lila: how can a woman walk out of a beautiful flat, leave shoes and cars and the Solaras for uno studente senza una lira who keeps her in a place almost worse than the rione? Envy of Nino, because lo stronzo secco e brutto che piaceva a me turns out to be the same one Lila chose. He turns the question over until it nests in his nerves, his hands knotting and unknotting come se pregasse, and finally decides he must free Lila even if she doesn't want to be freed.

He insists later, telling Elena, that Michele never ordered a beating; that was his own decision, half wanting to take Nino out of her hands and give her back what she'd inexplicably thrown away, half — he admits, blushing — for the pleasure of punishing quello che noi due ragazze gli avevamo attribuito: the importance she and Elena had assigned to a flimsy, breakable Sarratore boy. Elena, hearing this, will actually be moved enough to stroke his cheek. To prove he isn't a beast he adds: «Dopo l'ho aiutato» — he picked Nino up, hauled him groggy to a pharmacy, left him in the doorway, then went back to the rione to talk to Pasquale and Enzo.

The two friends are reluctant to even see him. They no longer treat him as a friend — Pasquale especially, who is fidanzato with his sister and disgusted that he's gone over to the Solaras. Antonio doesn't care; he plays the meeting as if their hostility is a passing mood and lays it all out. Nothing about Nino. He's found Lila; she isn't with Elena; she's alone in a posto di merda at the Campi Flegrei; someone has to help her. «A far che?» — Pasquale, hostile. «A tornare a casa sua». Why is she there alone, with no money? Antonio doesn't know, hasn't spoken to her, found her for Michele Solara. «Sei un fascista di merda». He doesn't argue — non sono niente, ho fatto un lavoro — and confesses he hasn't told Michele he found her. He needs his job; he wants Pasquale and Enzo to go in his place and bring Lila home. Pasquale bristles: maybe she was right to leave Stefano and the Solaras; lasciamola in pace. Antonio: alone, with no money? Pasquale, embarrassed by his old debts to Lila, mumbles he can chip in. Enzo, silent the whole time, cuts him off with a wave and says simply: «Dammi l'indirizzo, vado a sentire io che intenzioni ha».
