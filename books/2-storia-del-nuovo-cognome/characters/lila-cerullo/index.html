Storia del nuovo cognome · Characters

Raffaella "Lila" Cerullo

Elena's brilliant childhood friend. Newly married to Stefano Carracci.

Family: Cerullo (the cobbler's family)

Raffaella Cerullo, called Lina by everyone except Elena, who alone calls her Lila. Born in August 1944 in the same Naples neighbourhood as Elena. She was a startlingly brilliant pupil — taught herself to read, wrote a story called La fata blu ("The Blue Fairy") at ten — but her father Fernando, a cobbler, refused to send her on to middle school after the elementary licence. She learned the shoemaking trade in the family workshop instead.

In book 1 she designed a line of new shoes with her brother Rino, hoping to lift the family out of poverty. She rebuffed Marcello Solara, the older of the two predatory Solara brothers, and chose instead to be engaged to the mild Stefano Carracci — son of the neighbourhood's late loan shark Don Achille — who agreed to invest in the Cerullo shoe factory and treasure her first prototype shoes forever.

Book 2 opens in the immediate aftermath of her sixteenth-birthday wedding to Stefano. At the reception Lila discovers that Marcello Solara is wearing the very pair of prototype shoes Stefano had sworn to keep — a sign that Stefano has made some business arrangement with the Solaras using Lila's work as collateral. From that moment on, something inside her shifts. She leaves on her honeymoon to the Amalfi coast and returns to her new apartment in the rione nuovo, already a different person — Signora Raffaella Carracci, holder of her new name (nuovo cognome).

In the spring of 1966, twenty-two-year-old Lila will give Elena a tin box containing eight notebooks for safekeeping, fearing her husband will read them. The notebooks chronicle her life from the end of elementary school onward — descriptions, language exercises, fragments of dialect, and a relentless account of the rione and the people in it, Elena included.

This page reflects what's known to a reader who has finished book 2. Earlier knowledge from book 1 is summarized briefly; no events from later books appear here.
