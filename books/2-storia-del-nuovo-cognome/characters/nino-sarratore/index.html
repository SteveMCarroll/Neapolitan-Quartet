Nino Sarratore

Brilliant student. The lifelong, mostly secret object of Elena's love.

Family: Sarratore (the railwayman-poet's family)

The eldest of the five Sarratore children and the lifelong, mostly secret object of Elena's love. Son of Donato Sarratore — railway worker, occasional poet and journalist, and serial seducer — and of Lidia Sarratore. Nino openly despises his father, who in book 1 became Melina Cappuccio's lover, broke the Cappuccio household, and later sexually harassed Elena during her summer on Ischia.

Nino is a brilliant student, two years ahead of Elena at the same high school and already publishing pieces in small journals. He gave the speech that closed Lila's wedding ceremony's intellectual subplot in book 1: in particular, he was the one who got Elena's first article — about her clash with her religion teacher — accepted in principle and then dropped from the magazine for lack of space (or, as Elena fears, for lack of merit).

In the opening of book 2 he is at Lila's wedding for most of the afternoon, spending it largely in conversation with Elena. He leaves the reception abruptly — "like an archangel without an annunciation" — shortly before Marcello Solara walks in wearing Lila's shoes. Antonio Cappuccio, Elena's official boyfriend, has been jealously watching the whole thing from the terrace.

This page reflects what's known to a reader who has finished book 2. Earlier knowledge from book 1 is summarized briefly; no events from later books appear here.
