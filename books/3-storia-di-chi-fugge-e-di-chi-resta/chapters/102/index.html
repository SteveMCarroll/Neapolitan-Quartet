Capitolo 102 / 123

Thirty days of writing — and Nino returns, with his wife

Summary

Elena resolves not to count the days, but instead pours the wait into work. She studies as if Nino had commissioned the project: she wants to be able to hand him a sketch when he returns and say, ho seguito il tuo consiglio — I followed your advice. The trick works beautifully. The thirty days fly. She forgets to call Elisa, never phones Mariarosa, doesn't think about Lila, ignores the newspapers and the television, barely registers the rancorous election campaign; she writes, and only writes.

The chapter rehearses the heart of what she writes, partly because this is the work Nino will praise and partly because it is the closest Elena will come, in this whole stretch, to a thesis of her own. She sets the two Genesis creation stories side by side, treating the first as a compact summary of the divine creative act and the second as a more leisurely retelling. From the dust of the earth, God shapes Ish and breathes into his nostrils the alito vitale (the breath of life). Then he extracts Isha'h, the woman, from the man's side — not from raw dust this time but from a male material already alive. The result, Elena writes, is that Ish can say: this thing is not, like the rest of creation, other than me — it is flesh of my flesh, bone of my bones. God begot her from me. Io sono Ish e lei è Isha'h. In the very word that names her, she derives from me, who am made in the image of the divine spirit and carry the Word within me. She is therefore a pure suffix attached to my verbal root, and can express herself only inside my word.

She works for days in a state of gradevole sovreccitazione intellettuale — pleasant intellectual overdrive. Sometimes she catches herself in surprise: the prospect of Nino's approval, far from paralysing her, sets the writing free. Her one anxiety is finishing in time.

But the month passes and Nino does not appear. The extra time helps at first; she completes the draft. Then she gets nervous and asks Pietro. It turns out the two of them have been speaking often at the faculty — only Pietro has heard nothing for a few days. "Often?" she snaps. Yes, Pietro says — work calls. Then why hadn't he told her? Because, he says, they were work calls. Fine, Elena says coldly: now that the two of you are such close friends, you can phone him and ask when he means to grace us with his return. Pietro doesn't. Elena mutters: very well, we wait — Nino promised the girls he would come back, he won't disappoint them.

He calls a week late, in the evening. Elena answers; Nino sounds embarrassed, throws out a few generic phrases, then asks: Pietro non c'è? Pietro is there. She passes him the receiver. They talk for a long time and Pietro's voice slips into tones she has never heard him use: too loud, too many exclamations, easy laughter. Only now does she understand that the friendship with Nino reassures her husband, makes him feel less isolated, helps him forget the headaches and put in the hours on his book. She withdraws to her room, where Dede is reading and Elsa is playing, both waiting for dinner. Even through the door she can hear that strange, almost-tipsy voice of his.

Then footsteps. Pietro pokes his head in, beaming, and tells the girls: bambine, domani sera si va a mangiare le frittelle con zio Nino — tomorrow night we're going to eat frittelle with uncle Nino. Dede and Elsa shriek with joy. Elena asks the only question she cares about — is he coming to stay with them? No, Pietro says: è con la moglie e il figlio, sono in albergo — he has his wife and son with him, they're at the hotel.
