Capitolo 105 / 123

Nino reads overnight — and a confession about Lila

Summary

Elena doesn't sleep. In the morning she resigns herself to the shopping date — she is to meet Eleonora outside the hotel at ten. She gives herself a strict instruction: don't be stupid enough to ask Eleonora whether her husband has started reading; Nino is busy, he will take time, don't think about it, it will be at least a week.

At nine on the dot, just as she is about to leave the house, the phone rings. It's him.

"Sorry," he says, "I'm about to go into the library and I won't be able to phone you again until tonight. Sure I'm not bothering you?"

"Not at all."

"Ho letto" — I've read it.

"Already?"

"Yes. It's excellent work. You have an enormous capacity for study, an admirable rigour, and an inventiveness that takes my breath away. But what I envy most is your ability as a narratrice — a storyteller. You have written a text that's hard to define, I don't know if it is a saggio or a racconto, an essay or a story. But it is straordinario — extraordinary."

"Is that a flaw?"

"What?"

"That it's not classifiable."

"Not at all — that's one of its merits."

"In your view should I publish it as it is?"

"Absolutely."

"Thank you."

"Thank you. I have to go. Be patient with Eleonora, she seems aggressive but it's only shyness. Tomorrow morning we go back to Naples, but I'll come back after the elections and if you like we can talk."

"I'd love that. Will you come and stay with us?"

"Sure I'm not in the way?"

"Not at all."

"All right."

He doesn't hang up. She can hear his breathing.

"Elena."

"Yes."

"Lina, da ragazzi, ci ha abbagliati entrambi." — Lina, when we were young, dazzled both of us.

She feels a strong unease. "In what sense?"

"You ended up attributing to her capacities that are only yours."

"And you?"

"I did worse. What I had seen in you, I then stupidly thought I'd found in her."

She stays silent for a few seconds. Why has he felt the need to drag Lila into a phone call? And, above all, what is he actually saying? Compliments only? Or is he trying to tell her that as a teenager he would have wanted her, but on Ischia he ended up assigning to the one girl what really belonged to the other?

"Come back soon," she says.
