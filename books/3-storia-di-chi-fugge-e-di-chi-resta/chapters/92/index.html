Storia di chi fugge e di chi resta · Tempo di mezzo

Capitolo 92 / 123

Michele&#39;s toast — Lila as something to be bought

Summary

The long age of disembodied phone calls breaks open: Lila in person, in a knee-length blue dress, all tendons, a little taller than she should be even in low heels. Deep lines bracket her mouth and eyes; the skin of her face is unnaturally tight; the ponytail shows streaks of white above her almost lobeless ears. She smiles at Elena with her eyes screwed up. Elena doesn't smile back, doesn't even say hello. Though they are both thirty, Lila looks older — più sgualcita ("more crumpled") — than Elena imagines herself to be. Gigliola yells: finally the other little queen has arrived, the children are starving.

Dinner is unbearable. Elena can't swallow. She rages silently at the luggage strangers have unpacked and repacked, at the fact that she will sleep in Marcello Solara's house to please her sister, at her mother's flushed satisfaction as she heaps Lila's plate with good manners she has never shown Elena. She watches Enzo eating with his head down while Gigliola presses her enormous breast against his arm and flirts loudly. She watches Pietro — pestered on three sides by her father, by Marcello, by Manuela — give most of his attention to Lila, who sits opposite him, indifferent to everyone except, somehow, him.

Whose theatre is this? Elena turns the question. Elisa staged it, but Elisa was prompted — by Marcello, surely, and Marcello by Michele, who sits next to Elena, eating and drinking with great ease, ignoring his wife and children, and watching Pietro be charmed by Lila with ironic satisfaction. For the first time all evening Michele turns to her: Hai visto mammà? — have you seen mamma? — sixty years old and who would ever say it. He says it loudly so the whole table must hear, and Elena understands she is being forced to pronounce a public compliment. Compliment for mammà — the woman the local fantasy makes the strozzina who founded the Solara fortunes, the keeper of the red ledger of debtors, la donna del delitto senza castigo ("the woman of the unpunished crime") — Manuela who, in Elena and Lila's phone-fed mythology and in the pages of Elena's aborted second novel, murdered don Achille to take his place in the usura — the loan trade. Elena feels Lila's eyes on her, lips slightly parted, sees the sarcasm and suddenly suspects: was it Lila who suggested the trap to Michele? She gives the smallest possible toast — tanti auguri — and nothing more. Marcello swoops in to thank her, Pietro echoes, the table joins in: cento di questi giorni — "a hundred more like this!" Manuela fans herself slowly, then faster, with a blue ventaglio showing the gulf and a smoking Vesuvius.

Then Michele gets up, full speech-mode. He praises his mother extravagantly — the Solaras started from nothing at the corner bar, ma è a mia madre che dobbiamo tutto ("but it is to our mother that we owe everything") — and dismisses the voci odiose ("vile rumours") that have lately been circulating about her. Then, abruptly: c'è solo un'altra donna che è quasi come nostra madre. There is only one other woman who is almost like our mother. The room stops. Gigliola's pupils dilate. Elena's mother goes alert: maybe Elisa? But Michele turns greenish, fixes Lila, and calls her over. Lina, vieni qua, per favore.

Lila gives Elena a flicker — now you remember how the game works? — and walks meekly to him. Michele does not touch her, as if a blade lay between them. He lays his fingers a moment on Elena's shoulder instead and addresses her with what passes for tenderness: Elena, you mustn't take this badly, you have gone so far, you are l'orgoglio di tutti noi ("the pride of all of us"). But Lina has una cosa viva nella testa — "something living in her head" — that nobody else has, something doctors can't see, that she herself may not even know she carries: when she is in a bad mood it ruins everyone, when she is in a good mood she leaves them speechless. Io questa sua particolarità da un sacco di tempo me la voglio comprare — for ages now I have wanted to buy that quality of hers. Buy, yes — like pearls, like diamonds. So far it hasn't been possible. But tonight he is celebrating a first small step: ho preso la signora Cerullo a lavorare dentro al Centro meccanografico che ho messo su ad Acerra — I have hired signora Cerullo to run my data-processing centre at Acerra, and Pietro, Elena, you must come and see it tomorrow.

Lila's face contracts in disgust. She does not flay him for the ghoulish image, does not mock the pearls and diamonds, does not slip sideways out of the compliments as she normally would. Instead she lets them toast her hiring as if she had been assunta in cielo — assumed into heaven. Pietro, with his unfailing ease around people he considers inferior, blurts that he would love to visit Acerra and turns to Lila for the technical details. For a moment Elena thinks: if I gave her time she'd take my husband from me too, the way she took Nino. But the thought slides off; she knows Pietro doesn't appeal to Lila and that he is not the type to betray.

What hits her instead is more tangled. In this rione she has always been the one held up as the success story; she had assumed that, at least here, this was indisputable. And now Michele — as if to engineer her declassamento ("demotion") in front of her own family — has arranged for Lila to eclipse her and has made her ratify the eclipsing herself. Elena's mother, across the table, has the look she used to wear when she had to stop herself from slapping her: she wants Elena to fight back, to show what she knows, roba di prima qualità ("first-rate material"), not that cretinata from Acerra. But Elena holds her tongue. Manuela Solara, eyes darting, suddenly fans herself harder: sento caldo, voi pure lo sentite? Don't you feel hot too?
