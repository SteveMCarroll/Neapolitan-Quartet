Capitolo 51 / 123

Two doctors, two verdicts

Summary

The cardiologist Adele has arranged works out of an apartment in via Crispi, walls hung with nineteenth-century portraits and a vast hunting scene. Elena dresses for the visit — Adele's dress, Adele's perfume — so the professorone will speak well of her if he ever mentions her to her mother-in-law. Lila comes as she is. In the waiting room Lila murmurs, half-amused, that Elena looks like the lady in one of the paintings and she herself is the fantesca, the servant.

The exam is thorough. The doctor chats with Elena for ten minutes — his son was at the Scuola Normale, his brother is a writer — before he even turns to the patient. When he does, Lila answers in dialect or in deliberately broken Italian. Elena has to step in to confirm symptoms Lila tries to downplay. After half an hour the verdict comes: the heart is perfect, no soffio after all. What's wrong is general exhaustion. She must eat, sleep eight hours, take a tonic course — and, he adds carefully, see a neurologist for a check.

That word, neurologo, snaps Lila to attention. «Sta dicendo che sono malata di nervi?» — are you telling me I'm crazy? She offers a counter-argument about an aunt who shouted and laughed from her window all her life and never went to a doctor: nervous illness, in Lila's framing, is for signore, ladies. She isn't one. The cardiologist sends them on anyway to a neurologist friend of his on Toledo, whose waiting room shelves hold only philosophy.

This second doctor is a small, bald, voluble man who lectures more than he listens, declaring the anima irascibile and anima concupiscibile are taking the upper hand over the rational soul. His prescriptions are vague — long walks, but not by the sea; reading, but only by day; keep the hands busy, perhaps with crochet. Then Lila cuts in with her own question: could he prescribe le pillole che non fanno fare figli, the pills that keep you from having children? The neurologist stiffens; that, he says, is for a gynaecologist. He scribbles down the address of a sympathetic doctor at a lab on Ponte di Tappia. His secretary makes them pay — he is not, Elena realises, part of Adele's chain of favours.

On the street Lila explodes — she won't take a pill that stronzo prescribed; her head will come unstitched anyway. But she calms, and they wander toward Ponte di Tappia almost without deciding to. Elena asks whether things have changed with Enzo. «Sempre allo stesso modo» — exactly the same. Then why the pill? «Adesso no, ma in seguito chi lo sa» — not now, but later, who knows. They turn into a phone booth, call the lab, and walk over. The woman in the white coat receives them like old friends, talks freely, refuses payment because contraception is a mission she shares with a few other doctors, and sends them off with a prescription each. On the pavement afterwards Lila says quietly: «finalmente una persona perbene» — at last, someone decent. She is cheerful in a way Elena hasn't seen for a long time.
