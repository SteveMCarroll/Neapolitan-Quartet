Storia di chi fugge e di chi resta · Tempo di mezzo

Capitolo 6 / 123

Pietro at the table — Nino vanishes

Summary

Pietro jumps up to embrace her. She has never told him anything about Nino. About Antonio, only a few words; about Franco, only what was already common knowledge in Pisa. Nino, she had kept entirely to herself — the story is too painful, includes too many shameful moments, and telling it properly would mean telling Lila, Ischia, and finally admitting that the scene of sex with a much older man in her novel was based on a real night on the Maronti beach. So she had kept her own counsel. If Pietro had known, he would have understood at a glance the cold mood with which she is now receiving him.

He sits at the head of the table between his mother and Nino. He eats his steak hungrily, drinks wine, but watches her with worry: he can feel her displeasure and is sure it must be because he was late, because he missed the event, because she might read this as not loving her enough. The truth — that her dark face is exactly because he did come, because he has wedged himself between her and Nino — is impossible to say.

Nino, sitting beside her, makes things worse: he doesn't address her even once. He seems delighted by Pietro, pours him wine, lights his cigarettes, asks engaged questions about his work. (He even mentions an article in which Pietro had set latte e miele — milk and honey — against wine and all forms of intoxication.) Pietro, normally taciturn on his own work, opens up. Elena notes the contrast between the two men: Nino lanky and loud, Pietro stocky, with that overgrown tangle of hair on his huge forehead and a low voice.

Just as Pietro is hitting his stride, Adele cuts in: «E la sorpresa per Elena?» — "And Elena's surprise?" Pietro reluctantly announces — half under his breath, irritated to be made to brag — that he has become a professore di ruolo, a tenured professor, at Firenze (Florence), unusually young. Elena understands at once what this really means: prestige, money, a way out of Pisa's poisonous atmosphere, and — most importantly — that they will marry this autumn or early next year and she will leave Naples. The whole table congratulates them. Nino congratulates them too, then looks at his watch, makes a sharp little remark about academic careers, and says he's sorry, he has to go.

They all stand up. In the street Elena hopes wildly that Nino will give her a phone number, an address. He doesn't. He shakes her hand and wishes her all the best. Every gesture now feels like he is deliberately closing the door. As a farewell she mimes writing in the air with an invisible pen — a plea: you know where I live, write to me, please. He has already turned his back.
