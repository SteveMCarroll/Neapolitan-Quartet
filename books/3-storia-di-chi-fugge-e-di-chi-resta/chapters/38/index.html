Capitolo 38 / 123

Sleepless after the visit

Summary

In the car Lila lays into Pasquale — sei diventato il servo di quella gente? "Have you become those people's servant?" — and he lets her spend her fury. Then he launches into his political formulary: the worker's condition in the South, the permanent blackmail, the absent or feeble unions, the necessity to forzare le situazioni and push to struggle. Lina, he says in dialect, his voice catching, you're scared of losing the few lire they pay you, and you're right — Gennaro has to grow. But I know you are una compagna vera, a real comrade; you understand. Down here we workers were never even included in the wage zones; we are sotto zero — below zero. So it's blasphemy to say "leave me alone, I'll mind my own business." Each person, where they happen to be, has to do what they can.

Lila is exhausted; mercifully Gennaro is asleep on the back seat with the little car clutched in his fist. Pasquale's speech reaches her in waves. In between she keeps seeing the Galiani apartment, Armando, Isabella, Nino — who slipped out of her life to find himself da qualche parte una moglie della specie di Nadia, "a wife of the Nadia type, somewhere" — and Marco, three and a half, reading better than her son. Che fatica inutile — what useless effort — trying to make Gennaro clever. The boy is already being pulled backward and she hasn't got the strength to hold him.

When they reach the building she has to invite Pasquale up. Enzo hasn't cooked much, she warns; forse non ti conviene. Maybe skip it. But Pasquale says ten minutes, and as they climb she touches his arm with her fingertips and whispers:

— "Don't tell him anything. About the fascists. If he finds out, he'll go this very night and break Gino's face."

— "Do you love him?"

— "I don't want to hurt him."

Upstairs, they tell Enzo only that they were at Nadia's for a meeting, and Pasquale chatters non-stop till midnight so Enzo can't ask questions. Naples, like all the world, un ribollire di vita nuova — a boiling-up of new life. He praises Armando — a fine doctor who, far from chasing a career, treats the poor for free, who runs a free clinic in the Quartieri with Nadia and Isabella, who is up to his neck in mille progetti al servizio del popolo (a thousand projects in service of the people). Nobody is alone any more, comrades help comrades, the city is living its best moments. Don't stay shut in this house, he tells them; come out, we should be together more. Then he drops his bombshell: he's done with the Communist Party, troppi compromessi — too many compromises. Enzo is upset; they argue late about parties and strategy, no, sì, no, basta con le politiche di stabilizzazione. Lila gets bored fast, puts Gennaro to bed, doesn't come back.

But she can't sleep after Pasquale leaves. She takes her temperature — 38. The reading lesson comes back. Destinazione — what a word to put in front of him. Gennaro had never heard it. Non basta conoscere l'alfabeto — knowing the alphabet isn't enough; the obstacles are many. If Nino had made the child with Nadia, that child would have had a wholly different fate. She feels herself a madre sbagliata, a wrong mother. And yet I wanted him, she thinks: with Stefano I never wanted children; with Nino I did. With Nino there was real love, real desire. She had wanted to please him and had done willingly for his pleasure all the things she had done by force for her husband — vincendo il ribrezzo, solo per non farsi ammazzare (swallowing revulsion just to avoid being killed). But what was supposed to happen inside her when she was penetrated — that thing she had heard about all her life — had not happened, with Stefano or with Nino. Men cared so much about il cazzo (the cock), were so proud of it, and were sure you should care even more.

Even Gennaro spends his time fiddling with his little thing — it is sometimes embarrassing how he twists and pulls. Enzo, by contrast, is discreto: never in his underwear around the house, never a vulgar word. That is why she feels such intense affection for him, and gratitude for his patient waiting in the other bedroom, never once a wrong move. His self-control feels like her only consolation. Then the guilt: what consoles me must be hurting him. The thought that Enzo is suffering because of her piles on top of every other ugly thing of the day. Tones of voice, single words. How should she behave at the factory tomorrow? Was there really all this fervore in Naples and the world, or did Pasquale and Nadia and Armando just imagine it to quiet their own anxieties? Should she trust it and risk being a prisoner of fantasies, or go back to placating Bruno and risk him attacking her again?

She doesn't get very far. Half-asleep, she lands on the old principle she and I learned together as little girls: per salvarsi… dovesse mettere soggezione a chi la voleva tenere in soggezione, dovesse mettere paura a chi voleva metterle paura — to save herself and her son, she must put fear into those who wanted to put fear into her. She falls asleep with two intentions: to wreck Nadia — show her she's nothing but a sweet-talking little rich girl — and to wreck Bruno's pleasure in sniffing salami and women in the curing room.
