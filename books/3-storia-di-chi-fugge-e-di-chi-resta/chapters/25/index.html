Chapter 25 — Pasquale, Enzo, and the trip to San Giovanni

Summary

It's been a while since Elena has seen either Pasquale or Enzo, but there are no pleasantries: they came for Lila and they speak about her straight away. Pasquale has grown a beard come Che Guevara, which suits him — eyes larger and more intense, thick moustache hiding his bad teeth even when he laughs. Enzo is the same: silent, focused. Only once they're in Pasquale's old car does Elena register how surprising it is to see them together. She had assumed nobody from the rione was willing to have anything to do with Lila and Enzo any more. Invece ecco — but no: Pasquale comes to their flat, has driven Enzo here tonight, and Lila sent the two of them together.

Enzo gives the dry, orderly version. Pasquale had been due to stay for dinner after work on a building site near San Giovanni. Lila, who usually got back from the factory at half four, wasn't home by seven. The flat was empty; Gennaro was with the neighbour. The two men cooked, fed the child. Lila finally appeared around nine, pallidissima, molto nervosa — very pale, very on edge. To their questions she gave no answers. The only thing she said — in a frightened voice — was «mi si stanno staccando le unghie», my nails are coming off. False: Enzo took her hands and checked — the nails were fine. Lila got furious, locked herself in her room with Gennaro. After a while she shouted out that they should come and see if Elena was in the rione: she needed to talk to her urgently.

Elena presses Enzo. Did you fight? No. Did she hurt herself at work? He doesn't think so; he doesn't know. Pasquale tries to defuse it: let's not work ourselves up; bet you Lina calms down as soon as she sees you. I'm so glad we found you, ormai sei una persona importante — you're an important person now, you must have a lot on. Elena deflects; Pasquale produces the old Unità article as proof. Enzo nods — he read it too. «Pure Lila l'ha visto» — Lila saw it as well. What did she say? She was happy about the photo. "Though," Pasquale grumbles, "they made it sound like you were still a student. You should write to the paper to say you're laureata."

He vents about all the space l'Unità gives to students; Enzo agrees. The arguments aren't far from what Elena had heard in Milan, only the frasario — vocabulary — is coarser. Pasquale clearly wants to engage her with topics worthy of a woman who appears in the party paper. Maybe he and Enzo are also keeping their own panic — and hers — at bay.

Elena listens. She quickly realises their bond has tightened around passione politica: they see each other often after work, in party meetings or some committee. She replies politely; meanwhile she can't get out of her head the picture of Lila eaten by some unknown anguish — Lila, who had always been the resistant one. By the time they reach San Giovanni she can sense their pride in her. Pasquale especially has hung on every word and kept checking her in the specchietto retrovisore (rear-view mirror). For all his know-it-all manner — he is segretario della sezione rionale del partito comunista, secretary of the local PCI section — he treats her political nod as the seal of his being in the right. So when he feels backed he confides, with some pain, that he and Enzo and others are in a bitter fight inside the party — which, he says — banging the wheel — prefers to wait for un fischio di Aldo Moro (a whistle from Aldo Moro), as you whistle to a dog, rather than make a clean break and go to the showdown. «Che pensi?» he asks. "That's how it is," she says. «Sei brava» — he praises her with solemn weight as they climb the dirty stairs. "You always have been. True, Enzo?"

Enzo nods, but Elena can tell his worry for Lila is rising with each step, as is hers, and he feels guilty for letting himself be distracted by chatter. He opens the door, calls siamo qua — we're here — and points her toward a frosted-glass door with a thin yellow light behind it. She knocks lightly and goes in.
