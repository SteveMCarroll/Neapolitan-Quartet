Storia di chi fugge e di chi resta · Tempo di mezzo

Capitolo 96 / 123

Becoming, outside Lila&#39;s wake

Summary

In the corridor at Acerra Lila throws off scraps of news without ever opening up. Compliments on Pietro: nice husband, intelligent, speaks like a believer though he isn't one, knows so much about Naples that she felt ashamed she knows nothing. Gennaro is growing, mostly under her mother's care, doing well at school. Things with Enzo are good but they barely see each other from work. Stefano has ruined himself: the carabinieri found stolen goods in the back of his shop, he's been arrested and is out now but flat broke — sono io che do soldi a lui, non lui a me, "I'm the one giving him money, not the other way round." See how things turn? If she had stayed la signora Carracci she'd be on the floor like the rest of them; instead she is Raffaella Cerullo, capocentro for Michele Solara at quattrocentoventimila lire al mese. Her mother treats her like a queen, her father has forgiven everything, her brother bleeds her for cash, Pinuccia is suddenly devoted, the children call her zietta. But the work bores her: too slow, too much wasted time. Speed devours everything — like a photo come out mosso, blurred, without sharp edges. Alfonso used the phrase the other day, joking about himself: è venuto mosso, senza linee chiare di contorno.

Then Lila pivots into something stranger. Alfonso lately won't stop talking about amicizia — friendship. He wants to be her closest friend. He'd like to copy her con la carta copiativa — with carbon paper. He swears he'd love to be a femmina like her. "Quale femmina," she told him, "you are a man, you don't know what I'm like, and even if we are friends and you study me and copy me, you will never know." And then he confessed — vuole bene a Michele da sempre, he has always loved Michele, Michele Solara, sì — and he wishes Michele wanted him the way Michele wants her. Tu capisci, Lenù, che cosa succede alle persone — do you understand what is happening to people? — we have too much inside us, it bloats us, breaks us. She told Alfonso: fine, we'll be friends, but get out of your head this idea that you can be a femmina the way I am; the most you'll manage is la femmina secondo voi maschi — the female as you men imagine her. Puoi copiarmi, farmi il ritratto preciso come fanno gli artisti, ma la mia merda resterà sempre la mia, e la tua la tua. You can copy me, you can paint my exact portrait, but my shit will always stay mine, and yours yours. Ah Lenù, what is happening to all of us, we are like pipes when the water freezes — che brutta cosa è la testa scontenta, what an ugly thing the discontent head is. Remember what we did with my wedding photograph? I want to keep going down that road. A day will come when I'll be reduced entirely to diagrams, I'll become a punched tape, you won't find me any more.

Little laughs, nothing more. Those corridor confidences confirm to Elena that the bond has lost its intimacy. It has shrunk to terse news, jokes, parole in libertà — loose words — no real disclosure shared only with her. Lila's life is hers alone now. It's pointless to ask what she knows about Pasquale, where he has gone, how involved she was in Bruno Soccavo's death or Filippo's kneecapping, what made her say yes to Michele, what she means to do with his dependence on her. Lila has retreated into the inconfessabile — the unconfessable. Even now, writing, Elena has no firm material on which to begin a sentence with Lila went, Lila did, Lila met, Lila planned. And yet on the drive back to Florence she has the impression that there in the rione, tra arretratezza e modernità ("between backwardness and modernity"), Lila has more story than she does. How much I've missed by going away, she thinks, believing I was destined for who knows what life. Lila, who stayed, has a brand-new job, earns plenty, acts in full freedom according to disegni — designs — that remain indecifrabili. She is devoted to her son and yet able to set him aside when she chooses; he does not give her the anxiety Elena's girls give her. She has broken with her family of origin and still shoulders them when she can. She looks after the disgraced Stefano without any reconciliation. She loathes the Solaras and bends to them. She mocks Alfonso and befriends him. She says she won't see Nino again and Elena knows she will. La sua era una vita mossa, la mia era ferma. Her life is in motion, mine is at a standstill.

Driving with Pietro silent and the girls bickering in the back, Elena makes the prophecy that will haunt this book and the next: Lila will take Nino back; she will arrange a meeting, condition him the way only she can, peel him from his wife and son, use him in a war whose enemy is no longer clear, make him divorce, slip free of Michele after extracting plenty of his money, leave Enzo, finally divorce Stefano, and maybe marry Nino — or maybe not — but for sure they will pool their two intelligences and become something Elena cannot picture.

Diventare. "To become." It is a verb that has always obsessed her, and only now does she notice it. She wanted to become — though she never knew quite what. And she had become, no doubt, but without a true object, senza una vera passione, senza un'ambizione determinata — without true passion or settled ambition. She had wanted to become something only because she was afraid Lila would become someone and she would be left behind. Il mio diventare era diventare dentro la sua scia. My becoming was a becoming inside her wake. I have to start again — per me, da adulta, fuori di lei — on my own terms, as an adult, outside of her.
