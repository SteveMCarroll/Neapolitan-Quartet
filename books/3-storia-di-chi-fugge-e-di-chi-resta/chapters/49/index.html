Storia di chi fugge e di chi resta · Tempo di mezzo

Capitolo 49 / 123

Summary

The two phone calls — to Pietro, then to Adele — release a feeling Elena has been holding down. It has to do with her change of status. The Airota, probably Guido especially, perhaps Adele too, see her as a nice enough girl but a long way from what they would have wanted for their son. Her origin, her dialect cadence, her overall lack of elegance must stretch their broadmindedness. With some excess she could even imagine the publication of her book as part of an emergency plan to make her presentable in their world. But the fact remains — incontrovertibly — that they accepted her, that she's about to marry Pietro with their consent, that she is about to enter a protective family, una sorta di castello ben fortificato — a kind of well-fortified castle from which she can advance without fear, into which she can retreat if she feels endangered.

So she has to get used to this new belonging, especially become aware of it. She is no longer the little match-girl always one match away from the last one — she has a substantial reserve of matches now. And therefore — she suddenly understands — she can do for Lila much more than she had reckoned.

In this frame she asks Lila for the documentation she'd assembled against Soccavo and Lila hands it over passively, not even asking what Elena means to do with it. Elena reads with growing involvement. How many terrible things Lila has managed to say con precisione ed efficacia — precisely and effectively. How much unbearable experience behind the description of the plant. She turns the pages a long time; then, almost without deciding, looks up the number in the directory and phones Soccavo. She bends her voice to the right pitch, with the right amount of haughtiness: Pronto, sono Elena Greco, mi feci passare Bruno.

He is cordial — che piacere sentirti. She is cold. He praises her — ho visto una tua foto sul Roma, brava, che bei tempi quelli di Ischia. She answers that yes, it's nice to hear him, but Ischia is a long time ago, for better or worse everyone has changed, and about him she has heard ugly things she hopes aren't true. He catches on instantly and detonates: a torrent of abuse about Lila — l'ingratitudine, i guai che gli aveva combinato. Elena changes register: I believe Lila more than I believe you. Get a pen and paper. Write down my number. Now order that her last lira be paid — and let me know when I can come collect it. Non vorrei che comparisse anche la tua foto sui giornali — I wouldn't want your picture to end up in the papers too. She hangs up before he can object.

She's proud of herself: not a flicker of emotion, just a few sentences in Italian, polite then distant. She hopes Pietro is right — that she really is acquiring Adele's tone, learning, without noticing, il suo modo di stare al mondo, her way of being in the world.

Now she wants to know if she can back the threat. More nervous than she was with Bruno — still, after all, the boring boy who once tried to kiss her on the beach at Citara — she dials l'Unità. While the phone rings she hopes her mother's voice won't echo from the back of the apartment yelling something in dialect at Elisa. Mi chiamo Elena Greco, she says to the operator. The operator interrupts: Elena Greco la scrittrice? — the writer? She has read the book; effusive compliments. Elena, suddenly cheerful, suddenly strong, explains she has in mind an article about a little factory on the outskirts and gives the editor's name Adele suggested. The operator gushes again, then becomes professional: Aspetti in linea.

The man who comes on is very gruff, his greeting amused. Da quando in qua i cultori delle belle lettere sono disposti a sporcarsi la penna col cottimo, i turni e gli straordinari, roba noiosissima da cui si tenevano lontano in particolare le giovani romanziere di successo? Since when are cultori delle belle lettere — practitioners of belles lettres — willing to dirty their pens with piecework, shifts and overtime, very boring stuff, the kind young successful lady novelists in particular steer well clear of?

Di che si tratta? Edili, portuali, minatori? What's it about — construction workers, dockers, miners? — È una fabbrichetta di salumi, she murmurs — a little salami plant, nothing much. He keeps teasing: don't apologize, if Elena Greco, a cui questo giornale ha dedicato nientemeno che mezza pagina di lodi sperticate, to whom this newspaper devoted no less than half a page of unbridled praise, has decided to write about sausages, can we poor editors say it doesn't interest us? Trenta righe le vanno bene? Sono poche? Abbondiamo, facciamo sessanta. Thirty lines? Too few — let's be generous, sixty.

She writes the article. She has to squeeze her sixty lines out of Lila's pages and she wants to do well by her. She has no real experience of journalistic reporting — apart from a botched fifteen- year-old's piece against the religion teacher for Nino's magazine. The editor's sarcasm has lodged in her ears — especially his sign-off asking her to give his regards to her mother-in-law. She works long. When she thinks it's done she isn't satisfied and doesn't take it to the paper. Devo parlare prima con Lila, she tells herself, this is something to decide together; I'll deliver tomorrow.

Next day at Lila's, Lila looks particularly bad. She mutters that when Elena isn't there, certe presenze se ne approfittavano e uscivano dalle cose per molestare lei e Gennaro, certain presences take advantage and come out of things to torment her and Gennaro. Seeing Elena alarmed, Lila brightens her face and says it's nonsense, she just wanted Elena to spend more time with her. They talk a long time. Elena does not show her the article. The reason: if l'Unità rejects it, Elena would have to tell Lila it wasn't good enough, and the humiliation would be too much.

What pushes her over the line in the evening is Adele's call. Adele has consulted Guido and Mariarosa and moved half the world in a few hours: luminari della medicina — medical luminaries — and socialist professors who know labor law, and a Christian Democrat she calls un po' babbeo — a bit dim — but a decent man, very expert in workers' rights. Result: tomorrow Elena has an appointment with the best cardiologist in Naples — a friend of friends, no fee. The ispettorato del lavoro will visit Soccavo immediately. For Lila's money, Elena can go to the lawyer Mariarosa pointed her to — a young socialist with an office in piazza Nicola Amore, already briefed.

Contenta? Yes. — Hai scritto il tuo articolo? — Yes. — Vedi? Ero sicura che non l'avresti fatto. — It's ready. I'm taking it to l'Unità tomorrow. — Brava. Corro il rischio di sottovalutarti. — Is that a risk? — La sottovalutazione lo è sempre. — And then, without transition: Come va con quella povera bestiola che è mio figlio? How are things with my son, that poor little beast?
