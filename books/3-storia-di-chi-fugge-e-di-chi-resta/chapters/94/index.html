Capitolo 94 / 123

After dinner — Pietro&#39;s verdict on Lila

Summary

Once the children are asleep and the bedroom door closes behind them in Elisa's apartment, Pietro asks half-scandalised, half-amused: dove siamo finiti? — "where have we ended up?" He wants to laugh about the evening's worst moments. Elena lays into him instead, in a low furious whisper. From the swarm of feelings inside her surfaces again the old, terrifying wish — that Lila were ill, that she would die. Not from hatred: Elena loves her more than ever. What she cannot bear is il vuoto del suo sottrarsi — the vacuum of her withdrawal. How did Pietro think it was acceptable, she hisses, to let them hijack the luggage and move them all into this house under cover of hospitality? Non sapevo che gente era, Pietro says — I didn't know what kind of people they were. No, Elena fires back, the truth is you never listen to me; I've told you again and again where I come from.

They argue at length. He tries to calm her; she pours everything out. He was too timid; he let them walk all over him; he only knows how to dig in his heels with the well-bred people of his own world; she no longer trusts him, no longer trusts his mother either. How is it possible the German edition of her novel came out two years ago and the publisher said nothing? In which other countries has the book appeared without her knowing? She wants to get to the bottom of all of it. Pietro, to placate her, agrees about everything, suggests she telephone Adele and the press first thing in the morning.

Then he changes subject. He speaks with real sympathy of l'ambiente popolare — the working-class world — she comes from. He calls her mother generous and remarkably intelligent. He has warm words for her father, for Elisa, for Gigliola, for Enzo. But his tone hardens sharply when he turns to the Solaras: due farabutti, due furbi malvagi, due delinquenti melliflui — "two scoundrels, two malevolent schemers, two honey-tongued criminals."

And finally he comes to Lila. He says, quietly: è quella che mi ha turbato di più — "she is the one who disturbed me the most." Elena flares: I noticed, you talked to her all evening. But Pietro shakes his head and to her surprise says Lila struck him as the worst person there. She is not Elena's friend; she despises Elena. Yes, she is extraordinarily intelligent. Yes, she is very charming. But hers is l'intelligenza maligna che semina discordia e odia la vita — "an evil intelligence that sows discord and hates life" — and her charm is the most intolerable kind, il fascino che asserve — the kind that enslaves and ruins. Just so.

At first Elena listens with feigned protest and secret satisfaction. She had read this wrong, then; Lila had failed to land with him; Pietro is trained to read subtext and he has spotted her dark sides easily. But soon he overshoots. Non capisco come questo vostro rapporto sia potuto durare tanto, he says — "I don't understand how your relationship has lasted this long" — clearly you both work hard to hide whatever would shatter it. And then the worst line of all: Lila e quel Michele sono fatti l'uno per l'altra — "she and that Michele are made for each other; if they aren't already lovers, they will be."

Elena turns on him. She hisses that she will not put up with his high-handed borghese stracolto tone — overeducated, bourgeois — and that he is not to speak about her friend again like that. While she attacks, she begins to see something he doesn't yet see himself: Lila did land; Pietro registered the scale of her and frightened himself and now needs to belittle her. Not for himself — for her, Elena. He's afraid Lila will pull Elena away from him even at this distance, that she will tear them apart. To protect Elena he is exaggerating, throwing mud, hoping she will be disgusted enough to cut Lila out of her life. Elena murmurs buonanotte, turns over, faces the wall.
