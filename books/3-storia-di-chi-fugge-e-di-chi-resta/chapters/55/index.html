Capitolo 55 / 123

Gigliola in Posillipo: &#39;lui vuole bene a Lina&#39;

Summary

Elena takes the metro to Mergellina and climbs up to Posillipo, partly on foot, partly by bus. She is curious. She belongs now to a power people respect — a power haloed with high culture — and she wants to see what loud costume the other power, the one she grew up under, has put on. The crime, the strutting impunity, the smiling end-runs around the law, the showy waste — all of it in Solara form. But Michele is out. On the top floor of a brand-new building she finds only Gigliola, who lets her in with visible surprise and visible astio, sourness. Elena realises that as long as she'd kept using Signora Spagnuolo's telephone the family had been warm; the moment she got her own line, the Spagnuolos quietly fell out of her life.

Gigliola sulks, then her vanity takes over. She wants Elena to envy her, and she walks her room by room through the still-unfinished flat: the costly furniture, the chandeliers, two big bathrooms, the huge water heater, the fridge, the washing machine, three telephones not yet active, an enormous television, and finally the terrace — a hanging garden whose flowers are wasted by the grim weather. «Guarda, l'hai mai visto il mare così?» Have you ever seen the sea like this? Vesuvius is wrapped in violet shadow; through a gap in the storm-clouds an arc of bleached light pours down on it. Elena is hypnotised. Naples sells, at a steep price, concrete viewpoints over an extraordinary landscape, and Michele has bought a memorable one.

«Non c'è confronto con la casa di Lina al rione, no?» No comparison. «Qui è assai più da signori.» Yes. Yes. Yes. Then, suddenly, Gigliola turns. The vanity curdles into ironic self-mockery. She tallies their schoolmates' fates — Carmen with the petrol-pump man, Pinuccia ruined behind Rino, Ada the puttana di Stefano — and contrasts them with herself: she has Michele, beata me, lucky me, who's finally decided to marry her, who's installing her here, who's throwing a wedding bigger than the Shah of Persia's with Soraya. Tu pensi che esisto? Do you think I exist? Do I exist? She slaps her own breast as if to show her hand goes right through her. Michele took her at fourteen, used her up, and now at twenty-five he barely looks at her. Chiava di qua e di là come gli pare — he sleeps around as he pleases. In front of people, when somebody asks how many children they want, he sneers: ask Gigliola, I already have children, I don't even know how many. He never loved her. He's marrying her because a wife is something a man has to have. He calls her stupid, useless, says she'd make any beautiful house ugly. She begins to cry. «Scusa, parlo così perché hai scritto quel libro che m'è piaciuto e so che conosci i dolori.»

Then comes the secret, and Gigliola makes Elena promise to keep it. (Lo scrivo qui, adesso, solo perché lei è morta — narrative voice intruding from the future.) Michele loves Lila. Not as he's ever loved anyone, and not as he'll love anyone. Since the day Lila put the blade of a shoemaker's knife to Marcello's throat. He's said it himself, drunk. He's counted out for Gigliola the 122 women he's been with and said: you're in the list, and not among the ones I enjoyed. With Gigliola he can't even get a decent bocchino — she's no good, he says, you can feel that it disgusts her. He sneered at her for an evening — only an idiot can't fottere bene, fuck well. Then he turned melancholy. Women, he said, sono pazzièlle con un poco di buchi per giocarci — little toys with a few holes to play with. All of them. All except one. Lila is the only woman in the world he loves — amava, sì, come nei film, loves the way they love in films — and respects. Lei sì che avrebbe saputo arredare questa casa. Spending money on her — quello sì che sarebbe stato un piacere.

He keeps going. He thinks about Lila day and night, but not with ordinary desire. He doesn't want her the way he wants the others — to flip them, to open them, to break them, to grind them under his feet. La voleva nella delicatezza della testa piena di idee. La voleva nell'inventiva. E la voleva senza guastarla, per farla durare. He wants her in the delicacy of the head full of ideas. He wants her in the inventiveness. He wants her without spoiling her, so she'll last. He doesn't want to fottersela — that word applied to Lila bothers him. He wants to kiss her and stroke her. He wants to be stroked by her, helped, guided, comandato — commanded. He wants to see her change with time, grow old. He wants to think alongside her and be helped to think. Capisci? He has spoken of her, to Gigliola his fiancée, as he has never spoken to Gigliola of anything. Mio fratello Marcello, e quel coglione di Stefano, ed Enzo con la sua faccia di culo, cosa hanno capito di Lina? His brother Marcello, that idiot Stefano, Enzo with his asshole face — what have any of them understood about Lina? Lo so soltanto io cos'è lei, chi è. L'ho riconosciuta.

For a moment Gigliola says she thought about killing him in his sleep and taking her Michele back. Lila, no — she doesn't hate Lila. She hated her once, when Michele took the piazza dei Martiri shop away from her to send her back behind the pastry counter. Now she even feels affection for her: almeno c'è una che gli farà cacare sangue — at least there's one who'll make him bleed. Elena offers thin comfort — if he's marrying you, however he talks, he must care for you. Gigliola shakes her head violently: non lo conosci, nessuno lo conosce come me. And to Elena's question — could Michele lose his head and hurt Lila? — Gigliola makes a noise between a laugh and a cry. Lui? A Lina? He can hurt anyone, even his father or mother or brother. He can hurt everyone Lila cares about — her child, Enzo. He can do it cold. Ma a lei, a lei in persona, non farà mai niente. But Lila herself — he will never touch.
