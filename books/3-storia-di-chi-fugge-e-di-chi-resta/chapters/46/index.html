Capitolo 46 / 123

Summary

Now we are back fully inside Elena's voice. She has been a quiet listener, letting Lila talk. Some moments — when Lila's face and sentences contract painfully — disturb her deeply. Questa è la vita che poteva toccarmi — this is the life that could have been mine, she thinks, e se non è successo è anche merito suo — and if it didn't happen, that's partly thanks to her. She nearly hugs her, more often wants to ask questions or chime in. She lets herself interrupt only two or three times.

She intervened over the Galiani. She wanted Lila to repeat her former professor's exact words about her — had her name come up with Nadia and Armando? — but caught herself in the smallness of the question. Aloud she only said: before I leave for Florence I should stop by Professor Galiani to say goodbye; come with me if you like. I rapporti si sono un po' raffreddati, dopo Ischia, lei ha dato la colpa a me se Nino ha lasciato Nadia. Relations have cooled since Ischia; she blamed me for Nino's leaving Nadia. She tries to soften the heart verdict: the Galiani are well-meaning but supercilious, the murmur should be verified by someone else. Lila, sharp: Il soffio c'è. The murmur is there. Armando — even Armando — heard it.

But it's on sex that Elena feels personally implicated. When Lila described the curing-room assault, Elena nearly told her: in Turin an old intellectual jumped on me; in Milan a Venezuelan painter I'd known two hours came into my room and into my bed as if it were a favor I owed him. She holds her tongue. What was the point.

The question gets sharper when Lila — for the first time in their adult friendship — moves from sexual events to her own sexuality. In the crude vocabulary of the rione, Lila says that chiavare (screwing) has never given her the pleasure she expected as a girl; that after Stefano, after Nino, doing it actually annoys her; that she hasn't even been able to bring herself to take Enzo, gentle as he is. Even with Nino, even at the height of love, even when she conceived Gennaro, the pleasure people talked about wasn't there.

Elena ought to match the confidence. The vulgar dialect register disgusts her; the precious Italian she's built doesn't fit either; the discomfort wins. She cuts short. Per me non è così — for me it's not like that.

It isn't a lie. It isn't the truth either. The truth would need words they don't have. The truth would be: with Antonio, rubbing up against him, letting him touch me, always gave me great pleasure, and I still want that pleasure. With Franco — what I know about sex I mostly owe to him — penetration disappointed me too, ruined by guilt, by the discomfort of the conditions, fear of being caught, the hurry, the terror of pregnancy. But before and after, he let me rub myself against his leg, his belly, and that was lovely — sometimes it made penetration itself lovely. I'm waiting for marriage, Pietro is very kind, in the calm legitimacy of the marriage bed I'll have time and quiet to discover the pleasure of coitus. That would have been honest. At nearly twenty-five they have no tradition for confidences that articulated. Of Donato Sarratore she has said nothing; of Franco, nothing.

So her per me non è così must sound, to Lila, like maybe you aren't normal. Lila, defensive: Nel libro hai scritto un'altra cosa. You wrote another thing in your book. Then she read it. Elena murmurs that she barely remembers what's in it. Lila: there's filthy stuff in it, roba che i maschi non vogliono sentire e le femmine conoscono ma hanno paura di dire — stuff men don't want to hear and women know but are afraid to say. Adesso però che fai, ti nascondi? Now what — you hide?

She used the word sporche, dirty, just like Gigliola once did. She doesn't deliver a full verdict on the book; she uses it as a bridge back to repeating, with insistence, il fastidio di chiavare — the nuisance of screwing. That's what's in your novel, she says. If you wrote it, you know it; don't tell me per me non è così.

And while Lila keeps offering shameless, tormented confidences — great arousal, little satisfaction, a sense of disgust — Elena's mind keeps sliding back to Nino. Should she tell Lila she saw him again? That Gennaro won't get any help from him, that he has another son, leaves sons behind without thinking? Should she tell her what Nino said in Milan — Lila è fatta male anche nel sesso, Lila is badly made even sexually? In the confidences Lila is now making, even in the way she reads the dirty pages of Elena's book, isn't Nino's verdict, in substance, being confirmed? Lui è esperto, Elena tells herself. He's been with many women, he knows good and bad. Bad means: not managing to enjoy the male's blows, twisting in want while rubbing against him to settle the desire, seizing his hands and pressing them to your sex the way she once did with Franco, ignoring his annoyance, even the boredom of someone who's already come and wants to drift off. The discomfort grows. Did I write this in my novel? Did Gigliola recognize it? Did Lila? Did Nino — and is that why he wanted to talk about it? She pushes it all back, mumbles:

Mi dispiace. — I'm sorry.

— For what?

— That you got pregnant without joy.

Lila, with a sudden flash of sarcasm: Figurati a me. Imagine how I feel.

When dawn comes Elena finally cuts her off. She has Lila take her temperature: thirty-eight and a half (38.5 °C). She hugs her: I'll take care of you, and if I have to go to Florence you and the boy come with me. Lila refuses energetically, then offers her last confession of the night: she shouldn't have followed Enzo to San Giovanni, she wants to go back to the rione.

— You're crazy. — As soon as I'm better I'm doing it. Elena scolds: this is the fever talking, the rione will finish you off, going back is stupid. Io non vedo l'ora di andarmene, she exclaims — I can't wait to leave. Lila: Tu sei forte, io non lo sono mai stata. Tu tanto più ti senti vera e stai bene, quanto più ti allontani. Io, se solo passo il tunnel dello stradone, mi spavento. Ricordi quando cercammo di raggiungere il mare ma venne a piovere? Chi di noi due voleva continuare ad andare avanti e chi invece fece dietrofront, io o tu? You're strong; I never was. The more you go away, the more real and well you feel. If I just cross the tunnel under the boulevard, I'm afraid. Remember when we tried to reach the sea and it started to rain? Which of us wanted to keep going and which one turned back?

— I don't remember. And you're not going back to the rione.

Elena tucks her in, covers Gennaro too. As she leaves the room Lila stirs in half-sleep: Guardami finché non mi addormento. Guardami sempre, anche quando te ne vai da Napoli. Così so che mi vedi e sto tranquilla. Watch me until I fall asleep. Watch me always, even when you've left Naples. Then I'll know you see me and I'll be calm.

And just before the end she calls back the promise: Se mi succede qualcosa, devi prenderti Gennaro. If something happens to me, you take Gennaro.

Key vocabulary

Italian English Note
chiavare to screw, to fuck the dialect verb Lila uses repeatedly — there is no neutral Italian equivalent in this register
il fastidio di chiavare the nuisance / annoyance of screwing her formulation of what sex is for her
il soffio the murmur what Armando heard — Lila insists it's real
sboccatezza foulness of speech the rione's defense-and-attack register — useful for aggression, useless for intimate confidence
confidenze (intimate) confidences what they don't have a tradition of making to each other
spudoratezza tormentata tormented shamelessness Elena's phrase for Lila's tone in this conversation
roba sporca dirty stuff what Lila and earlier Gigliola called the racy pages of Elena's novel
fatta male nel sesso badly made even sexually Nino's dismissal of Lila to Elena in Milan
amplesso coitus, sexual embrace the formal Italian Elena would use in her head, never out loud
dietrofront about-face, turning back Lila's word for who turned back from the sea — and her self-diagnosis
stradone the wide boulevard the artery that separates the rione from the rest of Naples; "the tunnel under it" is Lila's threshold of fear
dormiveglia half-sleep, drowse the state in which Lila murmurs her final asks
