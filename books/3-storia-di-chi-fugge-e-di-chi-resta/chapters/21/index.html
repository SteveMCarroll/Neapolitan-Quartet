Chapter 21 — Rione gossip and Gigliola&#39;s verdict

Summary

Back in Naples, Elena doesn't make time to chase the questions Milan left her with. Pietro phones to say he'll come down the following week to meet her family; Elena treats the visit as una disgrazia inevitabile (an unavoidable misfortune), books him a hotel, scrubs the flat, and tries to calm her parents.

The rione has only got worse since the book came out. Neighbours are scandalised, and Immacolata — to dampen the gossip — has started telling everyone Elena is about to be married, but in Genova rather than Naples, "to keep her godless choices out of sight." Predictably, the lie has only fed the chatter. One evening her mother corners her: the butcher's sons called Elena a zoccola (slut) in the street, so Peppe and Gianni gave them mazzate — a beating; another classmate asked Elisa to do "the dirty things her big sister does," and got his face broken for it. «Che hai scritto?» — what did you write? — her mother screams. Elena tells her to read it for herself. The fight ends with the usual threat: if your father hears what they're saying, he'll throw you out. «Me ne vado io» — I'll go myself, Elena snaps, and walks out before she says something she'll regret.

The streets feel hostile, like ombre stizzose — irritable shadows — of a world she no longer belongs to. On the stradone she runs into Gigliola Spagnuolo, who lives in the same building. To Elena's surprise the always-aggressive Gigliola is shy, almost reverent. She has read the book and loved it. "Che coraggio hai avuto a scrivere quelle cose" — what courage you had to write those things. Quelle cose, she clarifies, means the beach scene. Elena protests: it's the character, not me. Gigliola brushes that off — she recognised it: «Sono segreti che si sanno solo se una è femmina» (those are secrets only a woman knows).

Then Gigliola grips her arm and asks her to pass on a message to Lila. Tell her she was right, she says: right to fottersene of her husband, her mother, her father, her brother, of Marcello, Michele, di tutta la merda — all the shit. "I should have escaped from here too, taking the two of you as my model. But I was born stupid and there's nothing I can do about it."

Elena climbs to her landing in silence. Two things stick. Gigliola has lumped Lila's collapse and her own ascent together as if, measured against her own life, they had the same positive sign. And, more unsettling, she has recognised in the smut of Elena's novel her own experience of smut. Un fatto nuovo, a new fact — Elena doesn't know what to do with it. Pietro arrives, and she forgets.
