Capitolo 64 / 123

The baby will not nurse

Summary

What looked like easy good fortune ends as soon as they leave the clinic. Dede, who latched fine in the hospital, refuses Elena's breast at home. She sucks for a few seconds, then screams come una bestiola furiosa — like a furious little animal. Elena, helpless, slides into old superstitions. Are her nipples too small? Does the milk taste wrong? Has someone put a maleficio — an evil eye — on her from a distance? She drags Dede from doctor to doctor by herself; Pietro is always at the university. Her chest swells uselessly, then turns to hot stones; she fears infection, fears amputation. She tortures herself with a breast pump to drain enough for the bottle. She whispers to the baby — succhia, dài, sei così buona, sei così dolce ("suck, come on, you're so good, so sweet") — and it changes nothing. She gives in: mixed feeding, then full formula, then a punishing regimen of sterilizing bottles and weighing the baby before and after each feed, guilt over every loose stool. She remembers Silvia in Milan, in the middle of the student assembly, breastfeeding Nino's son Mirko con naturalezza — naturally. Why not me? She cries long secret cries.

For a week things calm. Then for the whole first year, Dede does not sleep. The tiny body twists and shrieks for hours with shocking energy. Elena can only quiet her by walking her up and down the apartment, talking: ora questa creatura splendida della sua mamma fa la brava, ora sta zitta, ora riposa — now mama's splendid little creature is good, now she's quiet, now she rests. The baby won't sleep; she seems to fear sleep, like her father. Elena cycles through explanations: stomach, hunger, abandonment from the failed breastfeeding, the evil eye, a demon, her own milk. And the leg — isn't the leg starting to hurt again? Is it her mother's curse for having spent a lifetime trying not to resemble her?

One night, in the dark, she remembers Gigliola going around the rione saying Lila has a terrible potenza ("power"), that she can work fire spells, that she suffocates creatures in the womb. Elena is ashamed and tries to push it away — she needs to sleep. She finally asks Pietro, who works through the night anyway, to take over for two hours. She falls into sleep like fainting. An hour later Dede's screaming wakes her. She gets up. Pietro has dragged the crib into his study and is hunched over his cards as if deaf, ignoring his daughter. Elena loses every shred of manners and regresses entirely — she insults him nel mio dialetto, in dialect: te ne fotti di tutto, quella roba è più importante di tua figlia? — "you don't give a damn about anything, that stuff is more important than your daughter?" Pietro, cool and distant, tells her to leave the room and take the crib. He has an article for an English journal, the deadline is tomorrow. From then on she stops asking him for help, and when he offers she waves him off: vai, grazie, lo so che hai da fare — "go on, thanks, I know you have things to do." After dinner he hovers, uncertain, then shuts himself in his study and works until late.
