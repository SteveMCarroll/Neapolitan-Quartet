Capitolo 63 / 123

Astronauts on the moon

Summary

The pregnancy is confirmed and Elena, panicked, calls her mother. It's a mistake: Immacolata wants to come live with her, or drag her back to the rione and the old midwife. Elena fends her off — she has Adele's gynecologist, a great professor, she'll deliver at his clinic. Her mother hisses preferisci tua suocera a me ("you prefer your mother-in-law to me") and stops calling.

A few days later Lila phones. She's heard about the pregnancy from Immacolata bragging in the rione, not from Elena. She catches her former friend out: credevo che prendessi la pillola — "I thought you were on the pill." Then she launches into a monologue on what motherhood actually is, leaning on her own two pregnancies: the body won't be deformed, the pain is too much, the new life ti tiene al guinzaglio — keeps you on a leash. She talks as if it's inconceivable that Elena's experience could differ from hers; any future joy of motherhood would feel like a betrayal. Elena holds the receiver away from her ear. They hang up coolly. Tu mi hai aiutata, ora ti voglio aiutare io, Lila says — "you helped me, now I want to help you." It doesn't help; it leaves Elena rattled.

Florence stays strange. The colors of the houses depress her, the shopkeepers' Tuscan teasing sets her on edge. Adele's swarm of friends who'd been so warm at the reception never reappear. She buys books on how to be a perfect mother and prepares with her usual diligence.

Then, against the forecast, the pregnancy is easy. No real nausea, energy, mood, work — all intact. In her fourth month her novel wins a significant prize and she goes to collect it, despite the hostile political climate. In the speech she overshoots and says she feels come gli astronauti sulla distesa bianca della luna — "like the astronauts on the white expanse of the moon." A couple of days later she calls Lila to tell her about the prize. Lila has read the line in il Mattino and tears into it: the moon is one rock among billions, la cosa migliore era stare coi piedi ben piantati dentro i guai della terra — keep your feet planted in earth's troubles. Then she pivots brightly into her own news: peace made with her brother and parents; another fight with Michele Solara over old debts on the shoe brand; pressure on Stefano to at least support Gennaro financially the way he supports Maria. She uses a hard, vulgar register about all of them and asks Elena if she did right. Elena doesn't answer. To wound, she asks if Lila still has the symptoms that "unhook her head." Lila says no — solo qualche volta vedo con la coda dell'occhio gente che esce dai mobili — "only sometimes I see people, out of the corner of my eye, coming out of the furniture."

She travels heavily through the pregnancy, writing on factory strikes and boss reactions, slipping through picket lines and police, feeling invulnerable. She's in Milan at the publisher when a bomb tears apart the Banca dell'Agricoltura; nothing fazes her. Late, she's at a Nuovo Pignone clash, takes a wrong step, and a hot iron of pain shoots from her right buttock down her leg. She terrifies herself: she has been caught by il passo di mia madre — her mother's limping gait, lodged in her body. The professor reassures her — sciatica from the weight — and the pain fades. Adele is born February 12, 1970, at 5:20 a.m. They call her Adele. Her mother screams horrible things on the phone over the missed baptism, vows never to come see the child. Elena calls Lila to head off another grievance. È stata un'esperienza bellissima, she says — it was beautiful. Lila answers: ognuno si racconta la vita come gli fa comodo — "everyone tells their life the way it suits them."
