Capitolo 73 / 123

The second novel, in a square-ruled notebook

Summary

The phone call about don Achille lingers. For the first time in a long while, Elena feels she and Lila have recovered something of their old affiatamento — their old back-and-forth — even if it was only an intertwining of breaths on the line. They haven't seen each other in years. Lila has no idea what Elena looks like after two pregnancies; Elena has no idea whether Lila is still white-thin or has changed. For some time now, she realizes, she has been talking to a mental image that the voice lazily reanimates.

By the next morning, the murder of don Achille has turned, in her head, into the kernel of a possible story. After the call she can't sleep; she begins to hear in that material a kind of ledge she could lean out from to seize a narrative. In the days that follow she mixes Florence and Naples, the disorders of the present with old voices, her present comfort with everything it cost her to wrench herself out of her origins, the anxiety of losing everything against the fascination of going back. She convinces herself she can make a book of it. With effort and continual painful second thoughts, she fills a quaderno a quadretti — a square-ruled notebook — with a plot that welds together the last twenty years of violence. Lila phones sometimes: "Why don't I hear from you, are you sick?" / "I'm fine, I'm writing." / "And when you write, I no longer exist?" / "You exist, but I get distracted."

The work is nothing like the cheerful unconsciousness with which she dashed off her first novel. Once the story is roughed in, she labors over a second draft, going back over every line until — thanks to a Lettera 32 she bought while expecting Dede, and carbon paper — she has turned the notebooks into a solid typescript in triplicate, nearly two hundred pages, without a single typo. It is summer; she is enormous; the pain in her gluteus has come back; her mother's footfalls in the corridor grate on her nerves.

She is afraid of the pages. For days she can't decide. Showing it to Pietro worries her — he is in the wrong frame of mind for a novel full of workers, bosses, fights, blood, camorristi, loan sharks. Besides, with his usual stubbornness, he is making his own life impossible at the university, coming home in knots, lecturing her in the abstract about il valore della legalità — the value of legality. Mi tiene lontana dal garbuglio che ha dentro — he keeps me at arm's length from his own tangle — non s'è mai interessato a ciò che ero e a ciò che sono diventata. She sends Adele a copy instead and calls her: "I've finished." / "I'm so happy. Will you let me have it?" / "I sent it to you this morning." / "Wonderful, I can't wait to read it."
