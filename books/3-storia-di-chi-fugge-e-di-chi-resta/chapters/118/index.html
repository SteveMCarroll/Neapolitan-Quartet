Storia di chi fugge e di chi resta · Tempo di mezzo

Capitolo 118 / 123

Back to Florence — and &#39;zoccola, zoccola ipocrita&#39;

Summary

They return to Florence. They reduce the talk between them to essentials and to a few friendly notes in the children's presence. Pietro moves to the studio to sleep, as he had when Dede never closed an eye; Elena stays in the marital bed. She thinks over what to do. The way Lila's marriage to Stefano had ended is no model — una vicenda d'altri tempi, gestita senza legge, an affair of another era, handled without law. Elena counts on a civilised, legal procedure suited to the times and to her condition. But in fact she still doesn't know how to act, and so does nothing. Mariarosa phones to say the French volumetto — little book — is well on its way, proofs are coming, while her serious, fussy Italian editor sends queries on various passages. For a moment she's pleased; she tries to get interested in her work. But she can't. She feels she has problems far weightier than a verse badly read or a limping passage.

Then one morning the phone rings. Pietro picks up. He says pronto, then pronto again, hangs up. Her heart starts hammering; she gets ready to run to the receiver next time. It doesn't ring again. Hours pass; she tries to distract herself by re-reading her text. Una pessima idea — a bad idea: it strikes her as un cumulo di sciocchezze — a pile of stupidities; the exhaustion makes her doze off at her desk. But the phone rings again. Pietro answers, and this time he urla — shouts — pronto, scaring Dede, and slams down the receiver as if he wanted to break the machine.

It is Nino — she knows, Pietro knows. The Montpellier conference is approaching; he must want to insist again that she come. He will try to pull her back into la materialità delle voglie — the materiality of wants. He will argue that their only possibility is a clandestine relationship to live to exhaustion, tra cattive azioni e piaceri — between bad deeds and pleasures. The way is to lie, to leave together. She will take her first plane; she'll sit beside him as it takes off, come nei film. And why not — after Montpellier they could push on to Nanterre, see Mariarosa's friend, talk about her book, lay out plans, introduce her to Nino. Ah yes — to be in the company of a man she loves and who carries his own potenza — power — that everyone notices. The hostile feeling softens. She is tempted.

Next day Pietro goes to the university. She waits for Nino to call again. He doesn't, and so, con una irragionevole impennata — with an unreasonable lurch — she calls him. She waits several seconds; the only thing in her head is the urgency of hearing his voice. What comes after, she doesn't know — perhaps she'll attack him, perhaps cry, perhaps shout va bene, vengo con te, sarò la tua amante, fine, I'll come with you, I'll be your lover until you tire of me. For the moment all she demands is an answer.

Eleonora answers.

She catches her own voice in time, before it goes off down the line with compromising words to Nino's ghost, and bends it into a festive tone: ciao, sono Elena Greco, stai bene, come sono andate le vacanze, e Albertino? — ciao, it's Elena, are you well, how were the holidays, and the baby?

Eleonora lets her go, in silence, then urlò — shouted:

Sei Elena Greco, eh, la zoccola, la zoccola ipocrita, lascia stare mio marito e non t'azzardare a telefonare mai più, perché so dove abiti e quant'è vero Dio vengo lì e ti spacco la faccia.

— You're Elena Greco, eh? The slut, the hypocrite slut, leave my husband alone and don't you dare phone again, because I know where you live and as God is true I'll come there and break your face.

Then she cuts the line.
