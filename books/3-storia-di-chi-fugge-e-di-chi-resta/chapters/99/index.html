Capitolo 99 / 123

An invention by men — and a key in the lock

Summary

So that is where they have arrived: Elena's sister-in-law considers the marriage a mistake and tells her so plainly. Elena does not know whether to laugh or cry; the verdict feels like the final, unbiased confirmation of her marital unease. And what can she do? She tells herself that maturity means accepting the shape life has taken without too much agitation, drawing a furrow between daily practice and theoretical insight, learning to watch herself, to know herself, awaiting the big shifts. Day by day she settles.

Dede starts first grade slightly early but already reading and writing. Elsa is happy to spend the whole morning alone with Elena in the becalmed apartment. Pietro, for all his greyness as an academic, seems at last close to finishing a second book that promises to be even more important than the first. And Elena is la signora Airota, Elena Airota — a woman dulled by acquiescence who, prodded by her sister-in-law and to fight off depression, has begun studying — almost in secret — l'invenzione della donna da parte degli uomini, the invention of woman by men, mixing ancient and modern. She does it without an objective, only to be able to say to Mariarosa, her mother-in-law, an acquaintance: sto lavorando — "I'm working."

She lets her thread carry her from the first and second creation in Genesis to Defoe-Flanders, Flaubert-Bovary, Tolstoy-Karenina, on to La dernière mode, to Rrose Sélavy, and further still, in una frenesia disvelatrice — in an unveiling frenzy. Slowly a small contentment comes. Everywhere she finds automi di donna fabbricati da maschi — female automata manufactured by males. Nothing properly theirs; the little that rises is immediately material for the male manufacture. When Pietro is at work and Dede is at school and Elsa plays a few steps from her desk and she feels at last a little alive digging into words and between words, she sometimes imagines what her life and Lila's would have been if they had both sat the esame di ammissione alla scuola media — the middle-school entrance exam — and then gone on through liceo and university shoulder to shoulder, a perfect pair pooling intellectual energies, a writing partnership signing together, drawing strength from each other. È un dispiacere la solitudine femminile delle teste — the solitude of female minds is a sorrow — è uno sciupio, a waste, this cutting each other away, without protocols, without tradition. On those days her thoughts come truncated, attractive but defective, in need of a check, of development, but without confidence in themselves. Then the urge returns to phone Lila: listen to what I'm thinking, talk it through with me, give me your opinion, remember what you told me about Alfonso? But the chance is gone — has been gone for decades. She must learn to settle for herself.

Then one day, just as she is leaning into that necessity, she hears the key turn in the lock. Pietro coming home for lunch after his usual stop to pick Dede up from school. She closes her books and notebooks as the child bursts into the study, greeted by Elsa with delight. The girl is hungry; Elena knows she is about to ask mamma, cosa si mangia? Instead, before she has even dropped her satchel, Dede announces: c'è un amico di papà che deve pranzare con noi — there's a friend of papa's who is going to have lunch with us.

The date is exact: 9 March 1976.

Elena gets up in a bad mood, Dede grabs her hand and pulls her into the corridor. Elsa, alerted to a guest, clings prudently to her skirt. Pietro calls cheerfully from the doorway: guarda chi ti ho portato — "look who I've brought you."
