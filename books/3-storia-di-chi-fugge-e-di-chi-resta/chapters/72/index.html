Capitolo 72 / 123

Dario, the fascists, and the murder of don Achille

Summary

One evening Lila calls with bad news: Dario, the boy from the political committee outside the Soccavo gates, has been clubbed to death just outside school, in Piazza del Gesù. Lila sounds genuinely shaken. A black pall, she says, has settled over the rione and the whole city: aggressioni su aggressioni — one attack after another. Behind many of the beatings are Gino's fascists, and behind Gino is Michele Solara — names she pronounces with old disgust and a fresh anger that suggests she is leaving out as much as she is telling. Elena wonders silently whether Lila has kept up her contacts with the students from via dei Tribunali — whether her life really is reducible to Enzo's computers.

Lila rattles off the geography of the squads: they pour out of the missina party section opposite the elementary school, sweep through the Rettifilo, Piazza Municipio, up to the Vomero, attacking compagni (comrades) with clubs and knives. Pasquale has been beaten twice; they've smashed his front teeth. Enzo, one night, exchanged blows with Gino in person right under their own front door.

Then she stops and shifts register. Do you remember, she asks, what the rione felt like when we were small? It's worse. Or no, it's the same. She names her father-in-law, don Achille Carracci, the loan shark, the fascist; and Peluso, the carpenter, the communist; and the war that played out under our eyes. The two of them slide gradually back into those years, each supplying a detail — until Lila accelerates into the visionary register she used as a child and retells the murder of don Achille with the old mix of fact and fantasy. The knife at the neck, the long arc of blood across a copper pot. As she did then, she rules out the carpenter, and now, as an adult, she says it more sharply: la giustizia di allora, come del resto quella di oggi, s'è subito accontentata della pista più ovvia — back then, as now, justice immediately settled for the most obvious lead, the one that pointed to the communist. Then: who said it was Carmen and Pasquale's father at all? Who said it was a man and not a woman?

Elena, as in a childhood game where they once felt perfectly complementary, follows her step by step, layering her voice over Lila's. They reach the answer together, almost in chorus: a guadagnarci era stata la donna col libro rosso — the one who profited was the woman with the red account book, Manuela Solara, Marcello and Michele's mother. She killed don Achille, they say, raising their voices. Then both deflate at once. Ma che stiamo dicendo — what are we saying — basta, siamo ancora due bambine, non cresceremo mai: we're still two little girls, we'll never grow up.
