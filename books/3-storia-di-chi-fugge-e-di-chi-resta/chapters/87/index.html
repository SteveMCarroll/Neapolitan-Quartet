Storia di chi fugge e di chi resta · Tempo di mezzo

Capitolo 87 / 123

The fantasy of Lila as urban guerrilla

Summary

On the beach already, ugly thoughts begin. Lila, Elena tells herself, calmly pushes feelings and emotions back. The harder Elena works to explain herself with the tools she's reading, the more Lila hides; the harder she tries to drag Lila out, the further she retreats into the shadow. Pareva la luna piena quando si acquatta dietro il bosco e i rami le scarabocchiano la faccia (she was like the full moon when it hunkers behind the woods, branches scribbling on its face).

Back in Florence in early September, the bad thoughts intensify instead of fading. There is no telling Pietro. He is furious that they've come back; his book is overdue; the academic year is about to start. One evening, in the middle of a squabble between Dede and gennaro-carracci at the table, he stands up and slams the door so hard the vetro smerigliato (frosted-glass panel) shatters into pieces. Elena calls Lila and tells her bluntly to come take her son back: he has been with her for a month and a half. Non lo puoi tenere fino alla fine del mese? — No. Qui è brutto. — Anche qui.

Enzo drives up overnight and arrives in the morning while Pietro is at work. gennaro-carracci's bag is ready. Elena explains that the tension between the children has become unbearable. Enzo says he understands, thanks her for everything, murmurs as if in apology: Lina lo sai che tipo è. She doesn't reply; Dede is wailing at the departure of gennaro-carracci; and because if she did reply she might say things she'd regret — things precisely about che tipo Lila is.

What sits in Elena's head is a syntax she will not pronounce, fully formed, that frightens and seduces her. She has connected the violent death of Gino to that of Bruno Soccavo (Filippo the watchman survived). And the connecting figure she has reached is Pasquale, maybe Nadia. She thought about calling Carmen, then drew back, in case the phone was tapped. When Enzo came she thought she'd raise it with him; she stayed silent for fear of saying too much — for fear, above all, of naming the figure standing behind Pasquale and Nadia. Lila: Lila who doesn't say things, she does them; Lila imbevuta della cultura del rione, who doesn't believe in police or laws or the state, only in the trincetto (cobbler's knife); Lila who knows the horror of inequality; Lila who at the time of the via dei Tribunali collective found in revolutionary theory and practice a way to use her too-active mind; Lila who turned her old and new rages into political objectives; Lila che muove la gente come personaggi di un racconto; Lila who has been wiring their personal knowledge of misery to armed struggle against fascists, bosses, capital.

Lo ammetto qui per la prima volta in modo chiaro, Elena, much later, writing: in those September days she suspected that not only Pasquale — pushed by his own history toward weapons — and not only Nadia, but Lila herself had shed that blood. While she cooked and looked after the girls she saw the three of them firing at Gino, firing at Filippo, firing at Bruno. Pasquale she imagined with trouble: he is a buon ragazzo un po' fanfarone, capable of beating people but not of killing. Nadia: a well-bred girl who at most stings with words. About Lila, she had no doubt: lei avrebbe saputo escogitare il piano più efficace, lei avrebbe ridotto al minimo i rischi, lei avrebbe tenuto sotto controllo la paura, lei era in grado di dare alle intenzioni omicide un'astratta purezza, lei sapeva come levare sostanza umana ai corpi e al sangue, lei non avrebbe avuto scrupoli e non avrebbe avuto rimorsi, lei avrebbe ucciso e si sarebbe sentita nel giusto.

Whole little scenes play out: the car slowing in front of the pharmacy and the shots into Gino's white-coated body; the dusty road to Soccavo with garbage in heaps; Pasquale firing through the gatehouse; Lila, who knows the place, crossing the courtyard, going up the stairs, walking into Bruno's office and, just as he says cheerfully ciao, come mai da queste parti, blowing three holes in his chest and one in his face.

Antifascismo militante, nuova resistenza, giustizia proletaria — all the slogans Elena imagines Lila would be exactly the right person to give weight to. Maybe these actions are entrance tickets into the Brigate Rosse, Prima Linea, the Nuclei Armati Proletari. Lila would vanish from the rione like Pasquale already has. Maybe that's why she pretended to leave gennaro-carracci for "a month": really to hand him over for good. Either she'll never be seen again, or she'll be arrested like Curcio and Franceschini, the BR leaders, or she'll outrun the police forever, fantasiosa e temeraria com'era. And when the cosa grande is done she will reappear triumphant, revolutionary leader, and tell Elena: tu volevi scrivere romanzi, io il romanzo l'ho fatto con le persone vere, col sangue vero, nella realtà.

At night the fantasy feels like history happening; she fears for Lila, sees her hunted, wounded, and pities her — and envies her. The childhood conviction that Lila was destined for imprese straordinarie swells up. She regrets having fled Naples, having detached herself; she wants to be back beside her. And she rages that Lila set off on that road without consulting her — come se non mi avesse ritenuta all'altezza (as if she had not considered me up to it). She knows about capital, exploitation, class struggle, the inevitability of revolution; she could be useful, could take part. She lies awake unhappy, her future shrunken to riti domestici in cucina, nel letto coniugale.

By day she is more lucid: horror takes over. She pictures a capricious Lila stirring up hatreds artfully and getting pulled deeper into ferocious actions. Brave, yes, con la determinazione cristallina, con la crudeltà generosa of those who believe they're right. But to what end? Civil war? Turning the rione, Naples, Italy into un campo di battaglia, un Vietnam in mezzo al Mediterraneo? Throwing them all into endless slaughter between East and West? Cities destroyed, the dead in the streets, factional savagery even within the revolutionary camp — perhaps even la guerra nucleare.

She squeezes her eyes shut: the girls, the future. She clings to phrases — il soggetto imprevisto, la logica distruttiva del patriarca, il valore femminile della sopravvivenza, la pietà. I have to talk to Lila, she tells herself. She has to tell me everything, what she's doing, what she's planning, so I can decide whether to be her accomplice or not.

But she never calls, and Lila doesn't call her. Elena begins to believe the long thread of voice that has been their only contact for years has hurt them. They kept up the bond per sottrazione (by subtraction), and have become for each other abstract entities: Elena can imagine Lila as either an expert capocentro or a steely urban guerrilla; Lila, in all likelihood, can see Elena either as the cliché of the successful intellectual or as a cultured lady, all children, books, dotte conversations with her academic husband. They both need new weight, real bodies, and have drifted too far to give it to each other.
