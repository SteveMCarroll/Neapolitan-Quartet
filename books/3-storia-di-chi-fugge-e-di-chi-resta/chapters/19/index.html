Storia di chi fugge e di chi resta · Tempo di mezzo

Capitolo 19 / 123

mirko-sarratore&#39;s father has a name

Summary

She can't sleep. Tense nerves, contradictory thoughts — and now mirko-sarratore starts crying again and won't stop. She remembers the strong feeling of holding him; she gets up, follows the noise to a door rimmed with light. Silvia answers roughly. The room is nicer than her own — old wardrobe, dresser, a double bed where the girl sits cross‑legged in a pink baby‑doll with a vicious face. Arms hanging loose, the backs of both hands on the sheet, she holds mirko-sarratore on her bare thighs come un'offerta votiva — like a votive offering — naked, purple, mouth a black hole, eyes pinched shut, limbs flailing. Silvia softens, says she's a useless mother, doesn't know what to do; if he isn't eating, this is what he does; forse sta male, mi morirà qui sul letto — maybe he's sick, he'll die on me right here. She bursts into tears.

Both their crying disarms Elena. She asks if she can take him. Silvia nods through sobs. The baby goes back to her chest and the same flow of warmth and sound and smell, as if his vitality were hurrying back to her after a separation. She paces the room murmuring a litania asintattica — a syntax‑less litany invented on the spot — a long senseless declaration of love. mirko-sarratore miraculously settles. She lays him down beside his mother, doesn't want to part from him, doesn't want to go back to her own room — part of her is sure Juan is in there and would rather stay where she is.

Silvia thanks her without gratitude, then lists Elena's virtues in a chilly tone — intelligent, knowing, respected, a real mother in waiting, beati i figli che avrai — lucky the children you'll have. Elena demurs, makes to leave. Silvia panics and begs her to stay: ti sente, fallo per lui, dormirà tranquillo — he can feel you, do it for him, he'll sleep. Elena agrees at once. They lie down with the baby between them, kill the light, and start to talk.

In the dark Silvia is less hostile. She tells the story of the pregnancy: the disgust of discovering it, the hiding it from the man she loved and from herself, the conviction it would pass come una malattia che deve fare il suo corso — like an illness that has to run its course. The body reacted; she had to tell her parents, well‑off Monza professionals; there was a scene; she left home. Instead of admitting she'd let the months slide hoping for a miracle — not having considered abortion only out of physical fear — she switched stories and started claiming she wanted the baby out of love for the man. He'd said: if you want him, for love of you I want him too. Silvia keeps coming back to this with pain: both meant it at the time. But before the pregnancy even ended, the love drained out of both of them. Nothing left but rancour. She would have sunk without Mariarosa, whom she praises with great warmth.

Elena says all the Airotas are admirable, that she's engaged to Pietro, that they'll marry in autumn. Silvia bursts out: il matrimonio mi fa orrore — marriage horrifies me, family too, it's all old stuff. Then, abruptly melancholy:

— Anche il padre di mirko-sarratore lavora all'università. mirko-sarratore's father works at the university too. — Yes? — It all started because I sat in on his course. So sure of himself, so well prepared, so intelligent, bellissimo. He had every quality. Even before the protests he'd say: rieducate i vostri professori, non fatevi trattare come bestie — re‑educate your professors, don't let yourselves be treated like animals. — And does he look after the baby?

Silvia laughs into the dark, sharply: a man, except for the mad moments when he loves you and enters you, stays outside. So afterwards, when you no longer love him, even the thought that once you wanted him annoys you. She liked me, I liked her, end. Many times a day she likes someone. Solo il bambino resta — only the baby stays, he's a part of you. The father was a stranger and becomes one again. Even the name doesn't sound the same. Nino, I used to say, repeating it the moment I woke up — a magic word. Now it's just a sound that saddens me.

Elena is silent for a beat, then whispers: the father of mirko-sarratore is called Nino? Yes — everyone knows him, he's well known at the university. Nino, and then? Nino Sarratore.
