Capitolo 62 / 123

Wedding, reception, first night

Summary

Of the wedding itself, Elena remembers almost nothing — the photos have frozen the day into a few images: Pietro distracted, Elena looking irritated, her mother out of focus but unmistakably displeased. What she does remember is the fight that came first. A few days before the rite she told Pietro she wanted to go on the pill: no children yet, she needed to try another book first. To her surprise he resisted — legality, health, a long argument about love and procreation, and finally the line that if you really have something to write, you'll write it even with a baby. They reached the wedding without making up, him silent, her cold.

The other surprise was the reception. They had agreed: civil rite, family, home, no party. Adele blew that up. She steered them to a friend's nobilissima dimora fiorentina ("very noble Florentine house") "for a toast," and they walked into a full reception with the Airota clan and a long list of famous guests. Pietro went dark and parked himself in a corner with Elena's relatives. Elena, after a disoriented moment, started to enjoy it: politicians, intellectuals, even a well-known poet and novelist congratulating her on her novel and her l'Unità pieces. Her father-in-law praised an article in Mondo Operaio on the rigging of Italian democracy through schools, courts, and the press — and named the author: Giovanni Sarratore. Nino. Elena couldn't stop herself: she said she knew him, called Adele over, talked him up. Nino partecipò al mio matrimonio anche se non era presente — "Nino took part in my wedding even though he wasn't there."

The newlyweds got home and started to make love. Pietro went on for what felt like forever, methodical, hard, sweating. Elena's initial pleasure leaked away into pain and the slow death of desire. She stroked him, whispered, hoped he would stop. When he finally collapsed with a roar she was relieved, sore, unsatisfied. He got up almost immediately, went to the bathroom, then to his study. She woke an hour later, found him at his desk: Lavoro. — "I'm working." Sono convinta che restai incinta quella notte. — "I'm convinced I got pregnant that night."
