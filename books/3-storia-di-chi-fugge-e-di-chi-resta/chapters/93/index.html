Storia di chi fugge e di chi resta · Tempo di mezzo

Capitolo 93 / 123

Antonio&#39;s gift — the German edition of Elena&#39;s novel

Summary

Elisa won't let Elena's prestige sink further. She turns to her radiant and announces she has something to give her — and then, in her usual hummingbird way, jumps subjects: sei mai andata in aereo? Have you ever flown? No. Of those at the table only Pietro has, and he speaks of it as if it were nothing. For Elisa it was a marvel: Germany with Marcello, gusts and shudders, a jet of icy air drilling at her head, and then the porthole opening on cloud-white below and pure blue above. Oltre le nuvole c'è sempre bel tempo — beyond the clouds the weather is always fine.

"Guess who we ran into in Düsseldorf?" Elena, sour, gives up. Antonio. He sends his warmest greetings, he sent a gift. Elisa runs off to fetch it. Pietro asks who Antonio is. A dipendente — an employee — Marcello says smoothly. "A boyfriend of your wife," Michele corrects, laughing: women these days brag about their boyfriends peggio degli uomini — worse than men. How many girlfriends has Pietro had? Pietro, serious: none, I have only ever loved my wife. Bugiardo — liar — Michele gets up, goes behind Pietro and whispers a count in his ear. Incredibile, Pietro answers, cautiously ironic. They laugh together.

Elisa hands her a package wrapped in brown paper. The whole room is watching — Lila most of all, sidelong, intent, as if waiting for a snake to spring out. Elena opens it and they look almost disappointed: no nostalgic trinket, no sentimental relic, just a book. Then they see Elena change colour, see her stare at the cover with a joy she can't suppress. It is not just any book. It is her book — the German translation of her novel, six years after its Italian publication. For the first time in her life she is watching her own words dance in a foreign language. Antonio — Melina's son, the Solaras' semi-literate strong-arm, her adolescent boyfriend — has tracked her down with this.

Non ne sapevi niente? Elisa asks proudly. No. Elisa announces to the whole table: è il romanzo che ha scritto Lenuccia, ma con le parole tedesche ("it's the novel Lenuccia wrote, but with the German words"). Elena's mother turns red with vindication: Avete visto quant'è famosa? "See how famous she is?"

Gigliola grabs the book, flicks through, mutters that the only thing she can make out is the name Elena Greco. Lila reaches out an imperious hand — give it to me. Elena recognises the old urgency on her face, the childhood pull toward any unknown object that contains something she does not yet possess, and is moved by it. But Gigliola yanks the book back angrily: aspetta, adesso ce l'ho io — wait, it's mine now. Cos'è, sai pure il tedesco? Then, in the loaded silence, she turns the pages slowly, pretending to read a few lines here and there, and finally hands it back with a wine-thickened speech: "Brava, Lenù — complimenti per tutto, il libro, il marito, le bambine. The Germans even know you. What you've got you earned, you earned it senza stronzeggiare coi mariti delle altre — without playing the slut with other women's husbands. Thanks, now I really need to go. Buonanotte."

She heaves herself up — heavier with the wine — calls her sons. The older one says something greve — coarse — in dialect; she slaps him and drags him toward the door. Michele shakes his head with a smile: ho passato un guaio con questa stronza — "I drew the short straw with this one, she always has to ruin my day." Then, very calmly: wait, Gigliò, where are you running, we have to eat your father's dolce first. The boys break free, sensing the order, and dart back to the table. Gigliola heads on toward the hall, snapping that she'll go alone, she doesn't feel well. Then Michele, with a flat violent shout: mettiti a sedere immediatamente. Sit down right now. She freezes as if her legs have been cut from under her. Elisa jumps up, murmurs vieni, accompagnami a prendere la torta, and pulls her toward the kitchen by the arm. Elena reassures Dede with a look — the child has been frightened by Michele's voice — then offers the book to Lila: lo vuoi vedere? Lila waves it off with a small grimace of indifference.
