Capitolo 91 / 123

The Solara family arrives for dinner

Summary

Elena's parents arrive at Elisa's apartment with Dede, Elsa and Pietro. Elisa fusses over the children with showy affection — Dede, dolcetta ("little sweet one"), come kiss your aunt; Elsa, you've grown so plump, come, we share a name. Elena's mother slips straight into the kitchen without meeting Elena's eyes. Pietro tries to draw Elena aside to confess something serious, but the apologetic look on his face only sharpens her unease, and before he can speak Elena's father drags him in front of the television and turns the volume up.

Gigliola comes in next with her two raging little boys. She is fresh from the parrucchiere — the hairdresser — heavy with gold at her ears, neck and wrists, stuffed into a low-cut bright-green dress, her makeup already smearing. She greets Elena with open sarcasm: Eccoci qua, siamo venuti apposta per omaggiare voi professori — "Here we are, we came specially to pay tribute to the two professors." Pietro springs up; his eyes catch involuntarily on her cleavage, and Gigliola notes the look with satisfaction. Comodo, comodo — "sit, sit, don't trouble yourself" — no one around here ever rises for a lady.

Gigliola makes plain she has no wish to be there: if her husband hadn't ordered her, io starei assai meglio a casa mia — I'd be much happier at home. Then the doorbell. Elisa floats to answer, and comes back leading her future mother-in-law: Manuela Solara, dressed for a feast, a fake flower in her reddish-dyed hair, eyes set in deep hollows, almost skin and bone. Behind her stands Michele, well shaved and quietly forceful. And behind him a omone — a giant of a man, so big Elena barely recognises him: long heavy legs, enormous chest, a broad forehead, hair combed back, a shining anthracite beard. It is Marcello, and Elisa lifts her lips to his as to a god owed both respect and gratitude.

It is Manuela's sessant'anni — sixtieth birthday — Elisa whispers proudly. The whole room treats the matriarch's presence as a great honour. Marcello immediately greets Pietro as if they already knew each other — tutto a posto, professó ("all set, professor") — and Pietro answers with a sheepish smile and a tiny headshake at Elena: I did what I could. Then the doorbell again. Michele goes out and returns amused, trailed by an old man dragging luggage — Elena's and Pietro's luggage, from the hotel. Elena protests in a furious whisper but Elisa won't hear of it; Marcello insists he asked Pietro's permission first. Elena pleads with Pietro across the room, and as her voice climbs she hears her own Neapolitan returning, the cadence of the rione settling back over her tongue word by word — the neighbourhood imposing its language and its way of acting on her even in protest.

The bell rings a third time. Gennaro bursts in; Dede stops crying instantly; the two children stare at each other, astonished by the reunion. Then Enzo — l'unico biondo tra tanti bruni ("the only blond in a room of dark heads") — and yet sombre. And finally, into all of it, walks Lila.
