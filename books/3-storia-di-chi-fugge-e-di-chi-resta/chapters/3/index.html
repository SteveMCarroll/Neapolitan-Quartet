Capitolo 3 / 123

Greeting Nino — autograph queue

Summary

Elena leaves her seat dazed. Nino comes over smiling, walking deliberately; his hand is hot, hers ice‑cold. She introduces him to the elderly critic who had praised her novel, a kind man called Tarratano, telling him Nino is an old schoolmate from Naples. The professor, despite having been jabbed by Nino's intervention, is gracious. Nino explains he has been in Milan for years now, works on economic geography, and belongs (he says with a smile) to the most miserable rung of the academic ladder — the assistenti, "junior assistants." Elena registers with relief that he isn't wearing a wedding ring.

Some of Adele's friends drift over to have their books signed — Elena's first time. She stalls: she doesn't want to lose sight of Nino for a second, but she also wants to undo the impression of being a fumbling girl. She lingers over each dedication in her best handwriting, the kind la maestra Oliviero had drilled into her, while keeping Nino in her peripheral vision the whole time. She is terrified he'll leave.

He doesn't. He stays, talking now with Adele in a tone that is both deferential and easy. Watching him, Elena consciously chooses to unbookmark the old Nino — the lost student in Ischia, the secret lover of her married friend, the boy hiding in the back room of the Solaras' Piazza dei Martiri shop, the father of a child he has never seen. That whole episode, she tells herself, was a parenthesis; he has put himself back together. She decides: I'll tell Lila I saw him, that he's well. Then she changes her mind. No. I won't tell her.

Once the queue is gone, Adele takes Elena's hand, praises her warmly, and asks Nino and Tarratano to confirm she did well. Nino fixes her seriously and tells the company what an extraordinary student she had been — clever, well‑read, brave, beautiful — and tells the funny story of her teenage clash with the religion teacher. Adele then announces dinner at a nearby restaurant and, when Elena protests that she'd rather take a quick walk with Nino, says with an ironic look that of course her friend is invited too — and adds, mysteriously: «ho in serbo per te una bella sorpresa» — "I've got a nice surprise in store for you." Nino glances at his watch and accepts.
