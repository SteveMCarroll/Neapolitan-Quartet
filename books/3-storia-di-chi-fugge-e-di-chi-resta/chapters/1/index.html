Capitolo 1 / 123

Gigliola in the flowerbed — the frame

Summary

The whole book opens with a brief frame set in 2005, decades after the events that will fill the rest of it. An aging Elena is walking through the old rione one winter morning with Lila for what will be the last time. Lila is rail‑thin, white‑haired, and short‑tempered; the two old friends can no longer find their way back to easy intimacy.

A young man rushes past with news: a woman's body has been found in a flowerbed beside the church. Lila recognises her at once — Elena does not. It is Gigliola Spagnuolo, their childhood friend and Michele Solara's ex‑wife. She lies on her side in a dark‑green raincoat, one shoe kicked a metre away, hair dyed a thin red. Elena bursts into tears; Lila looks at her with irritation. They sit on a bench, then go back to Lila's small apartment — the old place where her parents lived, where she now lives with her grown son.

Walking back through the rione, Elena reflects on how everything she once tried to escape has only got worse: a Naples that floods every winter, a country and a planet on the same rotting trajectory. "Non è il rione a essere malato" — it isn't the rione that's sick — she tells Lila now: it's the whole globe, the whole universe. Lila, who hates this kind of late‑life pessimism, shows her teeth in a sour grin and asks the question she has been circling for years: «Vuoi scrivere di noi? Vuoi scrivere di me?» — "Are you going to write about us? About me?" Elena denies it. Lila warns her flatly: «Ti vengo a frugare nel computer, ti leggo i file, te li cancello» — "I'll come and dig through your computer and delete your files." Elena laughs that she knows how to protect herself. Lila answers with the three words that will hover over the whole book: «Da me no» — "Not from me."
