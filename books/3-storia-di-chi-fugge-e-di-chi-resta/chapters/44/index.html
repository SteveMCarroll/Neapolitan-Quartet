Storia di chi fugge e di chi resta · Tempo di mezzo

Capitolo 44 / 123

Summary

Michele Solara reappears in Lila's life with the force of a childhood ghost in a dark corner. He stands up, arms wide, looks almost moved. Lina, che piacere, come sono contento. He goes to embrace her; she stops him with an instinctive jerk of disgust. He recovers by gesturing at her like a curio: look at that — la signora Carracci hidden in the salamis.

Lila tries to turn around — torno più tardi. Bruno: sit down. — I prefer to stand. — Sit, you'll get tired. — Michele, complicit: she's made that way, give up, non obbedisce mai. His voice has gotten weightier, every word with its full ending, like he's been doing diction exercises. To save her energy — or just to contradict him — she sits.

What follows is a long, casual monologue. Michele speaks to her as though she were still his employee at the piazza dei Martiri shop — new shelves, new lighting, he had the courtyard bathroom door bricked up again. Me ne fotto del tuo negozio, she says. Vuoi dire il nostro, he corrects — ours, we invented it together. Insieme a te non ho mai inventato niente, she says.

He smiles, shakes his head: whoever puts up the money invents just as much as whoever works with hands and head. I soldi inventano i panorami, le situazioni, la vita delle persone — money invents landscapes, situations, people's lives. Tu non sai quanta gente posso fare felice o rovinare solo firmando uno scèk — you have no idea how many people I can make happy or ruin just by signing a check.

Then a leisurely catalog of news, presented as friendly small-talk:

  • Alfonso is doing well at piazza dei Martiri, earning enough to start a family — but he doesn't want to marry, prefers to keep poor Marisa as an eternal fiancée while he does as he pleases. So Michele, as his employer, has encouraged the wedding, will pay for it; June.
  • Michele himself is marrying Gigliola in June, il grande amore della mia vita. Sorry I can't invite you, he says, non voglio mettere in imbarazzo tuo marito.
  • Stefano is taking on water. His father's money kept him afloat for a while but commerce is un mare agitato now, new shops everywhere.
  • Marcello has expanded don Carlo's old basement warehouse into a one-stop shop selling everything from soap to mortadella to pastries. He called it Tutto per tutti — Everything for Everyone. And guess who he hired to run it? Tuo fratello. Rino — your brother. Lila: you've reduced Rino to your shop assistant? Well, Michele shrugs, you dropped him, and the kid has your father, your mother, a child, and Pinuccia pregnant again on his shoulders. He came to Marcello for help. Non ti fa piacere? Doesn't that make you happy?

No, Lila says, ice-cold: non mi piace niente di quello che fate. Nothing you people do makes me happy.

Michele turns to Bruno: see, like I told you — il suo problema è che ha un cattivo carattere, the problem is she has a bad character. Has she been bad to you too? A little. He launches into a story for Bruno: do you know that when she was a child she put a trincetto, a leather knife, to my brother's throat? She was half his size and dead serious. Really? Yes. Questa ha coraggio, è determinata.

The room sways for Lila. Live and dead bodies swelling. She watches Michele crush his cigarette stub with too much force — even with the calm tone, he's working off some discomfort. Once, she thinks, he asked me to be his lover. But that isn't really what he wants. There's something more, something he can't explain to himself either. It's become a superstizione. He thinks I have a power he needs. He'd take it if he could, but he can't take it by force — and that's eating him.

She decides to leave. Gets up, ready to set the list on Bruno's desk and walk out. But Michele stops her with a voice gone suddenly caressing. He launches into a pitch — to Bruno — about her. She doesn't just have a bad character, she has moltissime qualità. Tu credi di avere assunto un'operaia? Non è vero. You think you hired a worker? You didn't. This woman is far more. Let her run loose and she'll turn your shit into gold. Ha una testa che normalmente non solo non ce l'ha nessuna femmina, ma non ce l'abbiamo nemmeno noi maschi. A head no woman has, and that we men don't have either. She designed me shoes I'm still selling all over Naples. She redid my piazza dei Martiri shop into a salon for the gentlemen of via Chiaia, Posillipo, the Vomero. But she has la capa pazza, the mad head — she comes and goes, fixes, breaks. Did I fire her? No — one day, just like that, she didn't show up. Un'anguilla — an eel; you grab her, she slips out.

Her problem, he says, warming to a sermon: non ha trovato ancora l'uomo vero — she hasn't yet found a real man. A real man puts a woman in order. Can't cook? She'll learn. House dirty? She'll clean it. Alla donna un uomo vero può far fare tutto. I once knew a woman who couldn't whistle. Siamo stati insieme due ore soltanto – ore di fuoco — and afterward I said: now whistle. And she whistled. Se non la sai educare, la femmina, bene. Se non la sai educare, lasciala perdere, che ti fai male.

But even as he delivers his commandment he knows he himself never mastered Lila. The face changes. He turns on her with a sudden urge to humiliate: con questa però è difficile, non te la levi tanto facilmente dal cazzo. And look at her — small eyes, small tits, small ass, reduced to a broomstick. Con una così che ci puoi fare, non ti si rizza neanche. But it takes a second: one look and you want to fuck her.

A violent shock in Lila's head — as if her heart, instead of hammering her throat, had exploded inside her skull. She shouts back something just as filthy, grabs the bronze ashtray off the desk, scattering cigarette ends and ash, and tries to hit him. The gesture is slow, strengthless. Bruno's protest — Lina, per favore, che fai — drifts through her listlessly. Michele blocks her easily and takes the ashtray.

Then the speech that resets the room: Tu pensi che dipendi dal dottor Soccavo? You think you depend on Soccavo? Tu pensi che io qui non sono nessuno? Wrong. Il dottor Soccavo da un po' di tempo sta sul libro rosso di mia madre, che è un libro molto più importante del libretto di Mao — Soccavo has for some time been in my mother's red book, which is a much more important book than Mao's. So you don't depend on him. You depend on me. Sempre e soltanto. Until now I let you do as you pleased, I wanted to see where you and that asshole you're sleeping with were going. Ma da adesso ricordati che ti tengo gli occhi addosso e se mi servi devi correre, è chiaro?

Only now does Bruno spring up: Lasciala, Michè, adesso stai esagerando. Michele lets her wrist go, turns back to formal Italian: you're right, sorry. Ma la signora Carracci ha questa capacità: in un modo o nell'altro ti costringe sempre a esagerare.

Lila masters the rage, brushes ash off her clothes, unfolds the list of demands, sets it before Bruno, and on her way to the door turns back to Solara: So fischiare da quando avevo cinque anni. I've known how to whistle since I was five.
