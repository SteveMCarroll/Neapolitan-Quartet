Capitolo 59 / 123

Galiani — and Lila&#39;s pages

Summary

Elena hopes the professor didn't hear her shouting. She waits for Nadia to leap off Pasquale's lap and run to the sofa — she wants to see them humiliated by needing to feign distance. Lila watches the two of them ironically. But Nadia doesn't move; if anything she slips an arm around Pasquale's neck as if afraid of falling. «La prossima volta avvisami, se hai visite» — next time warn me if you've got guests — she tells her mother, who has just appeared in the doorway. The Galiani doesn't answer. To Elena and Lila she says coldly: forgive me, I'm late, let's go to my study. As they follow she mutters, exasperated: quello che mi dà veramente fastidio è la cafonaggine — what really gets to me is the boorishness. Pasquale, behind them, mumbles in a depressed tone, dài, andiamo di là.

The study is airy: an old desk, a great many books, austere upholstered chairs. The Galiani tries to be courteous and is plainly fighting her bad mood. Elena starts in too quickly: she apologises for the long silence, talks in a slightly winded way about the strain of her studies, the book, the thousand things that have swept over her, her engagement, the imminent wedding.

«Ti sposi in chiesa o solo col rito civile?» — church or only civil? Only civil. Brava. Then to Lila: Lei s'è sposata in chiesa? Yes. È credente? No. Allora perché s'è sposata in chiesa? Si faceva così — it was what was done. Non bisognerebbe fare le cose solo perché si fanno. Ne facciamo tante.

Then: Andrà al matrimonio di Elena? — Will you be going to Elena's wedding? «Non mi ha invitata.» Elena jumps: that's not true. Lila laughs: «È vero, si vergogna di me» — it's true, she's ashamed of me. The tone is mocking but Elena is wounded all the same. What has come over Lila? First taking Pasquale and Nadia's side; now this unpleasant line in front of the professor?

To steady herself Elena takes the copy of her book out of her bag and holds it out — le volevo dare questo. The Galiani looks at it for a moment without seeing, as if following some other thought, then thanks her and says she has it already and gives it back. Tuo marito che fa? Latin chair at Florence. Parecchio più vecchio di te? Twenty-seven. Così giovane, una cattedra? He's good. Come si chiama? Pietro Airota. The Galiani fixes her the way she used to in class when a student gave an incomplete answer. Parente di Guido Airota? È suo figlio. A pointedly malicious smile. Bel matrimonio. Ci vogliamo bene. — We love each other.

Have you started another book? Trying. I see you write for l'Unità. Just a few pieces. Io non ci scrivo più, è un giornale di burocrati — I don't write for them any more; it's a paper of bureaucrats. Then she turns again, deliberately, to Lila. «È notevole ciò che ha fatto in fabbrica» — what you did in the factory is notable. Lila grimaces, irritated: non ho fatto niente. Questo non è vero.

The professor stands up, rummages on the desk, pulls out a sheaf of pages, and hands them across as if exhibiting incontrovertible proof: «Nadia ha lasciato questo suo testo in giro per casa e io mi sono permessa di leggerlo. È un lavoro coraggioso, nuovo, molto ben scritto. Desideravo rivederla per poterglielo dire.» — Nadia left this text of yours lying around and I took the liberty of reading it. It's brave, new, very well written. I wanted to see you again to tell you so.

The pages are Lila's — the pages from which Elena drew her first piece for l'Unità. Elena's article, the article that Adele and Mariarosa and Franco rang to praise, was built on Lila's writing.
