Capitolo 123 / 123

Onto the plane — the floor that trembles

Summary

She puts the receiver down as if it were scalding. È gelosa — she is jealous, she tells herself, she is envious, she hates me. Yes — that was the truth. A long procession of seconds passes; Manuela Solara never returns to her mind, the death-marked body fades. Instead her anxious question is: why isn't Nino calling? Is it possible that now — now that she has told Lila — he will pull back and leave her ridiculous? For a moment she sees herself exposed to Lila in all her possible smallness, a person who has ruined herself for nothing. Then the phone rings again. For two or three long rings she sits and stares at the apparatus. When she picks up the receiver, the words ready on her tongue are for Lila: non ti occupare mai più di me, su Nino non hai nessun diritto, lasciami sbagliare come mi pare — don't ever concern yourself with me again; you have no rights over Nino; let me make my mistakes as I please.

But it isn't Lila. It's Nino. She floods him with broken sentences, happy to hear him. She tells him how things stand with Pietro and the girls; tells him it is impossible to reach any agreement with calm and reason; tells him her case is packed and she can't wait to hold him. He tells her about furious fights with his wife; the last hours have been unbearable. He murmurs: anche se sono molto spaventato, non riesco a pensare la mia vita senza di te — though I'm very frightened, I can't conceive of my life without you.

The next day, while Pietro is at the university, she asks the neighbour to keep Dede and Elsa for a few hours. She leaves the prepared letters on the kitchen table and walks out. She thinks: è in atto qualcosa di grande che dissolverà tutto il vecchio modo di vivere e io sono parte di questa dissoluzione — something great is happening that will dissolve the whole old way of living, and I am part of this dissolution. She joins Nino in Rome; they meet in a hotel a few steps from the station. As she holds him she tells herself: non mi abituerò mai a questo corpo nervoso, è una continua sorpresa — I will never get used to this nervy body, it is a continual surprise; long bones, an exciting skin-smell, a mass, a strength, a mobility utterly foreign to what Pietro is and to the habits between them.

The next morning, for the first time in her life, she boards an aeroplane. She doesn't know how to fasten the seat belt; Nino helps her. How thrilling it is to squeeze his hand hard while il rombo dei motori saliva, saliva, saliva — the roar of the engines rose, rose, rose — and the plane began its run. How moving to leave the ground with a jolt and see the houses turn into parallelepipedi — small rectangular blocks — and the streets into thin strips, and the countryside reduce to a green smear, and the sea tilt like a compact sheet, and the clouds collapse downward in una frana di rocce soffici — a landslide of soft rock — and her anguish, her pain, even her happiness become part of a single, very luminous movement. It seems to her that flying subjects everything to a process of simplification. She sighs and tries to let go. Every so often she asks Nino: sei contento? — are you happy? — and he nods, kisses her. At moments she has the impression that the floor under her feet — l'unica superficie su cui si potesse contare — the only surface you could count on — is trembling.

End of book 3, Storia di chi fugge e di chi resta.
