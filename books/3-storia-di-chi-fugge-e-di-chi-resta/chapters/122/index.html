Storia di chi fugge e di chi resta · Tempo di mezzo

Capitolo 122 / 123

Manuela Solara is dead — and Lila erupts

Summary

Now, just as the day of departure approaches, Nino stops calling. She tries him herself, hoping Eleonora won't answer. The maid answers; for a moment she feels relieved and asks for il professor Sarratore. The reply is sharp and hostile: le passo la signora — I'll put the lady on. She hangs up, waits. She hopes the call will become the trigger of a row between husband and wife and that Nino will learn she was looking for him. Ten minutes later the phone rings. She rushes. She is sure it is him.

Instead it is Lila.

They haven't talked in a long time, and she doesn't feel like talking now. Lila's voice irritates her. In this phase even just her name, as it crosses her mind come una biscia — like a small snake — confuses her, drains her strength. And this is not the moment for chit-chat: if Nino phones, he'll find the line busy, and communications are already very difficult.

Ti posso richiamare? she asks — Can I call you back? Hai da fare? — Are you busy? Un po' — A bit.

Lila ignores the request. As always, it seems to her she can come in and out of Elena's life without any concern, as if they were still one single thing and there were no need to ask come stai, come va, disturbo? — how are you, how's it going, am I bothering you? In a tired voice she says she has just had bad news: la madre dei Solara era stata ammazzata — the Solaras' mother has been killed.

She speaks slowly, as if weighing each word, and Elena listens without interrupting. Lila's words drag along behind them, as in a procession, l'usuraia vestita a festa — the moneylender dressed up for the feast — who had sat at the bride and groom's table at Lila and Stefano's wedding; la donna spiritata — the possessed woman — who had opened the door when Elena was looking for Michele; l'ombra di femmina della nostra infanzia — the woman-shadow of their childhood — who had stabbed don Achille; the elderly lady with a fake flower in her hair fanning herself with a blue fan and saying, distractedly: io sento caldo, voi pure? — I feel hot, do you? But Elena feels nothing, not even when Lila relays the rumours, listing them in her efficient way. They had killed Manuela by sgozzandola con un coltello — cutting her throat with a knife; or they had shot her five times, four in the chest, one in the throat; or they had massacred her with punches and kicks, dragging her round the whole flat; or gli uccisori — the killers, she called them — had not even entered the house: they had shot her as soon as she opened the door, Manuela had fallen face-down on the landing and her husband, who was watching television, hadn't even noticed. Quello che è certo — what is certain, says Lila, is that the Solaras have gone mad, they are competing with the police to find the culprits, have called in people from Naples and beyond, all their activities have stopped, even she isn't working today; the place is terrifying, you can't even breathe.

Elena recognises the old gift: Lila knows how to give weight and thickness to what happens to her and around her — the throat-slit moneylender, her shaken sons, their thugs ready to spill more blood, and her own person vigile in mezzo al mareggiare degli eventi — vigilant in the swell of events.

Then she comes to the real reason for the call:

Domani ti mando Gennaro. Lo so che sto abusando, hai le tue figlie, le cose tue, ma io qua, adesso, non lo posso e non lo voglio tenere. Perderà un po' di scuola, pazienza. Lui ti è affezionato, da te sta bene, sei l'unica persona di cui mi fido.

— Tomorrow I'll send you Gennaro. I know I'm taking advantage — you have your own daughters, your own things, but here, right now, I can't and won't keep him. He'll miss a bit of school, never mind. He's fond of you, he's well with you, you're the only person I trust.

Elena thinks for a few seconds about that last line: sei l'unica persona di cui mi fido. She wants to smile. Lila doesn't yet know that she has become inaffidabile — untrustworthy. Faced with this request that takes for granted the immobility of her existence inside the most serene of reasonablenesses — that seems to attribute to her the life of una bacca rossa sopra il ramo-foglia del pungitopo (a red berry on a holly leaf-twig) — she does not hesitate. She says:

Sto per partire, lascio mio marito — I'm about to leave; I'm leaving my husband.

Non ho capito — I don't understand.

Il mio matrimonio è finito, Lila. Ho rivisto Nino e abbiamo scoperto che ci siamo sempre amati, fin da ragazzini, senza accorgercene. Perciò me ne vado, comincio una vita nuova.

— My marriage is over, Lila. I saw Nino again and we discovered we have always loved each other, since we were kids, without realising it. So I'm leaving, I'm starting a new life.

A long silence, then:

Stai scherzando? — Are you joking?

No.

It must seem impossible to Lila that she is putting disorder into her house, into her well-ordered head; and now she presses on, clutching mechanically at her husband: Pietro è un uomo straordinario, buono, intelligentissimo, sei pazza a lasciarlo, pensa al male che fai alle tue figlie — Pietro is an extraordinary man, good, very intelligent; you're crazy to leave him; think of the harm you'll do to your daughters. She speaks and does not mention Nino, as if his name had stopped in the padiglione dell'orecchio (the cup of the ear) without reaching her brain. Elena has to be the one to pronounce it again, to say: no, Lila, I can't live with Pietro any more because I can't do without Nino; whatever happens, I'll go with him; and other such phrases, which she trots out as if they were a medal.

Then Lila starts to shout:

Tu butti via tutto quello che sei per Nino? Tu rovini la tua famiglia per quello lì? Sai che cosa ti succederà? Ti userà, ti succhierà il sangue, ti toglierà la voglia di vivere e ti abbandonerà. Perché hai studiato tanto? A che cazzo è servito immaginarmi che ti saresti goduta una vita bellissima anche per me? Ho sbagliato, sei una cretina.

— You're throwing away everything you are for Nino? You're ruining your family for that one? Do you know what will happen to you? He'll use you, he'll suck your blood, he'll take away even the desire to live and abandon you. Why did you study so hard? A che cazzo è servito — what the hell was it for — for me to imagine that you would enjoy a wonderful life on my behalf too? I was wrong, you're an idiot.
