Pietro Airota

Classical philologist; now an unusually young tenured professor at Florence.

Family: Airota (the academic family)

Elena's fiancé from the last year of book 2: a serious, stocky young classical philologist, son of the famous socialist Guido Airota and brother of the art historian Mariarosa. Elena described him in book 2 as occhialuto, timidissimo, una matassa ingarbugliata di capelli nerissimi, i piedi storti — bespectacled, painfully shy, with a tangle of jet‑black hair and crooked feet. He had given her a small diamond ring, the proofs of his own book on Bacchic rites, and a wedding date set for September 1969.

In the opening chapters of book 3 he turns up in Milan, late and sheepish, having driven non‑stop from Pisa to be at Elena's bookshop launch. The night's other surprise is announced by his mother: he has just been made professore di ruolo — a tenured professor — at Florence, an unusually young one. The implication is that the wedding will come forward, probably to autumn or early next year, and Elena will leave Naples.

He confirms what we already knew about his moral framework. The wedding must be civil, at the municipio, not in church; eventual children won't be baptised. He is wary about sex (he asks, hoarsely, to come up to Elena's hotel; when she says no, he simply walks away). After the savage Corriere review he calls her twice to console her, passing on Adele's advice — the important thing is that they're talking about the book — and the news that l'Unità will print a friendly piece the next morning.

This page reflects what's known to a reader who has finished book 3. Earlier knowledge from book 2 is summarized briefly; no events from later books appear here.
