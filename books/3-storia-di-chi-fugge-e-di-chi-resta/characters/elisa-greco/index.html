Elisa Greco

Elena's youngest sibling. The household's softening voice.

Family: Greco (the porter's family)

Elena's youngest sister. Quiet, attentive, often the small softening presence in the Greco household. In book 2 she was the one who scooped Elena's broken glasses off the floor after their mother's slap and handed them back, saying gently, "Here, Lenù, they're not broken."

In book 3 chapters 8–10 she plays the same role to even better effect. When their mother flies into a full‑volume rage about the civil wedding, it is Elisa who breaks the spell with a calm, exact question — "Is it you who wants the registry office, or your fiancé?" — that gives Elena the opening to explain the Airotas' principle and lets her father switch sides. In chapter 10 it is also Elisa who carries up the phone‑call notes from signora Spagnuolo downstairs, and offers to make her shaken sister a camomilla (chamomile tea) after the Corriere review.

This page reflects what's known to a reader who has finished book 3. Earlier knowledge from book 2 is summarized briefly; no events from later books appear here.
