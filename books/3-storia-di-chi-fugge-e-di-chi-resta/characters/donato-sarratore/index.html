Donato Sarratore

Railwayman‑poet; Nino's father; Elena's first violator on Ischia.

Family: Sarratore

Nino's father — the poeta‑ferroviere, railwayman‑poet, whose name has hung over Elena's life since the Cappuccio scandal of book 1 and his molestation of her on the Ischia beach at the end of that book. He has been off‑page for most of book 2 and most of book 3.

He returns at the edges of the story in the closing chapters as Nino's unwanted shadow: a man Nino has formally cut off (rotto definitivamente, broken definitively) but whose presence in the Sarratore family — and in Lidia's and Marisa's lives — Elena cannot quite avoid on her Naples visits. His old book of poems and his old habits of polished, self‑pitying flirtation are still part of the moral atmosphere around the Sarratore household.

This page reflects what's known to a reader who has finished book 3. Earlier knowledge from book 2 is summarized briefly; no events from later books appear here.
