Immacolata Greco

Elena's mother. Newly furious about a civil wedding.

Family: Greco (the porter's family)

Elena's mother. A housewife in the rione, mother of Elena, Peppe, Gianni and Elisa, with a pronounced limp and a slightly wandering eye that obsesses her eldest daughter. Across book 2 she alternated between violence (slapping Elena across the face on the night of Lila's wedding, breaking her glasses) and a fierce protective pride that took her, alone, on a night train to Pisa to nurse Elena through illness at the Normale; by the end of book 2 she had bought an album in which to paste every review of Elena's first novel — though she will never read the book herself.

In book 3 chapters 8–10 she takes centre stage at home. Elena returns from Milan to announce the wedding plan and Immacolata's reaction detonates in three stages. First a low, hissed speech: for us you're nothing… you came from this belly and you're made of this stuff, plus an immediate demand for money. Then a cautious admiration when Elena promises to install a telephone in the apartment and buy a television a rate (on instalments). Then full‑volume rage when Elena adds that she will not be marrying in church — only at the municipio, the registry office. She threatens to throw Elena out, to slit her throat, screams that her daughter is being turned into a concubina and a troia. She is calmed only by Elisa's quiet intervention and her father's surprising switch of sides. Through the rest of these chapters she still treats every kitchen object as if it were begging to be thrown — but she stops shouting.

This page reflects what's known to a reader who has finished book 3. Earlier knowledge from book 2 is summarized briefly; no events from later books appear here.
