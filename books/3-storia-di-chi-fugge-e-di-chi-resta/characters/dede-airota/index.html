Dede Airota

Elena and Pietro's elder daughter, born early in the Florence years.

Family: Airota (Elena's marital family)

The first child of Elena and Pietro Airota, born in the early years of their Florence marriage. She arrives during the long stretch of chapters in which Elena's writing dries up: a difficult pregnancy, a long labour, and then a baby who, in Elena's exhausted account, will not stop crying and will not let her sleep. Pietro is loving but absent — shut in his study, working — and Dede grows up in the close orbit of her mother and, soon, of her younger sister Elsa.

By the time of the affair with Nino she is a watchful little girl, fiercely attached to her father. In the closing chapters she registers every fight; she wets the bed the night Elena confesses; she clings to Pietro's legs when Elena tries to leave for Viareggio, and shouts that she wants to stay with papà. When Elena finally departs for Nino, it is Dede whose distress — refusing to eat, refusing to be carried out of the apartment — most makes Elena waver.

Through her, the book registers the human price of Elena's choice without having to underline it.
