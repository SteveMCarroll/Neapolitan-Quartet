Eleonora Sarratore

Nino's wife — a rich, pregnant Neapolitan he marries while still pursuing Elena.

Family: Sarratore (by marriage)

Eleonora is the young woman Nino has secretly married by the time Elena runs into him again in Milan. The daughter of a wealthy Neapolitan banker, she is small, pale, very devout, and — when she enters the book — heavily pregnant with their first child, Albertino. Nino describes the marriage to Elena as an accident he stumbled into out of fragility and obligation, and tells her repeatedly that he does not love Eleonora, only Elena.

Eleonora knows nothing of this when the affair begins. Once she does find out, she becomes the moral lightning rod of the closing chapters: she telephones the Airota apartment in Florence and screams «zoccola, zoccola ipocrita» ("whore, hypocrite whore") into the receiver in front of Pietro and the girls; she throws Nino out of their Naples flat; and she swallows pills in what Nino describes as a near‑successful suicide attempt before begging him back.

She is, deliberately, almost a stock figure — the wronged wife with the baby on her hip — and Elena's reluctance to picture her is itself part of the moral problem of the book.
