The eldest of the five Sarratore children, son of **Donato** (the
railway poet who, in book 1, broke the Cappuccio household and then
sexually harassed Elena on Ischia) and of **Lidia Sarratore**. Nino
was the lifelong, mostly secret object of Elena's love through their
shared Naples childhood and high school, and the man who set fire to
Lila's marriage in book 2 with the Ischia and Forio affair that
produced Gennaro.

Book 3 opens in spring 1968 with Nino almost unrecognisable: black
beard, lanky and confident, **assistente di geografia economica**
(junior assistant in economic geography) at Milan's Statale university,
welcomed in the Airota circle, and apparently with no connection of
any kind to his father — he has *rotto definitivamente*, broken
definitively, with him. He saves Elena at the bookshop launch by
demolishing her hostile reviewer in front of the room, then charms his
way through her dinner and walks her partway to her hotel.

Two things about this new Nino unsettle Elena. First, he speaks
gracefully but coldly about Lila: he says she is brave, but unable to
*piegarsi alla realtà* ("bend to reality"), without *dedizione*
("devotion"), *fatta male... anche nel sesso* — "badly made… in sex
too." Second, when Pietro turns up at dinner Nino doesn't address
Elena once. He leaves without giving her a phone number or address.
She is left waving an invisible pen at his back: a wordless plea to
write to her.
