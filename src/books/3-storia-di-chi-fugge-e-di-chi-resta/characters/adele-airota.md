Pietro's mother. *Magra, non bella ma fine, elegante senza sfoggio* —
thin, not beautiful but refined, elegant without display. A translator
and informal editor for a Milan publishing house. In book 2 it was
Adele who, after Pietro brought her Elena's secret *quaderno*, had it
typed up, passed it to her publisher, arranged the contract, the press
work and the spring launch, and travelled up specifically to attend it.

In book 3 she is the engine behind the entire Milan evening. She has
seated the room, primed the friendly critic **Tarratano**, parried the
hostile professor with a single facial expression, and arranged the
dinner and the surprise: Pietro himself, driven up from Pisa. She is
also unsentimental about reviews — when the *Corriere della Sera*
prints a hatchet job the next day, her message via Pietro is exactly
the line of a working publisher: *the important thing is that they're
talking about the book.* By the end of chapter 10 she is on the phone
to Elena with the news that the book is selling and that, if it
continues, they'll print a second run; the hostile reviewer, she says
with relish, has *worked for us.*
