The daughter of Elena's former high‑school teacher **Professoressa
Galiani** and sister of **Armando**. In books 1 and 2 she was a sleek,
well‑mannered Vomero girl who briefly dated Nino. In book 3 she
reappears transformed: ostentatiously plain, badly dressed, full of
slogans, and aligned with the extreme left of the student movement —
the kind that, by the late chapters, is sliding from theory into
political violence.

She comes down from Milan with **Armando** and a group of student
agitators to "help" Lila and Pasquale at the Soccavo factory, holding
meetings at the factory gates and pressing Lila to lead a strike. Lila,
seeing the consequences other workers will pay, treats her with open
contempt; Nadia and her circle treat Lila as raw material for an
abstract working‑class their books promised them. Through Nadia, Elena
sees how the language of liberation has begun, in some hands, to glide
into something cold and dangerous.
