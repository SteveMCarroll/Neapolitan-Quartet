Pietro's father, **Guido Airota**. A medium‑height, balding man in a
rumpled grey suit, with a tired wide face and large glasses; professor
of Greek literature at the University of Genoa and a *figura di spicco*
(a leading figure) of the Italian Socialist Party. Elena met him on the
family's day in Pisa near the end of book 2 and was won over by his
warmth — he was the first adult in her life who, in her phrase, had
*given his children fairy weapons before the battle.*

In book 3 chapters 1–10 he is not yet on stage but his name carries
real weight. Nino, walking Elena to the restaurant, calls him *un uomo
davvero eccezionale*, "a truly exceptional man," and quotes from a
recent piece of his in **Il Ponte** about the state of the universities.
At dinner the same article anchors a heated political conversation.
Elena tracks down the article herself in chapter 9 and, to her own
discomfort, is *not* impressed: the prose is as rigid as the man with
the thick eyeglasses, and one passage about *signorinelle in abiti
modesti* (young ladies in modest dress) hoping for *un futuro di non
sola ritualità domestica* ("a future beyond mere domestic ritual")
feels, to her, like a coded reference to herself — the Airotas'
buttonhole flower.
