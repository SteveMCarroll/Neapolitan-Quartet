Elena's classical *liceo* teacher of Latin and Italian and her single
greatest intellectual patron of book 1's later half — a communist
*signora* of the Vomero, married to a doctor, mother of **Armando** and
**Nadia** Galiani. She is the first adult who treated Elena as a
thinker and not just as a diligent student; she lent her *l'Unità* and
*Cronache Meridionali*, invited her to the party where Elena re‑met
Nino, and later, across book 2, drifted out of Elena's daily orbit
without ever quite losing her judging place in Elena's head.

In book 3 chapters 1–10 she is not on stage but she stays exactly
where Elena left her: above and slightly to the side, mentally
auditing every public move. In chapter 10, when the *Corriere* hatchet
job lands and Elena pictures who will read it, **Galiani and her
children** are on the short list of people whose opinion she most
fears. In chapter 9 it is again Galiani's old reading regimen of
daily papers that Elena reaches for as a model when she sits down in
piazza Cavour to start a writer's notebook.
