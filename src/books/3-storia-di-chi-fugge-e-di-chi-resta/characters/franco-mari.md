A wealthy, charismatic Milanese student at the *Scuola Normale
Superiore* and a well‑known militant of the student left. In book 2 he
was, for a long stretch, Elena's boyfriend, her unofficial finishing
school, and her quiet public certification at Pisa. He bought her
clothes, took her to Paris and to Versilia, pushed her past her own
scruples about *libertà sessuale*, lent her the books and slogans she
would later half‑digest at the Airota family lunch, and was expelled
from the Normale for political activism near the end of her third
year — with that expulsion her informal protection in piazza dei
Cavalieri lapsed.

In book 3 chapters 1–10 he is not yet on stage but Elena keeps
returning to him in narration. The bookshop disaster sends her into
the bookshops of **Port'Alba** in chapter 9 with the explicit aim of
re‑mastering the kind of left political reading Franco had once pushed
on her: the Marxist texts, the magazines, the disciplined catching‑up.
She is now consciously building, alone, the political education she
used to borrow whole from him.
