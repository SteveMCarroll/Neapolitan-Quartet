## Summary

The narrative jumps back over forty years, to a cold spring evening in **Milan**, at the bookshop launch of Elena's first novel — the moment where book 2 ended. Elena, now writing in old age, says she has been working at her desk for weeks, partly in the hope that a still‑living Lila will indeed sneak into the file and read it.

On the evening itself, a man with thick eyeglasses had been demolishing her in front of the audience, and Elena, intimidated by Adele Airota and her circle, had answered badly. Then Nino Sarratore had stood up — almost unrecognisable behind a black beard — and laid into her attacker. Elena's whole body had started silently shouting his name; she had not seen him in four or five years.

The man with the thick glasses asks to reply. He no longer cares about Elena's book; what offended him was the **political twist** Nino gave the discussion, his use of phrases like *protervia baronale* ("baronial arrogance") and *letteratura antiautoritaria* ("anti‑authoritarian literature"). He mocks these terms in a high sarcastic falsetto and lectures the audience on "authority" as a bulwark against badly‑educated young men spouting nonsense from some splinter course at the Statale university. He aims his final shots at Adele herself — *«gli adulti addottorati… sempre pronti a cavalcare per interesse l'ultima moda della stupidità»* ("PhD‑adults always ready to ride the latest fashionable stupidity for their own gain") — and walks out.

The audience parts deferentially: even Adele returns his stiff nod with a polite "arrivederla." Clearly he is a man of real prestige. But Nino is unimpressed. With long‑legged agility he cuts the man off at the exit, calls him by his title — *«professore, dove va, non scappi»* ("professor, where are you going, don't run away") — and says a few short, hard sentences that Elena can't quite hear. The man listens motionless, then waves him off and leaves.
