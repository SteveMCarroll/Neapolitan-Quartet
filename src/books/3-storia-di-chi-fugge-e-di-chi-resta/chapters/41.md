## Summary

Lila brings her list of demands to the committee on via dei Tribunali —
twelve people now, including Nadia, Armando, Isabella and Pasquale. She
circulates the first, fuller version she had drafted for Capone. Nadia
reads it carefully and is impressed: this is excellent work, beautifully
argued, beautifully written. *Come sei brava* — "how good you are," she
says, when has anyone seen anyone write about *questa materia* (factory
work, union demands) like this?

Then the soft brake. Nadia and Armando counsel patience. The
*fabbrichetta* — the little Soccavo plant — *ha bisogno di maturare*, it
needs time to mature; they've barely got a foothold there, a frontal
clash with Soccavo right now would risk having them swept away *per
pura avventatezza*, out of pure rashness. Better to call an enlarged
meeting, consolidate Lila's own faction inside the factory, then maybe
turn her material into another mimeographed leaflet.

Lila feels a coarse, fight-picking satisfaction at finally putting them
on the back foot. *Secondo voi io ho fatto questa fatica e sto mettendo
a rischio il posto di lavoro per permettere a voi di fare una riunione
allargata e un altro ciclostilato?* — so I did all this work and I'm
risking my job just so you can hold a bigger meeting and run off another
pamphlet?

But she can't enjoy it. Nadia, sitting opposite her, suddenly seems to
vibrate like a badly fitted pane of glass — she *si sgranò*, comes
apart, ungrains. Lila's throat tightens, every small gesture in the
room — even a blink — accelerates. She shuts her eyes, leans back on
her rickety chair, feels she's suffocating. The first proper
*smarginatura* episode witnessed by other people.

Armando catches it instantly — *qualcosa non va?* — Pasquale fusses for
water. Lila waves them off but accepts a sip and mutters that it's just
a touch of *influenza*. Armando takes her into another room — bare,
cold, three filthy mattresses — and gives her a proper exam. She hates
the silence of it: a *silenzio perfido*, a treacherous silence in which
he treats only her body, augmented by instruments, as a reliable
witness, while her answers are something to be cross-checked.

He works through the list. Do you sleep? Fine. How much? *Dipende dai
pensieri* — depends on the thoughts. Do you eat enough? *Quando mi va*
— when I feel like it. Period regular? She doesn't know — she doesn't
mark it down. *Bisogna segnarselo?* Do people mark that down? Are you
using contraception? She doesn't know what he means. He explains: the
new *pillola* (the pill), condoms, the IUD. She has barely heard of any
of it.

Diagnosis: she's undernourished and badly shaken, has a touch of
catarrh, her liver is slightly enlarged, she needs tests. And the
crucial finding: *hai un soffio* — you have a murmur. *Potrebbe essere
una cosa non benigna* — it might not be benign. Lila grimaces: am I
going to die? Armando smiles: no, you just need to see a cardiologist;
come by the hospital tomorrow and I'll send you to a good one.

Lila straightens up, ice-cold: tomorrow I have things to do. *Vado da
Soccavo* — I'm going to Soccavo's.
