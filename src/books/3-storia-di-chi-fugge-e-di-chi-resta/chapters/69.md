## Summary

Two flirtations push the home situation to the breaking point. The
first is an assistant in Greek literature, her own age, unhappy in
his engagement back in Asti; she pulls back as soon as he tries to
make it explicit. The second is more serious: Mario, the husband of
a papyrology lecturer, an engineer who teaches mechanics, with a
big political CV and serious local clout, long hair, drummer in a
rock band in his spare time, seven years older than Elena.

The pattern is the same. Pietro invites them over. Elena flirts.
Phone calls, demos together, long walks — sometimes with Dede,
sometimes alone — and a few films. Mario doesn't stop at the door
the assistant did. One evening, in his car, he kisses her at length
and slips his hands under her bra. She fights him off, says she
doesn't want to see him again. He calls, calls again, she misses
him, she gives in. Having kissed and groped her once, he treats
those as won territory and resumes from there — pressing, proposing,
demanding. When she half-encourages and half-pulls away, laughing,
he sulks and turns nasty.

One morning she's on a walk with him, Dede in tow with the doll she
loves, named Tes (the child invented the name). Elena is wrapped up
in the verbal game with Mario; she more or less forgets the toddler
is there. Mario doesn't acknowledge Dede either, except to lean in
playfully and whisper into her ear things like *per favore, puoi dire
alla tua mamma che dev'essere buona con me?* — "tell your mama she
has to be nice to me, please?" Time flies. They part. Elena and Dede
start back. After a few steps Dede announces severely: *Tes mi ha
detto che dirà un segreto a papà* — "Tes told me she'll tell papà a
secret." Elena's heart stops. What kind of secret? *Una cosa brutta.*
She threatens her daughter: tell Tes that if she tells papà she'll
lock her in the dark closet. Dede sobs, and Elena has to carry her
all the way home — a toddler who normally pretends not to be tired
to please her. So Dede had understood, or sensed, that what was
going on with that man was something her father would not tolerate.

Elena breaks it off again. What was Mario, after all? *Un borghese
malato di pornolalìa* — a bourgeois with a case of dirty-talk-itis.
But the restlessness only grows. She wants to break a rule. She
wants to step outside her marriage — *or, why not, outside her whole
life*: what she's studied, what she's written, what she's trying to
write, the child she's brought into the world. Marriage is a prison;
Lila, who had courage, escaped at the cost of her own life. What
risks is she running with Pietro, so distracted, so absent? *Nessuno.*
None. So? She calls Mario. She leaves Dede with Clelia and goes to
his studio. They kiss; he sucks her nipples; he touches her between
her legs *come faceva Antonio agli stagni tanti anni prima* — the way
Antonio used to at the ponds years ago. But when he pulls down his
pants, briefs at his knees, and grabs the back of her neck to push
her down toward him, she twists free, says no, fixes her clothes, and
runs.

She gets home shaking with guilt and makes love to Pietro with a
passion she has never had for him — she's the one who insists he not
use the condom. *Di cosa mi preoccupo,* she tells herself — what am
I worried about, my period is close, nothing will happen. Something
happens. A few weeks later she finds out she is pregnant again.
