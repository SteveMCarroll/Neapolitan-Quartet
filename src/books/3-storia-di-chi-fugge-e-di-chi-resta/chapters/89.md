## Summary

Lila has slammed the phone down. Elena calls her parents' house in
panic. Her mother answers with the usual cold welcome: *ogni tanto
ti ricordi che esistiamo* (every now and then you remember we
exist). What's happening with Elisa? *Quello che succede alle
femmine d'oggi* — what happens to girls today. *Sta con uno.*
With whom? The answer goes through her heart: *con Marcello
Solara.*

So this is what Lila wanted her to know. Marcello, the handsome
Marcello of their early adolescence, Lila's stubborn desperate
first suitor, the young man Lila humiliated by marrying Stefano
Carracci — Marcello has taken Elisa, the youngest, *la mia
sorellina buona*, the little sister Elena still feels as a magic
child. And Elisa let herself be taken. And her parents and brothers
have not lifted a finger to stop it. And the whole Greco family,
in some way Elena herself, will end up *imparentati ai Solara*
(family-related to the Solaras).

*Da quanto tempo? — Che ne so, un anno.* And you consented? *Tu
ce l'hai chiesto il consenso? Hai fatto come ti pareva. E lei ha
fatto la stessa cosa. — Pietro non è Marcello Solara. — Hai
ragione: Marcello non si farebbe mai trattare da Elisa come Pietro
si fa trattare da te.* You could have told me, consulted me. *E
perché? Te ne sei andata. "Penso io a voi, non vi preoccupate."
Macché. Hai pensato solo ai fatti tuoi, di noi te ne sei fottuta.*

She decides at once to leave for Naples with the children. She
wants the train; Pietro insists on driving, pretending the trip is
generosity but in truth fleeing his book. Coming down from the
*Doganella* into the city's traffic Elena feels herself seized
again by Naples, *comandata dalle sue leggi non scritte.* She
hasn't set foot here since she left to get married. The noise is
unbearable; the constant honking, the insults motorists throw at
Pietro when he hesitates and slows. Just before Piazza Carlo III
she makes him pull over, takes the wheel, drives aggressively up
to *via Firenze*, to the same hotel where Pietro stayed years ago.
They drop the bags. She gives the two girls meticulous attention,
then they go to her parents' flat in the rione. What is she going
to do? Impose her authority as elder, educated, well-married
sister? Make Elisa break it off? Tell her: I have known Marcello
since the day he grabbed my wrist and tried to drag me into his
Millecento, snapping our mother's silver bracelet — *fidati, è un
uomo volgare e violento.* Yes. She feels resolved. Her job is to
pull Elisa out of the trap.

Her mother greets Pietro with great warmth and pulls out one little
present after another for the children — *questo per Dede di nonna,
questo per Elsa* — to their delight in different ways. Her
father's voice goes hoarse with emotion; he looks thinner, more
beaten down. She waits for the brothers; they aren't home.

*Stanno sempre a lavorare*, her father says without enthusiasm.
What do they do? *Faticano* — they work hard, her mother cuts in.
Where? *Li ha sistemati Marcello.* Marcello has fixed them up. The
phrase opens a memory — how the Solaras *sistemarono* Antonio,
what they turned him into. *A fare cosa?* she asks. Her mother,
stiff: *portano i soldi a casa e questo basta. Elisa non è come te,
Lenù, Elisa pensa a tutti noi.*

She lets it pass. *Glielo hai detto che sarei arrivata oggi? Dov'è?*
Her father lowers his eyes. Her mother, dry: *a casa sua.* You
mean she doesn't live here anymore? *No. Quasi due mesi. Lei e
Marcello hanno un bell'appartamento al rione nuovo.*
