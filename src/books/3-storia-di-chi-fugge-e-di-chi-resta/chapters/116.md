## Summary

She hangs up in tears, comes out of the booth. Elsa asks: *ti sei
fatta male, mamma?* — did you hurt yourself, mum? She says, no,
she's fine; it's grandmother who isn't well. She goes on sobbing
under the worried eyes of both girls.

For the rest of the holiday she does nothing but cry. She tells
Pietro she's tired, that it's too hot, that her head aches, and
sends him with the children to the beach. She lies on the bed
soaking the pillow. She loathes this *fragilità eccessiva* —
excessive fragility; she had never been like this even as a child.
Both she and Lila had trained themselves never to cry; on the rare
occasions they had, they had stifled the sobs, *la vergogna era
grande* — the shame was great. Now a fountain has opened in her
head *come a Orlando* — like Orlando's — running through her eyes
without ever drying up, and even when Pietro and the girls are
about to come back and she forces the tears down and runs to splash
her face at the tap, the fountain just waits for the next chance
to come back to the surface of her eyes.

Nino, she tells herself, didn't really want her; Nino *fingeva
molto e amava poco* — pretended much and loved little. He had
wanted to *chiavarmi* — to screw her, blunt — as he had who knows
how many others. But to have her, to have her for ever, breaking
the link to his wife — that was never in the plan. Probably he
was still in love with Lila. Probably in the course of his life he
would love only her, like so many others who had known her. And
that love for Lila was the guarantee that no woman, however he
wanted her *al suo modo travolgente* (in his overwhelming way),
would ever endanger his fragile marriage — she least of all. *Ecco
come stavano le cose* — that was how things stood. She sometimes
left lunch or dinner half-eaten to go and sob in the bathroom.

Pietro handles her cautiously, sensing she could explode at any
moment. In the hours just after the break-up she had thought to
tell him everything, as if he were not only a husband to whom an
explanation was due but also a confessor. The need was strong,
especially in bed when he edged toward her and she pushed him off
murmuring *no, si svegliano le bambine* (no, the girls will wake).
But she stopped each time. There was no need to tell him about
Nino. Now that she no longer phoned the man she loved, now that
she felt him *definitivamente persa* — definitively lost — it
seemed pointless to *infierire* — to lay into Pietro. Better a
clean ending in few words: *non posso più vivere con te*. But
even that she couldn't manage. Just when, in the half-light of
the bedroom, she felt ready, she'd feel sorry for him, fear for
the children's future, stroke his shoulder, his cheek, murmur:
*dormi* — sleep.

Things change on the last evening of the holiday. It is nearly
midnight; the girls are asleep. For ten days she has not phoned
Nino. The bags are packed; she is worn down by melancholy,
fatigue, heat. She and Pietro sit on the balcony, each in his
deck chair, in silence. *C'era un'umidità estenuante* — an
exhausting humidity — wets hair and clothes; a smell of sea and
resin drifts up. Out of the blue, Pietro says:

*Come sta tua madre?* — How's your mother?

My mother? Yes. Fine. Dede told me she's ill. She's recovered. *Le
ho telefonato oggi pomeriggio* — I phoned her this afternoon. *Tua
madre è stata sempre in ottima salute* — your mother has always
been in excellent health.

She says nothing. *Com'era inopportuno quell'uomo* — how clumsy
that man was. The tears are starting again. She hears him say,
calmly:

*Tu pensi che io sia cieco e sordo* — You think I'm blind and
deaf. You think I haven't noticed how you flirted with those
imbeciles who came around the house before Elsa was born. I don't
know what you're talking about. *Lo sai benissimo* — you know
perfectly well. No, I don't — who are you talking about? People
who came over for dinner years ago? Are you crazy?

He shakes his head, smiles to himself, waits a few seconds, then
fixes the railing and asks: *non flirtavi nemmeno con quello che
suonava la batteria?* — weren't you flirting with the one who
played drums?

She is alarmed: he isn't backing off, isn't yielding. *Sbuffai* —
she huffed. *Mario?* You see you remember? Of course I remember,
why shouldn't I? He's one of the few interesting people you've
brought to the house in seven years of marriage. You found him
interesting? Yes. So what? What's got into you tonight?

*Voglio sapere. Non posso sapere?* — I want to know. Am I not
allowed to know? What is it you want to know? What I know, you
know. Four years since we last saw that guy, and you come out
with this rubbish now?

He stops staring at the railing, turns and looks at her, serious:

*Allora parliamo di fatti più recenti. Cosa c'è tra te e Nino?*
— Then let's talk about more recent things. *What is going on
between you and Nino?*
