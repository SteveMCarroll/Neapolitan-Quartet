## Summary

Elena walks down to Mergellina and on to piazza dei Martiri just as the black sky drops almost onto the rooftops. She rushes into the elegant Solara shoe shop convinced the storm is about to break, and finds Alfonso lovelier than she remembered — big eyes with long lashes, well-drawn lips, a slim, strong body, an Italian made faintly artificial by years of Latin and Greek. He is openly glad to see her. The shared exhaustion of *ginnasio* and *liceo* picks them up where they left off, and they fall into easy talk — old teachers, her book, his coming wedding, hers.

She steers it toward Lila. Alfonso doesn't want to say anything bad about Lila, his brother, or Ada. He only says: *«Era prevedibile che finisse così»* — that it was bound to end like this. Why? Remember when I used to tell you Lila scared me? Elena nods. *«Non era paura, l'ho capito molto dopo.»* What was it, then? *«Estraneità e adesione, un effetto di lontananza e vicinanza insieme»* — strangeness and adherence, an effect of distance and closeness at once. Hard to put, he says. With you and me it was always friendship, simple. With Lila it felt impossible, but she had something tremendous in her that made him want to kneel and confess his most secret thoughts. Elena teases — almost a religious experience. He stays serious: no, just an admission of *subalternità*. The good part was when Lila helped him study, summarising textbooks in a few clear sentences. There are still moments, he says, when he thinks: *se fossi nato femmina mi sarebbe piaciuto essere come lei* — if I'd been born a girl I'd have wanted to be like her. In the Carracci family the two of them were always foreign bodies; neither of them could last there. So her *colpe*, her faults — he never cared. He has always felt himself on her side. As for Stefano, even if he still hates Lila, he has too many troubles to notice. Lila now is the least of his problems.

Elena lets Lila go, asks instead about Marisa, the Sarratore family, Nino. Alfonso is vague on all of them, especially on Nino — *per volontà di Donato*, by Donato's will, nobody dared invite him to these intolerable nuptials. *«Non sei contento di sposarti?»* Elena risks. Alfonso looks out at the lightning, the thunder, the rain that hasn't quite begun. *«Stavo bene come stavo.»* — I was fine as I was. *«E Marisa?»* She wasn't fine. *«Volevi che facesse la fidanzata a vita?»* He doesn't know. So in the end you gave her what she wanted? *«S'è rivolta a Michele.»* — She went to Michele. Elena, confused: in what sense? A nervous little laugh. *«È andata da lui, l'ha messo contro di me.»* She went to him; she got him to push me. Elena sits on a pouf; he stands against the light, tense and compact as a *torero* in a film. *«Non ho capito: tu sposi Marisa perché lei ha chiesto a Solara di dirti che lo dovevi fare?»* *«Io sposo Marisa per non dare un dispiacere a Michele.»* — I'm marrying Marisa so I don't disappoint Michele. He put me here, he trusted my abilities, I owe him. You're crazy. *«Dici così perché avete tutti un'idea sbagliata di Michele, non sapete com'è.»* His face contracts, he holds back tears with difficulty. He adds: *«Marisa è incinta.»* — Marisa is pregnant. There's the real reason. Elena takes his hand, embarrassed, tries to calm him. He's slow to settle. *«La vita è una cosa molto brutta, Lenù.»* No, you'll see — Marisa will be a good wife and a fine mother. *«Me ne fotto di Marisa.»*

Then his eyes lock on her as if reading her, and he asks: *«Nemmeno a te Lina ha mai detto niente?»* What was she supposed to tell me? He shakes his head, suddenly amused. *Vedi che ho ragione?* That's how I know I'm right about her — she really is unusual. Once I told her a secret. I was frightened and I had to say the reason for the fear to somebody. I told her, and she listened with such attention that I calmed down. It was important. It was as if she didn't hear with her ears but with some organ only she had, which made the words bearable. I didn't even make her swear afterwards. And it's clear now that if she never said it to you — Elena, her oldest friend — she never said it to anyone, not even out of spite during her hardest months, the months of Stefano's hatred and beatings.

Elena says nothing. She is small-hurt: he confided in Lila and not in her. He notices, and tries to make it good. He hugs her tight, murmurs in her ear: *«Lenù, sono ricchione, le femmine non mi piacciono»* — Lenù, I'm queer, I don't like women. As she's leaving he says quietly, awkwardly: I was sure you'd already understood. That sharpens the hurt — it had never crossed her mind.
