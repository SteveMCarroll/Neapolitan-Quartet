## Summary

The disorder Pietro can't tolerate suddenly stops being abstract. Mariarosa
phones from Milan: Franco has been jumped by fascists. He is in terrible
condition. He has lost an eye. Elena leaves at once with Dede and baby Elsa.
On the train, playing and feeding the girls, she is shadowed by another
version of herself — the poor uncultured girlfriend of the well-off,
hyper-political student Franco Mari. *Quante me c'erano ormai?* — how many
me's are there by now?

Mariarosa meets her at the station, pale, on edge, and takes them to her
apartment — emptier than Elena remembered, even messier than the time she
stayed there after the university assembly. Once Dede is busy and Elsa is
asleep, she tells the story properly. Five days ago Franco spoke at an
*Avanguardia Operaia* rally in a packed little theater. Afterwards he walked
home with Silvia, who now lives near the theater with a *Giorno* editor in a
fine apartment. He was going to sleep there and leave for Piacenza in the
morning. They had almost reached the door — Silvia had her keys in her hand —
when a white van pulled up. Fascists jumped out. Franco was beaten almost to
pieces; Silvia was beaten and raped.

They drink a lot of wine. Mariarosa brings out drugs — *la droga* — and Elena
tries them for the first time, because despite the wine she can't find one
good thing to hold onto. After more increasingly angry talk Mariarosa goes
silent, then cries; the tears, Elena thinks, are loud as they slide down her
cheeks. Suddenly the room goes black; Elena faints. She comes to,
embarrassed, blames tiredness. She sleeps badly: her body weighed down by an
excess of discipline, *il lessico dei libri… grondava angoscia come se di
colpo i segni dell'alfabeto fossero non più combinabili* — the lexicon of her
books oozing dread, as if the letters of the alphabet could no longer be
combined.

The next day she leaves the girls with Mariarosa and goes to the hospital.
Franco is in a greenish ward smelling of breath, urine, medicines. He looks
shrunken and swollen at once — white bandages, a violet bruise covering half
his face and neck. He doesn't welcome her; he seems ashamed of his state.
She talks about her daughters. He murmurs: *va' via, non ti voglio qui* — go
away, I don't want you here. When she presses, he says, irritated: *non sono
io, vattene* — this isn't me, leave. Another operation may be coming. She
goes back wrecked. Mariarosa puts her to bed.

The next day Mariarosa insists they go see Silvia. Elena tries to refuse —
seeing Franco was already unbearable; she would rather remember Silvia as she
saw her at the Statale rally. No, Mariarosa says, *vuole che la vediamo com'è
adesso, ci tiene* — she wants us to see her as she is now, it matters to
her. They go.

Silvia's mother answers — well-groomed, with a fall of bright-blond hair to
her shoulders. With her is Mirko, blond too, now five or six. Dede, in her
bossy-pouty way, immediately presses him into a game with Tes, her doll.
Silvia is sleeping but has left word to be woken. They wait a long time.
She emerges heavily made up, in a beautiful long green dress. What strikes
Elena isn't the cuts and bruises — Lila came back from her honeymoon worse —
but the look in the eyes: empty. Out of phase with her chatter, which comes
out in a frenetic patter broken by little laughs. To Elena alone, who hasn't
yet heard, she recites, *come se recitasse una filastrocca atroce* — as if
reciting an atrocious nursery rhyme — exactly what the fascists did to her.
The story has sedimented through repetition. Her mother tries more than once
to stop her; Silvia waves her off, pitches her voice up, scans obscenities,
sketches a near future of fierce vengeance. When Elena cries Silvia stops —
but new visitors arrive, family friends and political *compagne*, and Silvia
restarts. Elena withdraws to a corner with Elsa.

After a while she goes looking for Dede. She finds her in the hallway with
Mirko and the doll. They are playing mother and father with their baby — but
not a peaceful game: they are staging a fight. Dede is coaching Mirko: *mi
devi dare uno schiaffo, capito?* — you have to slap me, all right? The new
living flesh repeats the old as a game. *Eravamo una catena di ombre che
andava da sempre in scena con la stessa carica di amore, di odio, di voglie e
di violenza* — we were a chain of shadows that had been onstage forever with
the same charge of love, hatred, desire and violence. Elena studies Dede:
she looks like Pietro. Mirko, on the other hand, is identical to Nino.
