## Summary

From her parents' house Elena makes a long call to Pietro and walks him
through every one of Lila's troubles, and how much she wants to help.
He listens with patience. At one point he even shows initiative: he
remembers a young Pisan classicist obsessed with computers and the
fantasy that they will revolutionize philology. *Rintraccialo*, Elena
begs — track him down, ask about Enzo, maybe a job lead will turn up.

He promises he will. And he reminds her that Mariarosa had a brief
romance with a young Neapolitan lawyer — maybe he could put Elena in
touch.

— To do what?

— To recover your friend's money.

Elena lights up. *Telefona a Mariarosa.* — Va bene. — *Non promettere
soltanto, telefona davvero, per favore.* Don't just promise, actually
call, please. A pause. Then: *In questo momento hai fatto il tono di
mia madre*, you just sounded like my mother. — In what sense? — *Sembravi
lei quando una cosa le sta molto a cuore.* You sounded like her when
something matters to her. Elena tries to laugh it off: I'm far too
different, unfortunately. Another pause. *Meno male che sei diversa*,
just as well you are. *Comunque lei in queste cose è ineguagliabile.*
In these matters she is unsurpassable. Tell her about this girl and
you'll see, she'll help.

So Elena calls Adele. With some embarrassment, which she beats down by
remembering everything Adele has already done — for the book, for the
search for the apartment in Florence. Adele is the kind of woman who
loves to be at work. When she needs something she picks up the phone
and, link by link, builds the chain that gets her to the goal. She
asks in a way that makes refusing impossible. She crosses ideological
borders without a blink, ignores hierarchies — she'll dial cleaning
women, low-level clerks, industrialists, intellectuals, ministers —
and addresses each of them with friendly detachment, as if the favor
she's about to ask is really one she is doing them.

Elena tells the whole story — too many apologies for the
inconvenience — and Adele is hooked, indignant, fired up. *Lasciami
riflettere*, let me think it over.

— Of course.

— *Intanto posso darti un consiglio?* Meanwhile, can I give you some
advice?

— Of course.

— *Non essere timida.* Don't be shy. *Sei una scrittrice, usa il tuo
ruolo, sperimentalo, fallo pesare.* You're a writer, use your role,
try it out, throw your weight around. *Questi sono tempi decisivi, sta
andando tutto per aria. Partecipa, sii presente.* These are decisive
times, everything is up in the air. Take part. Be present. *E comincia
con questa gentaglia delle tue parti, mettila con le spalle al muro.*
And start with that bunch where you come from — put their backs to
the wall.

— How?

— *Scrivendo.* By writing. *Spaventa a morte Soccavo e quelli come
lui.* Scare Soccavo and his kind to death. Promise you'll do it?

*Ci provo*, Elena says — I'll try. Adele gives her the name of an
editor at *l'Unità* — the Italian Communist Party's daily newspaper.
