## Summary

While Carmen is torn apart by the new persecution, Elena cannot stop
picturing the pharmacy: the smell of *caramelle e sciroppi* (candy and
syrups), the dark wooden shelves of coloured jars, and especially
Gino's parents — gentle, slightly stooped behind the counter as
behind a balcony — who must have been standing right there when the
shots cracked, and who, perhaps, watched their son fall on the
threshold in his own blood.

She calls Lila to share it. Lila is completely indifferent. The
killing is just one of many, *figurati* (of course) the carabinieri
would go after Pasquale; and even if Pasquale really had shot Gino,
which she rules out, she would still be on his side, because the
police would do better to hold the *morto* (the dead man) accountable
for all his ugly deeds than their old friend the bricklayer and
*comunista*. Then, with the brisk tone of someone moving on to real
business, she asks if Elena can take gennaro-carracci until school starts
again.

Elena is taken aback. *gennaro-carracci? E come facevo?* She already has Dede
and Elsa wearing her out. She hedges: she's going to Viareggio for
the rest of August; the boy hardly knows her, he'll want his mother;
if Lila comes too it's fine, but on her own she doesn't know how.
Lila throws Elena's own old promise back at her — *m'avevi giurato
che ti saresti occupata di lui* (you swore to me you would look
after him). The conversation grows curt. *E allora non lo puoi
lasciare a tua madre o a Stefano?* Lila falls silent, then drops
the courtesy: *Me lo fai questo favore, sì o no?* Elena gives in
at once.

Enzo arrives on a Saturday evening in a brand-new white *Cinquecento*
(little Fiat 500). Just seeing him from the window and hearing the
*dialetto* he uses with the boy still in the car gives Naples and
the rione weight again — *stesso gesto compassato, stessa compattezza
dell'organismo*. Then Elena opens the door, Dede attached to her
skirt, and one look at gennaro-carracci is enough: Melina was right five years
ago. The ten-year-old has nothing of Nino in him, and nothing of
Lila either. He is a perfect copy of Stefano Carracci.

Elena feels a confused mix of disappointment and a small, mean
satisfaction: it would have been pleasant to have a little Nino in
the house alongside her own girls — but she is glad to register
that Nino, in the end, left Lila nothing.
