## Summary

The workday passes in low-grade panic, hidden behind Lila's usual mask
— now contemptuous, now menacing. Everyone makes her feel it: this
sudden climate of tension is *her* fault. Two factions quickly form.
A small one wants to use the moment to send Lila to ask Bruno for some
small economic concessions; a larger one won't speak to her and wants
nothing to do with anything that complicates their already complicated
lives. Edo, sore-handed and on edge, threatens the second group with a
can of petrol and a match if his cut goes septic. Lila ignores both
camps and works with her usual efficiency.

But her mind churns. What happened to those beaten kids? Where did they
run? What hole have they dug for her? Gino will gossip her name all
over the rione, then straight to Michele Solara. And, humiliating
though it is, she will have to ask Bruno for help — she's afraid of
being fired, of losing the *salario* that lets her love Enzo without
needing him to survive. Then her own bad night comes back: should she
see a doctor? And if a doctor finds something, what then — about the
work, about the child? *Attenzione, non agitarsi.* Easy, easy.

In the lunch break she ducks into the toilets, fixes her hair, puts
on a little lipstick — *disprezzandosi*, despising herself — and goes
to find Bruno. The secretary tells her, hostile, that he's not in and
won't be in all week. Panic climbs. Maybe Pasquale can keep the
students away from the gate; if the boys vanish, the fascists vanish.
But she doesn't know where his building site is, and the rione is the
last place she wants to go: she's terrified of bumping into her
mother, her father, *suo fratello soprattutto* — Rino above all, who
she does not want to face.

So she decides to go straight to Nadia. After her shift she races
home, leaves Enzo a note about dinner, bundles Gennaro into coat and
wool cap, and rides bus after bus up to **corso Vittorio Emanuele** —
the same address she climbed, years ago, with me, to that disastrous
party at the Galianis'. The past keeps crumbling and falling on her.
She wants to tell Nadia to her face: *tu e gli altri state mettendo
nei guai mio figlio* — "you and the others are putting my son in
danger." For *you* it's a hobby; for me, for him, it's serious.

The front door is open. She climbs. **Professoressa Galiani** herself
opens — unchanged, gracious, immaculate. Lila feels filthy beside her:
the smell of raw meat clinging to her clothes, the cold in her chest,
the fever scrambling her feelings, the child whining in dialect. She
asks brusquely: is Nadia here? No, *fa quello che le pare* — "she does
as she pleases." Could you tell her Lina came by? Is it urgent? Yes.
Would you like to tell *me*? Lila falters. She looks past Galiani into
the rooms — the old aristocratic furniture, the wall of books that
once enchanted her, the paintings. *Ecco il mondo cui ambiva Nino* —
this is the world Nino was aiming for, before he got stuck with me.
And: what do I know of this other Naples? Nothing. *Che sia
distrutta, allora* — let it be destroyed, then; let fire and ash come;
let the lava reach the tops of the hills.

No, she says finally, I need to talk to Nadia myself. She's about to
leave — the trip was for nothing — but she likes the cool hostility
in the way Galiani spoke of her own daughter, and on a sudden frivolous
impulse she says: *sa che qualche anno fa sono stata in questa casa
per una festa? Mi aspettavo chissà che e invece mi sono annoiata, non
vedevo l'ora di andarmene* — "did you know I came to this house for a
party years ago? I expected I-don't-know-what and I was bored; I
couldn't wait to leave."

## Key vocabulary

| Italian | English | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| salario | wages, salary | what Lila is terrified of losing |
| attenzione, non agitarsi | "easy now, don't panic" | reflexive verb *agitarsi* |
| disprezzandosi | despising herself | gerund, reflexive |
| tirare la corda | to push your luck | Bruno's earlier warning, still ringing |
| la santarellina | "the little saint" | Lila's word for Nadia |
| fare quello che le pare | "to do as she pleases" | Galiani's dry line about her daughter |
| frivolo / frivola | frivolous, light | Lila's deliberate tone change |
| che sia distrutta | "let it be destroyed" | subjunctive *sia* |
| lava | lava | the Vesuvian image of city-erasing fury |
| non vedevo l'ora di andarmene | "I couldn't wait to leave" | a very useful idiom — *non vedere l'ora di* = "to look forward to" |
