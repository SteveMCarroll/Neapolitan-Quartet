## Summary

Elena slips back into her own bed around four in the morning. Pietro
stirs, murmurs *che succede?* — what's happening? She tells him,
peremptory, to sleep, and he obeys. She lies stunned. She is happy
about what has just happened, but cannot quite take it in *all'interno
della mia condizione*, inside the condition she actually inhabits —
this house, Florence, wife, mother. Everything between her and Nino
seems to have happened somewhere else: in the *rione* while his
parents were moving out and Melina was hurling things from the
window; on Ischia when they walked hand in hand; that evening in
Milan when he defended her against the ferocious critic. For a moment
this gives her *un senso di irresponsabilità, forse persino di
innocenza* — a sense of irresponsibility, perhaps even of innocence —
as if Lila's friend, Pietro's wife, the mother of Dede and Elsa had
nothing to do with the girl-young-woman-woman who loved Nino and had
finally taken him. The traces of his hands and his kisses are all
over her body. *La smania di godimento* — the craving for pleasure —
will not settle.

She dozes off; she wakes with a jolt to a room full of light. What
have I done? she thinks. *In casa mia, che sciocchezza* — in my own
house, what foolishness. She gets up, takes a long shower, dries her
hair, puts on careful makeup, dresses *come se dovessi uscire*, as if
she were going out. She and Nino had sworn in the middle of the night
not to lose each other again; but how, and when? Probably nothing
more would happen. She sets the breakfast table with care. She wants
to leave him with a good image of this stay, of the house, of her.

Pietro shuffles in, in his pyjamas, hair *arruffato* — rumpled. He
asks where she's going. Nowhere, she answers. He looks at her,
puzzled — she's never this put-together in the morning. *Stai molto
bene*, he says — you look very nice. *Non per merito tuo* — no
thanks to you, she replies. He looks out of the window, mutters that
he was very tired last night. *Anche molto maleducato*, she returns
— also very rude. He says he'll apologise. She tells him he should
apologise first of all to *her*. He says, simply: *scusa*. Then she
says: today he's leaving.

Dede appears barefoot. Elena fetches the slippers and wakes Elsa,
who, eyes still closed, smothers her in kisses. *Che buon odore
aveva, com'era morbida* — what a good smell, how soft. Yes, she
tells herself, it happened. She must now *darmi una disciplina* —
give herself a discipline: phone Mariarosa about France, talk to
Adele, go in person to the publisher and find out whether they
actually believe in her little book or are only humouring her
mother-in-law.

Then she hears noises in the corridor. Nino is up. She pulls free of
Elsa, says she'll be right back, runs out. She catches him sleepy in
the hallway, *lo spinsi nel bagno* — she pushes him into the
bathroom and closes the door. They kiss. She loses again any
awareness of where or when she is. The hunger surprises her; she is
good at hiding things from herself. They cling to one another with a
fury she has never known, *come se i corpi sbattessero l'uno contro
l'altro con l'intenzione di rompersi* — as if their bodies were
hurling themselves against each other meaning to break. Pleasure,
she discovers, is this: to shatter, to merge, no longer to know
what is hers and what is his. If Pietro had come in, if the children
had appeared at the door, they would not have been able to tell
them apart.

She whispers into his mouth: *resta ancora* — stay a bit longer. He
can't. Then come back — *giura che torni*. Yes. And telephone me.
Yes. Tell me you won't forget me, tell me you won't leave me, tell
me you love me. *Ti amo.* Say it again. *Ti amo.* Swear it isn't a
lie. *Giuro.*
