## Summary

Elena hands the money over. Lila counts it twice with satisfaction and returns at once what she'd borrowed. Enzo arrives back from his meeting with the computer expert Pietro lined up. He's pleased, within the limits of his impassive way — emotion in him still gets strangled before it reaches his mouth. Lila and Elena have to coax the news out of him in pieces.

The expert was kind. At first he repeated the standard line — the Zurich correspondence course is wasted money — but then realised that, despite the worthless course, Enzo was good. He told him IBM is about to begin Italian production at a new plant in Vimercate, and the Naples branch urgently needs *perforatori-verificatori*, *operatori*, *programmatori-analisti* — keypunch operators, machine operators, programmer-analysts. When the company opens its training courses, he promised to let Enzo know. He took down all his details.

*«T'è sembrato uno serio?»* — did he seem serious? Lila asks. Enzo, to vouch for the man's seriousness, nods at Elena: *«Sapeva tutto del fidanzato di Lenuccia.»* He knew all about Lenuccia's fiancé. Lila looks irritated. She knows, of course, that Pietro arranged the meeting and that the Airota name carried it, but she doesn't want Enzo to have to be aware of that. Elena reads the discomfort as protectiveness — a debt that between Elena and Lila has no weight could weigh on Enzo as gratitude, as subordination. Elena hurries to say her father-in-law's prestige counts for little, that the expert was clear with her too: he'd only help Enzo if Enzo was good. Lila punctuates with a slightly overdone *«lui è bravissimo»* — he's very, very good.

Then Enzo opens up about what most struck the expert: not the Zurich correspondence-course exercises but the practical *schemi* — schemas — Lila has been making him work on. The diagrams of an everyday task broken into elementary steps. *«Specialmente lo schema di cose tipo stirare, tipo piantare un chiodo»* — especially the schema for things like ironing, like driving a nail. From that moment Lila and Enzo fall into a private joking back-and-forth in a vocabulary Elena can't follow, and Elena suddenly sees them as a happy couple with a secret so secret that even they don't quite know it. She thinks back to the *cortile*, to Enzo and Lila duelling over arithmetic in front of the *direttore* and the *Olivieri*; to the time he, who never cried, wept because he'd hurt her with a stone. *Il loro modo di stare insieme viene dal rione migliore* — their way of being together comes from the best part of the neighbourhood. Maybe Lila is right to want to go back.
