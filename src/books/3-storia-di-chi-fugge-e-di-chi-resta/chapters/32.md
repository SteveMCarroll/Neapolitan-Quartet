## Summary

A few days later the consequences arrive. Lila gets to the gate already
unwell — sore throat, low-grade flu — and finds a couple of teenage
boys handing out not the usual flyer but a multipage *ciclostilato*
(mimeographed pamphlet). She recognizes one of them from the committee
meeting on via dei Tribunali. She pockets the pamphlet without reading
it and walks past Filippo, the guard, without so much as a hello.

At lunchtime in the courtyard, Filippo — sentimental about his sixth
baby one day, vicious the next — corners her with the same pamphlet
already balled in his fist. He growls in dialect: *Cerù, senti bene*
("Cerù, listen carefully"): if you told *'sti quatte strunz* — these
four little shits — what's written here, you have walked into serious
trouble. He flings it in her face. The title is grandly bureaucratic:
**Inchiesta sulla condizione operaia a Napoli e provincia** ("Inquiry
into worker conditions in Naples and province"). A whole page is
devoted to the Soccavo plant — every word she said in Nadia's room,
typed out clean.

She finishes her sandwich, drops the pamphlet on the ground, walks back
in without looking at the guard's booth. But she is *furibonda*. The
sanctimonious Nadia, she's sure, wrote it — all earnest and tidy and
soaked in feeling. As Lila works the cold meat, the hostility of the
other workers thickens around her. They have all been complicit
victims for years; they don't doubt for a second who *fatto la spia*
(played the snitch).

In the afternoon Bruno summons her, red in the face. *Sei stata tu?*
"Was it you?" No. *Dimmi la verità.* "Tell me the truth." No. Bruno
runs through his grievances: he gave her work, he kept quiet when she
joined the CGIL, he apologized for *what happened* in the curing room
— and now this? She reminds him that she works hard and is paid
*quattro soldi* — peanuts — and that the favor runs the other way. He
warns her: if her "friends" come back to the gate, he'll have them
beaten. As for her — *tira ancora la corda e vedrai che si spezza*
("pull the rope any more and you'll see it snap").

At clock-out, the red light of the *parziale* flicks on as Lila passes
— a thing that usually picks off the shy girls Filippo wants to grope.
This time it is only her. The wind is sharp. Lila tells him flatly:
if you so much as touch me I'll kill you, or have you killed. Filippo
points her to the little bar table beside the booth: empty your
pockets, one at a time. From her coat Lila pulls out — *con disgusto*
— a fresh sausage, soft meat tight in its casing. She bursts out
laughing: *che gente di merda siete, tutti quanti* — "what shitheads
you all are, every last one of you."
