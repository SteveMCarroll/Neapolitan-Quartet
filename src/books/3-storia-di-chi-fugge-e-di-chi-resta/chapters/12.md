## Summary

The string of rione encounters has planted a doubt: maybe the book is selling because both the hostile and the friendly reviews keep flagging the *pagine scabrose* — the racy pages. Elena even wonders whether Nino, back in the bookshop, brought up Lila's sex life only because he assumed a woman who writes what she writes can take that kind of talk. With that thought comes the urge to see Lila again. She pictures her after the factory shift — Enzo in one room alone, Lila with the child in another — exhausted but bent over the novel, *la fronte corrugata* (forehead furrowed), the way she always concentrated. Would she reduce it to the dirty pages too? Or does she even have money to buy a copy? Elena thinks of bringing her one as a gift, then drops the idea. She still cares about Lila more than anyone, but their last meeting — Lila in an apron over her coat in the factory courtyard, burning the *Fata blu* in a bonfire — felt like a final goodbye to the leftovers of childhood. She walks on.

Whatever the reason, the book keeps climbing. **Adele** calls in her usual ironic affectionate way: keep this up and you'll be rich, and you won't know what to do with poor Pietro. Then she passes the phone to her husband. *Guido vuole parlarti* — Guido wants to speak with you. Elena tenses; she's barely exchanged a word with **professor Airota**. But he is warm, congratulates her, mocks her detractors' prudishness, talks about Italy's endless medieval hangover, praises her contribution to dragging the country forward. He says nothing specific about the novel — clearly he hasn't read it, he's a very busy man — but it's a generous gesture of approval.

**Mariarosa** is no less affectionate. She launches into praise, then suddenly veers off topic: she wants Elena at the Statale in Milan to take part in what she calls *"l'inarrestabile fluire degli eventi"* — the unstoppable flow of events. Have you seen what's happening in France? Elena has — she lives glued to a greasy little blue radio her mother keeps in the kitchen. Yes, she says, it's magnificent, Nanterre, the barricades in the Quartier Latin. But Mariarosa knows more, is more involved; she's planning to drive up to Paris with comrades and invites Elena along. The fantasy is gorgeous: drive to Milan, cross into France, plunge into the most incandescent magma of the moment, brave the police, pick up where the trip to France with Franco left off. And — quietly thrilling — among Mariarosa's comrades there might be **Nino**. *Ah, incontrarlo, essere travolta da quell'avventura* — to meet him, to be swept up in it with him. The kitchen is silent now, her parents asleep, her brothers still out, Elisa locked in the bathroom. *Partire, domani mattina.* Leave, tomorrow morning.
