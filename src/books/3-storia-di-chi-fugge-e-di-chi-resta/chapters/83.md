## Summary

Enzo wants to drive straight back, but Pietro insists he stay the
night. After dinner Elena tries to get Gennaro to play with Dede, who
is willing; the boy refuses with one decisive shake of the head. What
strikes Elena is the care Enzo takes of this child who isn't his —
he knows the routines, the *abitudini* and *gusti*, gets Gennaro to
pee, brush his teeth, and, when the boy collapses asleep, undresses
him and puts him in his pyjamas with real tenderness.

While Elena does the dishes Pietro keeps the guest company. They have
nothing in common. They try politics: Pietro speaks approvingly of
the *progressivo avvicinamento dei comunisti ai democristiani* (the
PCI's drift toward the Christian Democrats — what will become the
*compromesso storico*), Enzo answers that if Berlinguer's strategy
wins he'll be helping the worst enemies of the working class. They
let it drop. Pietro, gracious, asks about his work, and Enzo
launches into a long, slightly too technical monologue. IBM has just
moved him and Lila up to a bigger factory near Nola, three hundred
workers, forty office staff. *Trecentocinquantamila lire al mese* to
him as *capocentro*, *centomila* to her as his assistant. They are
responsible for a *Sistema 3 modello 10* with two operators and
five *perforatrici* (punch-card punchers). The job is to encode
everything the factory does — accounting, wages, invoicing,
inventory, orders, shipping — into thousands of punched cards. The
holes are everything; the labour all flows into them.

Pietro listens like a disciplined student, occasionally asking pointed
questions — *i buchi contano, ma contano anche le parti non
bucate?* (do the holes count, but do the unpunched parts count too?).
Elena half-smiles as she dries dishes and gets lost: among the codes,
the bricks of cards, the *selezionatrici* (sorters), the *lettori*
(readers, here meaning machines that read holes, not people), and
above all a new word — *file*, which Enzo pronounces as the plural of
*fila* (queue) but treats as a mysterious masculine singular: *il file
di questo, il file di quello.* What undoes her isn't the technology
but the picture of Lila in the middle of it: knowing every word,
every machine, every operation, paid badly, with a husband who
proudly says *senza di lei non ce la potrei fare* (without her I
couldn't do it) — while her own husband never praises her, *mi
riduceva a madre dei suoi figli*, requires that she prove her
nullity to be loved.

She finally sits down, sullen because neither man has offered to help
clear. She interrupts only to be present: *Lui può sbagliare?* — can
he (the computer) make mistakes? Enzo: *non c'è nessun lui, Lenù,
lui sono io. Se sbaglia, ho sbagliato io.* Pietro, ready to call it
a night, lobs his usual concern: machines like these will replace
people; whole skills will vanish — at Fiat the welds are already
done by robots. Enzo first agrees, then reconsiders, then turns to
the only authority he recognizes: *Lina dice che è un bene: i lavori
umilianti e quelli che rimbecilliscono devono sparire* — Lina says
it's good, the humiliating jobs and the ones that turn you into an
idiot ought to disappear.

*Lina, Lina, Lina.* Elena needles him: if Lina is so brilliant, why
do they pay you 350,000 and her 100,000, why are you *capocentro*
and she the assistant? Enzo hesitates, on the edge of saying
something urgent, then retreats: *bisogna abolire la proprietà
privata dei mezzi di produzione* (private ownership of the means of
production has to be abolished). For a few seconds only the fridge
hums. Pietro stands. *Andiamo a dormire.*
