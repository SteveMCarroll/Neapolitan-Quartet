## Summary

Two wedding invitations arrive at Elena's flat — not at her parents' — addressed formally to *egregia dottoressa Greco Elena*. Gigliola Spagnuolo is marrying Michele Solara. Marisa Sarratore is marrying Alfonso Carracci. The pair of envelopes gives Elena an idea: rather than just RSVP, she'll use them as a pretext to take a sounding of the *rione* — to find out whether the Solaras and the Carraccis still bear Lila a grudge worth fearing. Alfonso, she thinks, is the only person who'll tell her honestly how much of Stefano's anger toward Lila is still alive. And Michele, however much she detests him — perhaps *because* she detests him — needs to be told, calmly, that Lila is unwell and that Elena now has the standing to complicate his business if he keeps tormenting her. She tucks both cards into her bag, away from her mother, and gives herself a whole day for the round.

Weather threatening. She takes her umbrella and starts, like a diligent schoolgirl, with the hardest visit first: Michele. He isn't at the bar; he isn't at his mother's flat either — the staff there say try the new shop on the *stradone*. She drops in like a woman with nowhere particular to be. The old grotto of don Carlo, where as a child she used to buy liquid soap, has been completely erased. A vast vertical sign hangs from the third-floor windows down over a wide entrance: *Tutto per tutti* — everything for everyone. Inside, lit up in daylight, is the triumph of abundance. Behind the counter — fat now, cold to her — is Rino, Lila's brother. He says the boss in there is *him*. He knows nothing about the Solaras. *Se cerchi Michele va' a casa sua* — if you want Michele, go to his house — and he turns his back.

So Elena climbs to the *rione nuovo*, to the enormous flat the whole Solara family bought years ago. Manuela the *strozzina* — the loan-shark mother — opens after a long study through the peephole. Elena hasn't seen her since Lila's wedding. She has dried up. Skin tight on the big bones; one pupil bright, the other almost extinguished. Gold at her ears and throat as if she's headed to a party, on a dark dress that hangs on her. She is courteous, would Elena come in, take a coffee. Michele isn't there. He has another place now — at Posillipo — where he and Gigliola will live for good after the wedding. They're furnishing it together.

*«Lasceranno il rione?»* — they're leaving the neighbourhood? *«E certo»* — of course. *«Per Posillipo?»* Six rooms, three with the sea straight in front; Manuela had pushed for Vomero, but Michele wanted his way. The mornings up there, she says, the light — *non ti puoi immaginare*. Elena is surprised. She'd never have thought a Solara would step away from the piazza where the family does business, from *la tana dove nascondevano il bottino* — the den where they hide the loot. So Michele, of all of them — the sharpest, the most rapacious — is moving high above the city. *La mania di grandezza dei due fratelli era cresciuta davvero* — the brothers' delusions of grandeur really have grown. The lawyer was right. But for a moment it cheers her: Michele leaving the *rione* clears the field for Lila's possible return.
