## Summary

Elena thanks Adele and Tarratano for everything; both go on praising Nino as if she had helped grow him into such a charming, brilliant young man. Pietro is silent. Elena tells him sharply that he doesn't need to walk her back — he can go with his mother. Nobody hears that she means it.

On the walk back she is impossible. She announces she doesn't like Florence (untrue); that she doesn't want to write any more, she wants to teach (untrue); that she's tired and sleepy (untrue). When Pietro mentions, without preamble, that he wants to come and meet her parents, she snaps: *«sei pazzo… tu non sei adatto a loro e loro non sono adatti a te»* — "are you crazy… you're not right for them and they're not right for you." He asks, frightened: *"Don't you want to marry me any more?"* She nearly says yes — *I don't* — but stops, because that isn't true either; murmurs an apology, says she's depressed, of course she wants to marry him, and takes his hand.

Even as she holds his hand, however, she knows clearly: *if Pietro had not turned up at the restaurant tonight, she would have tried to take Nino.* She would have happily betrayed Pietro for one night, never telling him; maybe one day, very old, she'd have told Lila. But she also knows Nino is built from childhood fantasies — he wouldn't have lasted. Pietro is *adesso*, now, *una pietra di confine* — a boundary stone. He marks out a new country for her, governed by the Airotas' rules: a civil culture of principle. They will marry civilly, not in church; their eventual children will not be baptised; even on sex Pietro is wary and decent. He despises smut, gossip, parties, waste; he has never spoken about other women with other men. He won't fail his commitments to her, won't betray her.

So she pushes the thought of Nino down, takes Pietro's arm, and pours out a small avalanche of practical wants: *let's marry soon, I want to leave home, I want a driving licence, a phone, a television, I've never had anything.* He lights up. At the hotel door he asks, hoarsely, if he can come up. This is the night's last surprise: she has always wanted to make love with him, and he has always pulled back; but tonight, after the bookshop and Nino, she can't. *«Abbiamo aspettato tanto, possiamo aspettare ancora»* — "We've waited a long time; we can wait a little longer." She kisses him in a dark corner and watches him walk away down corso Garibaldi, turning back to wave shyly. His clumsy gait, his flat feet, the high tangle of his hair, soften her.
