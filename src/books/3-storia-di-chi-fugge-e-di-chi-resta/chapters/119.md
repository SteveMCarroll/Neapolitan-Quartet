## Summary

She stands by the telephone she doesn't know how long. She is
*carica d'odio* — charged with hatred, with only sentences like
this in her head: yes, come, come right now, *stronza*, that's all
I'm waiting for — where the hell are you from, via Tasso, via
Filangieri, via Crispi, della Santarella — and you want to come
at *me*, *'sta cessa, 'sta loffa* — this dump, this rotten thing —
*non sai con chi hai a che fare, mappìna* (you don't know who
you're dealing with, you rag of a woman). Another Elena, buried
under the crust of mildness, struggles up in her chest, mixing
Italian and the voices of childhood; *ero tutta un clamore* —
she is all clamour. If Eleonora dares show up at her door she'll
spit in her face, throw her down the stairs, drag her by the hair
into the street and smash her *testa piena di merda* (head full of
shit) on the pavement.

Her chest hurts; her temples throb. Building work has started
under the window: heat comes in, and a thick chiselling, and dust,
and an annoying noise of some machine. Dede and Elsa are
quarrelling in the next room: *non devi fare tutto quello che
faccio io, sei una scimmia, solo le scimmie fanno così* — you
mustn't do everything I do; you're a monkey, only monkeys do
that.

Slowly she understands.

Nino has decided to talk to his wife. *E lei mi aveva aggredita
per questo* — and Eleonora has attacked her for that reason. The
rage flips into uncontainable joy. Nino wants her so much that he
has told his wife about them. He has ruined his marriage; he has
knowingly thrown away the comforts that came with it; he has
unbalanced his whole life choosing to make Eleonora and Albertino
suffer rather than Elena. *Dunque era vero, mi amava* — so it was
true: he loved her. She *sospirai di contentezza* — sighed with
contentment.

The phone rings again. She picks up at once.

It is Nino, his voice, sounding calm. He says his marriage is
over, he is free. He asks:

*Hai parlato con Pietro?* — Have you spoken to Pietro?

*Ho cominciato* — I've started. — You haven't told him yet? — Yes
and no. — Do you want to back out? — No. — Then hurry, we have to
leave.

He has already taken for granted that she'll go with him. They'll
meet in Rome; everything is ready, hotel, plane tickets.

*Ho il problema delle bambine*, she says, but quietly, with no
conviction — I have the problem of the girls. *Mandale da tua
madre* — send them to your mother. Out of the question. *Allora
portale con te* — then bring them with you.

*Dici sul serio?* — Are you serious? Yes. *Mi porteresti con te
comunque, anche con le mie figlie?* — You'd take me anyway, even
with my daughters? Of course.

*Tu mi ami davvero*, she murmurs — you really love me.

*Sì.*
