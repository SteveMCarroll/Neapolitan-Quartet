## Summary

One Saturday, in the press flood about the fascist bomb on the
*Italicus* (the train bombed at San Benedetto Val di Sambro on 4
August 1974), Pietro spots a short item in the *Corriere della
Sera* about a small factory on the outskirts of Naples. *Non si
chiamava Soccavo l'azienda dove lavorava la tua amica?* — Wasn't
that the factory where your friend used to work?

A *commando* of two men and a woman has broken into a salumificio
on the outskirts of Naples. They first shot the watchman, Filippo
Cara, in the legs — he's in critical condition — then went up to
the owner's office and killed Bruno Soccavo with four pistol shots:
three in the chest, one in the head. Reading, Elena sees Bruno's
face break apart along with his very white teeth. *Oh Dio Dio.* She
leaves the children with Pietro and runs to phone Lila. The phone
rings and rings.

She gets through the next day. Lila is alarmed: *che c'è, Gennaro
non sta bene?* No — Bruno is dead. Lila knew nothing, listens, and
murmurs at the end, without inflection: *mi hai dato proprio una
brutta notizia.* Nothing else. Elena pushes: phone someone, find
out where she can send a *telegramma di condoglianze*. Lila says
she has no contacts at the factory anymore. *E poi che telegramma,
lascia stare.*

She lets it go. But the next day she finds an article in *il
manifesto* signed *Giovanni Sarratore* — Nino. It gives a lot of
information about small industries in Campania, stresses the
political tensions in those backward realities, and remembers Bruno
with real affection and his tragic end. Elena follows the story for
days; it slides off the pages fast. Lila will not talk about it
anymore. When Elena rings in the evening with the children, Lila
cuts her off: *passami Gennaro.* When Elena mentions Nino, the
edge in Lila's voice sharpens: *il solito vizio, deve sempre
mettere bocca: cosa c'entra la politica, saranno state altre
questioni, qua si muore ammazzati per mille motivi, corna,
malaffare, anche solo uno sguardo di troppo.*

So the days pass and what stays with Elena is a single image — not
the *padrone* she once threatened on the phone in the name of the
Airotas, but the boy who tried to kiss her, the one she pushed
roughly away.
