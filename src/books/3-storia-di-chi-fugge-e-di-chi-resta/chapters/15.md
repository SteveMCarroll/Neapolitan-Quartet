## Summary

Franco speaks at the assembly with all of his old gifts — warm voice, the trick of grounding a sweeping argument in something everyone has seen — and Elena catches up to him afterwards. He hugs her in surprise, but they can barely talk; someone yanks his arm, someone else is already accusing him of horrible things. Mariarosa finds her in the crush, delighted: *"cosa ci fai qui?"* — what are you doing here? When Elena says she knows Franco, Mariarosa whispers that they'll make her pay for inviting him — *guarda che vespaio* — look at the hornet's nest. Franco is staying at her place and leaving for Turin in the morning; she insists Elena come too.

The assembly drags on. They leave at dusk and head to a trattoria with the breastfeeding girl, **Silvia**, the cigar‑smoking man (a Venezuelan painter, **Juan**), and Franco. In conversation Elena realises her first impression was wrong — Franco is not unchanged. A mask of identical features has been laid over the old face but has erased its generosity. He's now contracted, guarded, weighing his words. He never refers to their old relationship; when she pushes, he cuts her off — *doveva andare così* — it had to go this way. He's vague about university and hasn't graduated. *"C'è altro da fare,"* he says. There's other work to do.
What? Mariarosa says brightly: *la rivoluzione* — the revolution. Elena tries irony: and in your spare time? Juan, gently shaking the baby's fist, replies serious: *nel tempo libero la si prepara* — in your spare time you prepare it.

They pile into Mariarosa's car and drive to her huge old apartment in Sant'Ambrogio. Juan has a studio there — a chaotic room with photo‑realistic crowded city scenes onto which he has hammered tubes of paint, brushes, palettes, jars for turpentine, rags, ruining them. Mariarosa praises the work, addressing Franco above all, whose judgment she clearly values. Elena spies on the household and can't decode it. Juan lives there; Silvia and her baby **mirko-sarratore** live there too. At first she assumes Juan and Silvia are a couple subletting; she changes her mind quickly. Juan treats Silvia with distracted courtesy and puts his arm around Mariarosa's shoulders all evening; at one point he kisses her on the neck.

Conversation circles Juan's work, then opens into Italian cultural backwardness, the post‑election political landscape, the cascading collapses of social democracy, students and police repression, and *la lezione della Francia* — the lesson from France. The exchange between the two men turns polemical. Silvia, who can't work out what mirko-sarratore needs and keeps coming back in to scold him as if he were older, tosses in fragments of dissent from the corridor or the changing room. Mariarosa recounts Sorbonne nursery experiments and a Paris early‑June chilled to a halt by general strike — second‑hand, from a friend's letter; she didn't manage to leave. Franco and Juan listen distractedly to both women and never lose their argumentative thread; they get more aggressive with each other. The result, Elena registers with annoyance, is that the three women are reduced to *"sonnolente giovenche"* — drowsy heifers waiting for the two bulls to test each other's strength.

She tries to wait for Mariarosa to break in so she can break in too; the men leave no space. mirko-sarratore shrieks; Silvia handles him harshly. Elena thinks: Lila was younger than this when Gennaro was born. Something has been linking the two of them in her head all evening — the loneliness Lila felt after Nino disappeared and Stefano blew up; their beauty; the thought that Lila in this hall would have been even more striking, even fiercer. But Lila is *tagliata fuori* — cut out, off the map; the wave Elena is feeling here will reach San Giovanni a Teduccio and Lila, sunk where she is, won't even notice. Elena feels guilty. She hears, almost like Lila's voice inside her: if you sit there silently with these two talking, at least help the girl, think what it is to have a small baby. She gets up, takes mirko-sarratore from Silvia gently, and goes.
