## Summary

September and October pass. Elena talks to almost no one. Adele is
buried in work. Mariarosa has taken Franco in — Franco *invalido*,
in deep depression — and keeps promising to put him on the phone
but never does. Pietro is more and more silent. The outside world,
beyond his books, *gli pesava sempre più*. He hates the regulated
chaos of the university, calls in sick often. He says it's for
study, but he can't get to the bottom of his book, and as if to
forgive himself and be forgiven, he takes over Elsa, cooks,
sweeps, washes, irons. Elena has to be hard with him to push him
back into the faculty, then immediately regrets it. Now that the
violence has touched people she knows, she fears for him. He has
never stopped opposing publicly what he likes to call the
*sciocchezzaio* (collection of stupidities) of his students and
many colleagues. Out of worry — or perhaps because of it — she
never tells him he's right; she hopes that criticism will *fargli
ravvedere* him, make him drop his *riformismo reazionario* (her
formula); but to him she sounds like another voice in the chorus
of his attackers.

It isn't that simple. On the one hand she wants to protect him;
on the other she feels she is taking Lila's side — defending the
choices she secretly attributes to Lila. She thinks of calling Lila
and starting from Pietro and their fights, working her way around
to making Lila confess. She doesn't, of course — sincerity by
telephone on such topics is ridiculous. Then one evening Lila
herself calls, exhilarated.

*Ti devo dare una bella notizia. — Che succede? — Sono capocentro.*
Head of the data-processing centre — *del centro meccanografico
ibm che Michele ha noleggiato.* It seems impossible. Elena asks
her to repeat, to explain. Lila has accepted Solara's offer? After
all the resistance, is she back working for him as in the *piazza
dei Martiri* days? Yes, gleefully, ever more open: Michele has
handed over the *Sistema 3* he's rented and installed it in a
shoe warehouse in Acerra; Lila will have operators and punch-card
women under her; the salary is *quattrocentoventicinquemila lire
mensili.*

Elena is stunned. The image of the guerrillera evaporates in an
instant; everything she thinks she knows about Lila wobbles. *È
l'ultima cosa che mi sarei aspettata da te. — Che dovevo fare? —
Rifiutare. — Perché? — I Solara sappiamo cosa sono. — E con
questo? È già successo, e sotto Michele sono stata meglio che
sotto quello stronzo di Soccavo.*

*Non mi piace questo tono, Lenù. Sono pagata più di Enzo che è
maschio: cosa c'è che non va? — Niente. — La rivoluzione, gli
operai, il mondo nuovo e le altre cazzate?* Elena snaps: stop —
if you want a real conversation I'm in; otherwise let it go.

Lila, sharp: can I point something out? You always say *vero* and
*veramente*, whether speaking or writing; or *all'improvviso.* But
when does anyone really say things, and when do things ever
happen *all'improvviso*? You know better than anyone it's all a
tangle, one thing leading to another and another. *Io non faccio
più niente veramente, Lenù. E alle cose ho imparato a starci
attenta, solo i cretini credono che succedono all'improvviso.*

Elena, brittle: brava. So you have it all under control, you're
using Michele and not the other way around? Let's drop it, ciao.
Lila: no, speak, *parla, di' quello che devi dire.* Elena: I have
nothing to say. Lila: *parla se no parlo io.* Elena: go ahead,
let's hear it.

*Critichi me, mentre a tua sorella non dici niente?* Elena, falling
from the clouds: what does my sister have to do with this? *Non sai
niente di Elisa? — Che devo sapere?* Lila laughs nastily and tells
her to ask her mother, her father, her brothers.
