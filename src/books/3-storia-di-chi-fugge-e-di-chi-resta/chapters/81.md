## Summary

The "underground war" Elena has been reading about in the papers — *disegni
golpisti* (coup plots), police repression, armed bands, shootings, bombs,
massacres — reaches her again through a phone call. Carmen has not heard
from Pasquale in weeks. He had asked her for Elena's number and address
weeks earlier, saying he wanted to ask her for *un consiglio* (advice),
but no call ever came. Carmen has phoned everyone: Lila, Enzo, the
collective in via dei Tribunali, finally the Galiani house, where
Armando told her only that Nadia had moved out and left no address.

Elena tries the simplest explanation — Pasquale and Nadia must have run
off together, maybe to Germany, England, France. Carmen does not believe
it. Pasquale, she says, is too attached a brother to disappear like
that. She has a *bruttissimo presentimento*. The clashes in the rione
are now daily, the fascists have threatened her and her husband, and
Pasquale has been blamed for setting fire to both the MSI section and
the Solara supermarket — *gli scontri al rione sono ormai quotidiani*.
She fears Gino had him killed. The police, when she went to them,
nearly arrested *her*: *sono più fascisti dei fascisti.*

Elena calls Professoressa Galiani, who is icily mocking — Elena hasn't
been seen in bookshops or on the page in ages, has she retired? — and
hangs up when asked for a number for Armando. After some hunting Elena
reaches Armando himself, who is lonely and overflowing: his marriage
has ended, his wife took the child, the hospital eats him. About Nadia
he closes up: *non ho più nessun rapporto con lei*. Political
divergences, he says, *divergenze su tutto* — ever since she got
together with Pasquale, *non ci si ragionava più*. Are they living
together? *Diciamo così.* He pivots away to the *strage di Brescia*,
to the *padroni* funding the parties and the fascists when things
turn sour.

Elena calls Carmen back to reassure: it's love, Pasquale followed
Nadia like a puppy. Carmen is skeptical; Elena, embarrassed,
embellishes the afternoon she spent with the two of them. But in
mid-June Carmen calls again, frantic. Gino has been shot in the face
in broad daylight in front of his father's pharmacy. The carabinieri
have torn Carmen's apartment apart from top to bottom, even the
benzinaio's pump, looking for any trace that could lead them to
Pasquale. It is worse, she says, than the day they came to take her
father away for the murder of don Achille.
