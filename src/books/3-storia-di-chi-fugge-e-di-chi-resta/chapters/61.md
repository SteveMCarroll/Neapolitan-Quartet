## Summary

The night before leaving Naples for Florence, Elena can't sleep. The
thought that keeps cutting back at her is Pasquale's accusation in
Galiani's apartment: that she used her bourgeois connections to settle
the Soccavo affair as a favor to a friend. She has been throwing the
criticism off all evening and now, in the dark, she can't anymore. If
Lila sided with Pasquale, maybe Pasquale was right.

For the first time in her life she gets up at four in the morning and
walks out of the house alone. She wants something bad to happen to her,
some accident that would punish her — and, by deflection, punish Lila.
Nothing happens. The streets that frighten her when they're crowded are
safe and empty. She walks all the way to the sea. The sky goes
*violaceo* ("violet"); Castel dell'Ovo is split by the light into a
glowing ochre half on the Vesuvius side and a brown shadow on the
Mergellina side. There's no sound from the water, only its heavy smell.

For a few pages she lets herself ask the big questions. What is she
doing? Trying to change where she was born, change herself and the
people around her? Repopulate this empty city with people free of
*miseria* and *avidità* ("poverty" and "greed")? She pulled on the
Airota name, on generations of socialist work, not to fix the world but
to help one person she loved. Was that wrong? Should she have left Lila
in the mess? She lands on a flat decision: *mai più, mai più avrei
mosso un dito per nessuno* — never again would she lift a finger for
anyone. Then she goes to get married.
