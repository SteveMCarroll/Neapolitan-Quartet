## Summary

Mariarosa comes to Florence to present a colleague's book on Piero della
Francesca's *Madonna del Parto*. Pietro swears he'll be there, then ducks out
at the last minute. Mariarosa arrives alone, by car, a little worn but as
warm as ever, with gifts for Dede and Elsa. She never mentions Elena's failed
novel — though Adele has surely told her — and free-associates about travel
and books with her usual energy. The presentation looks set to stay inside
the academic rails, until Mariarosa swerves into a string of provocations,
some of them coarse: *non bisogna dar figli a nessun padre, men che mai a Dio
Padre* — no child should be given to any father, least of all to God the
Father; children must be given to themselves; it is time to study as women,
not as men; behind every discipline is *il cazzo* — the cock — and when the
cock feels impotent it reaches for the *spranga*, the police, prisons, the
army, the camps; bend and you're spared, push and they massacre you.
Discontented murmurs, applause; afterwards a crowd of women surrounds her.
A small, mixed group of Florentine women invites Elena to come every week
to one of their houses, to talk — they tell her — *di noi*, about
ourselves.

Mariarosa's salvos and the women's invitation push Elena to fish out from
under a pile of books the two pamphlets Adele once gave her. She carries them
around in her bag and reads outdoors under a gray late-winter sky. The first
one's title catches her eye: *Sputiamo su Hegel* — "Let's Spit on Hegel," by
Carla Lonzi. While Elsa naps in the pram and Dede mutters to her doll, she
reads. Every sentence strikes her, above all *la sfrontata libertà di
pensiero* — the brazen freedom of thought. She underlines hard, marks
exclamation points, vertical strokes.

The list she pulls from those pages: *sputare* — spit — on Hegel; on men's
culture; on Marx, Engels, Lenin; on historical materialism; on Freud; on
psychoanalysis and *l'invidia del pene* — penis envy; on marriage, on
family; on Nazism, Stalinism, terrorism, war; on class struggle, dictatorship
of the proletariat, socialism, communism; on the trap of equality; on every
form and organ of patriarchal culture. *Deculturalizzarsi.
Disacculturarsi* — deculturate yourselves, unlearn the culture — starting
from motherhood; *non dare figli a nessuno* — give children to no one. Tear
the inferiority out of the brain. While men go off to space, life for women
on this planet has yet to begin. The woman is *il Soggetto Imprevisto* —
the Unforeseen Subject.

Elena turns the pamphlet over and over. *Com'è possibile che una donna sappia
pensare così?* How can a woman think like this? She has labored over books,
she realizes, but she has suffered them rather than used them — never turned
them against themselves. *Ecco come si pensa contro.* This is how you think
against. After all that work, she doesn't know how to think. Not even
Mariarosa knows: she reads and reshuffles, full of flourishes. Lila knows.
It's her nature. If she had studied she would have learned to think like
this.

That conviction sticks. Every book she reads in this period bends, sooner or
later, toward Lila. She begins joining Mariarosa's group, dragging the
children with her — *quando ce ne andiamo?* / Elsa whooping. Most of the
discussions bore her: a clumsy restatement of things she already knows. She
isn't going to lay her marriage out in public. But the explicit, sometimes
unpleasant *urgenza di autenticità* — drive toward authenticity — seduces her.
She wants the same thing with Lila: to look at the two of them with the same
hardness, *dirci fino in fondo ciò che ci tacevamo* — say to each other all
the way down what they keep silent.

She tries on the phone. Disaster. She tells Lila about the books she's
reading, about the women's group. Lila lets her run, then laughs at titles
like *La donna clitoridea e la donna vaginale* — "The Clitoral Woman and the
Vaginal Woman" — and gets aggressively crude: *che cazzo dici, Lenù, il
piacere, la fessa, qua i problemi sono già tanti, sei pazza* — what the fuck
are you talking about, Lenù, pleasure, cunt, we've got plenty of real problems
here, are you crazy. She means it as proof she has no instrument for this
talk. Then dismissive: *fa' le cose belle che devi fare, non buttare il
tempo* — do the fine things you have to do, don't waste your time.

Elena lets it go and turns the questions on herself. She has overdone it,
she decides. She has tried to make herself male — *darmi capacità
maschili* — to be at the level of male reason, *la più irragionevole* — the
most unreasonable. All those memorized fashionable phrases, wasted effort.
*A quali patti segreti con me stessa avevo acconsentito, pur di eccellere?*
What secret bargains with myself have I agreed to in order to excel? And
she has been so close to Lila that she has been forced to imagine herself as
she isn't. *Non un'idea, senza Lila. Non un pensiero di cui mi fidassi.*
She must accept herself outside her — *accettare che ero una persona media*,
accept that she is an average person.
