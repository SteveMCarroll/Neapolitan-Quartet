## Summary

Lila wakes with a fever, takes an aspirin, goes to work anyway. The
predawn light is bluish; the ground is slush. At the gate the students
have multiplied — the two boys from yesterday plus a third their age
and a fourth, *grosso, decisamente obeso*, a heavy-set twenty-year-old.
They're pasting up posters calling for *lotta* (struggle) and handing
out leaflets. But where yesterday workers had taken the paper out of
curiosity or politeness, today they shove past or crumple it on the
spot.

The youngest boy comes running with a friendly *tutto a posto,
compagna?* ("all good, comrade?"). His name is Dario. Lila bites his
head off: *Lo so chi cazzo sei, ma non voglio avere a che fare né con
te né con gli amici tuoi* — "I know damn well who you are, but I want
nothing to do with you or your friends." She sees his bewilderment and
thinks she should explain — why the committee speech, why the
pamphlet was an outrage, why these kids standing in the cold handing
fine-print sheets to people who can barely read them is useless and
stupid — but she has the fever and no patience.

At the gate Filippo is screaming at the heavy student: *strunz*, cross
that line and I'll shoot you. The student is laughing back, *spara*,
shoot, this isn't private property, this belongs to the people. Lila
walks past both of them and tells Filippo, deadpan: *accontentalo* —
"give him what he wants" — anyone who could be sleeping or studying and
instead stands here being a pain in the ass deserves to be shot.
Filippo gapes; the student turns on her — *va', entra, va' a baciare il
culo al padrone* ("go on, go inside, go kiss the boss's ass") — and
backs off to keep distributing.

Two cars come screeching up the dirt track: one grey, one blue. Men
pile out and start ripping the just-pasted posters off the wall. *Si
mette male* — this is going bad — and Lila instinctively doubles back
to look. At the wheel of the grey car: **Gino** — the pharmacist's son
from the rione, *tutto muscoli*, all muscle now. He climbs out
brandishing a *bastone* (cudgel). Seven or eight more emerge with
chains and iron bars. Fascists, mostly from the rione, some Lila
recognizes by name. She's hated them since she was a girl old enough
to picture their *porcherie* (sordid stuff): don Achille, Stefano
once he showed his hand, the Solaras (grandfather, father, grandsons),
even when they were posing as monarchists or Christian Democrats by
turn. The rione, by majority, loves them.

Dario charges over to protest the torn posters, hands still full of
flyers — *buttali, cretino* (drop them, idiot), Lila thinks, but he
doesn't. One of the fascists punches him flat on his back in a flurry
of falling leaflets and keeps hitting him on the ground. The big
student rushes in bare-handed and gets a chain across one arm. He
manages to seize the chain and they wrestle for it, screaming. Then
Gino comes from behind and clubs him down.

Lila forgets the fever and runs toward the gate without a clear aim —
maybe to see, maybe to help, maybe just because *le botte non le
facevano paura* (blows have never scared her) and her anger spikes. She
has to dodge a knot of workers fleeing through the gate, including
**Edo** from her bone-stripping team, bleeding from one hand and
muttering that he's going to fetch *l'accetta* (the hatchet) and then
they'll see. A woman called Isa screams at Filippo: *intervieni, fa'
qualcosa, chiama le guardie* (do something, call the police). By the
time Lila reaches the road, the blue car has already pulled away and
Gino is climbing into the grey one — but he sees her, freezes
astonished: *Lina, tu qua sei finita?* "Lina, this is where you ended
up?" His friends drag him in. The car starts, and out the window comes
the parting shot: *facevi la signora, stronza, e guarda che cazzo sei
diventata* — "you played the lady, bitch, and look what the hell you've
become."
