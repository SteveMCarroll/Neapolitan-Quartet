## Summary

What a well‑made child. *mirko-sarratore* — the rose‑pink folds at his wrists and thighs, the long delicate feet, the good smell — seduces her instantly. She walks him through the house whispering compliments and the men's voices recede, along with their ideas and their animosity. Something new happens: pleasure. *Una vampa incontrollabile* — an uncontrollable rush of heat — the baby's warmth and motion, every sense sharpened to the point of ache, the sweetness and responsibility of this perfect fragment of life in her arms, the urge to shield him from every shadow lurking in the dark corners of the apartment. mirko-sarratore quiets. That brings its own kick of pride: she has been able to give him peace.

When she comes back into the room, Silvia — perched on Mariarosa's knees, jumping into the men's argument with brittle exclamations — sees her face and reads the pleasure with which she's holding the baby. Silvia is on her feet at once and takes him back with a sharp *grazie aspro* — a bitter "thanks" — and carries him off to bed. The loss is sharp and disagreeable: the warmth of mirko-sarratore leaving her, a sudden dip in mood, confused thoughts. She wants him back; she half‑hopes he'll start crying again, that Silvia will need her. *Che cosa mi prende?* — what's getting into me? Do I want children? Do I want to be a mother, to nurse and rock? *Matrimonio più gravidanza?* — marriage plus pregnancy? And if her own mother climbs back out of her belly just when she thinks she is finally safe?
