## Summary

A short, stand-alone aside in Elena's older narrating voice. She steps
out of the scene and tells the reader: this is perhaps *l'ultima volta*
— the last time — she'll narrate Lila in such detail. After this point
Lila will become *sempre più sfuggente* (more and more elusive), and
the material at Elena's disposal *si è impoverito* (has thinned). She
blames the divergence of their lives and the distance between them.
Even when they lived in other cities and almost never met — Lila
withholding news as usual, Elena forcing herself not to ask — Lila's
shadow kept goading her, depressing her, swelling her with pride,
deflating her, never letting her come to rest.

That *pungolo* (goad) is needed even more now, when she writes. She
wants Lila to be present; she writes for this. She wants her to delete,
to add, *che collabori alla nostra storia* — to collaborate on their
story — pouring in, on her own whim, the things she knows, has said,
has thought: the time she found herself face to face with Gino the
fascist; the time she met Nadia, Professoressa Galiani's daughter; the
time she went back to the house on corso Vittorio Emanuele where, years
earlier, she had felt out of place; the time she looked unsparingly at
her experience of sex.

Of her own embarrassment as she listened, of the pain, of the few
words she said to Lila during the long account — those, the narrator
notes, she'll consider later.

The chapter is the hinge: a frame around what follows, the long
flashback from Lila's point of view that will carry the next stretch
of the book.
