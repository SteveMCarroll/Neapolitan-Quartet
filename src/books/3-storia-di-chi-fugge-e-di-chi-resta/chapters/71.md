## Summary

Pregnant again, Elena begins phoning Lila almost daily — expensive
*interurbane* (long-distance calls) whose only real purpose is to crouch in her
friend's shadow, let the pregnancy pass, and hope that, by some old habit, Lila
will set her imagination going again. They both keep a careful watch on their
tongues. Elena cannot confess that a dark part of her still wishes Lila would
actually fall ill and die; Lila cannot say what really lies under the abrasive,
often offensive tone she uses with Elena. So they talk like normal mothers
boasting about normal children: gennaro-carracci is among the best in elementary school,
Dede can already read. Elena hints at her writing without dramatizing: *sto
lavorando, non è facile* — I'm working, it isn't easy.

When she tries to push the conversation toward men — does Lila want anyone
besides Enzo? — Lila shrugs it off. But if Elena so much as says Enzo's name,
Lila launches into a long, dazzled monologue about computers. Enzo has landed a
job at a small underwear factory fifty kilometers from Naples; the company has
rented an IBM machine and made him its *sistemista* — the systems man who turns
manual processes into flow diagrams. The central unit, Lila reports, is the
size of a three-door wardrobe; the memory is eight kilobytes; the heat is
ferocious; the whole thing smells of sweat. She walks Elena through ferrite
cores threaded with current, a ring as one *bit*, eight rings as one *byte* —
one character. Elena scribbles notes in case any of it might be useful for a
novel and answers a weak *sì* whenever Lila asks if it's clear. It isn't, and
Lila can tell, and Elena is embarrassed.

The enthusiasm builds call by call. Enzo now earns 148,000 lire a month — Lila
recites the figure twice, exact. He has made himself indispensable, and he has
talked the owner into hiring her as his assistant: she is back at work, and
this time she likes it. *Lui è il capo, Lenù, e io la sottocapo* — he's the
boss, I'm the deputy. She drops gennaro-carracci with her mother, or sometimes with
Stefano, and goes to the factory every morning. She and Enzo study the
operation step by step — counting entries, stamping invoices, checking
apprentices' booklets and time cards — then translate the work into diagrams
and punched cards. Lila is a *perforatrice* as well, sitting alongside three
other women; that brings her another eighty thousand lire. Together they earn
228,000. The owner has noticed her and wants to send her on a training course.
*Vedi che vita ho* — see what a life I have — *sei contenta?*
