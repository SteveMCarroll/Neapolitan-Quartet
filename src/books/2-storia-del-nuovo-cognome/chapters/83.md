## Summary

Elena arrives at the **Normale** all timidity and clumsiness. She
realises she speaks a *italiano libresco* that occasionally tips
into the ridiculous: when a word fails her she italianises a
Neapolitan one and powers on. She knows almost nothing of
*galateo*, *parlavo a voce molto alta, masticavo facendo rumore con
la bocca*, and has to relearn how to be in a room. To prove
sociable she interrupts conversations, pronounces on others' affairs,
gets too intimate too fast; then she pulls back, *gentile ma
distante.* A Roman classmate parodies her cadence and the room
laughs; Elena laughs too, even thickens her dialect, *come se mi
prendessi in giro allegramente da sola.*

The first weeks she fights the urge to flee. From within her
*consueta modestia mite* she begins, slowly, to please: students,
*bidelli*, professors, *all'apparenza senza sforzo. In realtà mi
industriai molto.* She tames her voice and gestures, learns the
written and unwritten codes, suppresses her Neapolitan accent. She
shows she's bright *senza assumere mai toni superbi, autoironizzando
sulla mia ignoranza, fingendomi sorpresa io stessa dei buoni
risultati.* She avoids enemies — when a girl is hostile she focuses
her *cortesia composta* on her until the hostility melts. She does
the same with professors, *con più cautela*, circling *i più
scostanti, i più austeri, con sorrisi sereni e un'aria devota.*
Inside a year she's a *studentessa promettente* who can return a
respectful nod with cordiality, *nessun professore mi diede mai
meno di trenta.*

Two real crises. First: the Roman girl one morning publicly accuses
her of stealing money from her purse. *Capii che non potevo reagire
con un sorriso accomodante.* Elena gives her *uno schiaffo
violentissimo* and a torrent of dialect insults. The Roman girl
ends up in the bathroom with a bleeding nose; hours later she
returns to apologise: *aveva ritrovato i suoi soldi*. Elena
embraces her. *Io ero cresciuta in un modo che, se pure avessi
sbagliato in qualcosa, non mi sarei mai scusata.* Second: the
inaugural Christmas ball, a debutante affair with the boys from
piazza dei Cavalieri. She has nothing to wear. *Il mio guardaroba
era composto da due vestiti invernali confezionati da mia madre…
da un cappotto consunto ereditato da una zia, da una grande sciarpa
blu che mi ero fatta io stessa, da un unico paio di scarpe con il
mezzo tacco, risuolate più volte.* After the Lila-clothes
experiences she won't borrow. She nearly skips it (*essere in
salute, morire dalla voglia di sembrare una Natascia al ballo con
il principe Andrej o con Kuragin*) but goes in her dark-blue dress,
a smear of lipstick, hair up. Her outfit, *probabilmente
umiliante*, generates *sensi di colpa che incoraggiavano la
solidarietà*; she dances all night.

That night she meets **Franco Mari** — *bruttino ma molto
divertente*, sharp, *sfrontato e spendaccione*, a year ahead,
*comunista militante critico verso le tendenze socialdemocratiche
del suo partito*, *di una famiglia molto agiata di Reggio Emilia*.
He spends on her: dresses, shoes, a new coat, *occhiali che mi
restituirono gli occhi*, books on *cultura politica*. He teaches
her Trotsky and the case against Stalinism — *in Urss non c'erano
né il socialismo né tantomeno il comunismo*. He takes her on her
first trip abroad, a young-communist convention in Paris, of which
she sees little — smoky rooms, more *neri* than she's ever seen,
the rude blare of police sirens. He drills her on study habits
(use the verb *studiare* even for sci-fi novels, *compilare schede
minutissime* for every text). She lets herself be *rieducata.* But
*non ce la feci a innamorarmi*. She loves him, his *corpo
irrequieto*, but never feels him *indispensabile*. When he fails an
exam and is sent away, the feeling burns off. They write for a
while; he tries to come back, fails another exam. *Per parecchio
non ne seppi più niente.*
