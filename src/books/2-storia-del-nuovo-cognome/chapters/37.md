## Summary

Elena makes her first real split from Lila. After the school year
(passes with all 8s, two 9s — the bet won), she takes a job in a
**bookshop on via Mezzocannone**, where a sixty-something owner
sends her up a ladder so he can look up her skirt. She doesn't even
tell Lila about the grades. The Galiani party turns out to have
been a real success: **Armando** turns up at Mezzocannone after
acing a med exam, fizzing about Latin America and guerrilla warfare,
asks for her phone number (she doesn't have one), asks her out
(she's busy). The next day **Nino** and **Nadia** arrive to invite
her to the cinema; she demurs again.

The Sunday after, Nino comes alone to the rione. He brings her a
copy of *Cronache meridionali*, the political journal her teacher
writes for, half-apologising for it: *è schematica, come la Galiani
e come Armando.* He delivers a small but important grown-up speech
— *Galiani is wonderful, but her flaw is she can't stand a head
that thinks differently from hers. Take everything from her, then
go your own way.* He hints, at the end, that Elena should come to
**Forio for Ferragosto** — *a big house, his friend's parents leave
them alone, Nadia might come for a few days too.*

Their goodbye is broken by **Melina** at her window, leaning out
and screaming a single word: *Donato! Donato!* She has mistaken
Nino for Sarratore the father. Nino, panicked, asks *do I look
like him? sicuro?*, then bolts. That night, in bed, Elena finds his
name in the journal's index — *Antonio Sarratore* — and reads his
article on **Programmazione**, twice, weeping. *Era un pezzo della
sua intelligenza, un pezzo dellasua persona, che senza vantarsi, in
sordina, aveva regalato a me. A me.* She decides not to tell Lila
about any of it. *Non sapeva apprezzarmi. Altri invece sapevano.*
