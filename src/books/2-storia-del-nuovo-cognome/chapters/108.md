## Summary

The day after the Airotas her gloom returns and sharpens. The afternoon has shown her that *la fatica della Normale era un abbaglio*: merit isn't enough, and what's needed beyond it she has neither nor knows how to learn. She is ashamed of her *affastellare parole agitate*, the flurry of references without rigour, calm or irony — the very things Mariarosa and Adele and Pietro slip into a discussion. She does have something — *l'accanimento metodico della ricercatrice che sottopone a verifica anche le virgole* — and that's what gets her good marks. But she is, she now sees, *una sprovveduta fin troppo acculturata*, without armour. *Il professor Airota era un dio immortale che aveva dato ai suoi figli armi fatate prima della battaglia. Mariarosa era invincibile. Pietro perfetto nella sua compitezza stracolta. Io? Io potevo solo restare accanto a loro, brillare grazie al loro fulgore*.

So she clings. She seeks Pietro out, attaches herself, learns to love him *for* the lack she discovered through him. She waits in vain for him to declare himself. One evening she kisses him on the cheek and at last he kisses her on the mouth. They meet in tucked‑away corners after dark, touch each other in the dimness — *lui non volle mai penetrarmi*. It is like being back with Antonio, with one decisive difference: she is going out at night *col figlio di Airota*, drawing strength from him.

Now and then she thinks of calling Lila from a public phone — to say she has a *nuovo fidanzato*, that the dissertations of both of them are almost certain to become *libri proprio come sono i libri veri, con la copertina, il titolo, il nome*; that she and Pietro might one day teach at university the way Mariarosa already does at twenty‑four; and to admit, *hai ragione tu, Lila, se ti insegnano le cose per bene fin da piccola, da grande si fa meno fatica in tutto, diventi una che sembra nata imparata*. But what would be the point? To listen in silence to Lila's news? Even if Lila let her speak, what would she say? She knows already that *a me non sarebbe successo mai ciò che di sicuro sarebbe successo a Pietro*; that he, like Franco, will disappear from her life soon enough, and that this is fine — *non lo amavo, ci stavo insieme nelle viuzze buie, nei prati, solo per sentire meno la paura*.
