## Summary

Elena can't resist. She asks Alfonso to set up a Sunday outing
with Marisa. At a pizzeria in via Foria she asks about Lidia, Ciro,
and finally Nino. Marisa is irritated to talk about her brother. He
had *un lungo periodo di pazzia*, even *mise le mani addosso* to
their father; nobody knew the cause; he wanted to stop studying and
leave Italy; then it all passed, he came back to himself and started
sitting exams again. *Quindi sta bene?* *Boh.* *E studia
soltanto?* *Sta sempre in giro. Adesso si è fissato col cinema, coi
romanzi, con l'arte.* Elena feels both relieved and bitter: *come
cambiavano in fretta le persone, i loro interessi, i loro
sentimenti. Frasi bene organizzate che sono sostituite da frasi
bene organizzate.* She had bent her own tastes toward Nino's. *Sì
sì, rassegnarsi a quello che si è, ognuno per la sua strada.* She
asks Marisa not to mention they met, and doesn't bring up Nino or
Lila again with Alfonso.

Elena tightens the discipline. Studies obsessively, takes another
private pupil, imposes *una linea retta cha andava dall'alba fino a
notte fonda*. *In passato c'era stata Lila, una continua felice
deviazione*; now everything has to come from herself. *Avevo quasi
diciannove anni, non sarei mai più dipesa da nessuno, e di nessuno
avrei mai più sentito la mancanza.* She fights through *geografia
astronomica, geometria, trigonometria*, with the fixed assumption
that her gifts are *costituzionalmente* limited and yet does the
maximum: dashes to the Museo archeologico and Capodimonte, learns
films by title and plot summary. She never sets foot in piazza dei
Martiri. She runs into Pinuccia *disfatta*, Carmen *avvelenata* at
the salumeria, Ada — *Ada, che aveva preso a detestare Lila* —
extolling Stefano: *non si meritava quella moglie che lo trattava a
pesci in faccia.*

Ada tells her Antonio has been *congedato con anticipo* from the
army for *un brutto esaurimento nervoso*: *lo sai che tipo è,
l'esaurimento ce l'aveva già con te.* She runs into Antonio one
Sunday, gaunt; he doesn't recognise her at first. He's at home,
unable to work, *paura* of the illness coming back. At Cordenons,
on sentry duty, he had remembered a childhood game with his late
father — drawing eyes and mouths on his five fingers and making
them talk. That night during his shift he felt his father's hand
inside his own, the fingers populated with tiny laughing, singing
people. He beat his hand against the *garitta* until it bled but
the fingers wouldn't stop. The fits returned and worsened until
he was sent off to the doctor. *Ora mi è passato. Però può sempre
ricominciare.*

Elena starts coming round on Sundays. They walk, sometimes with
Melina *vistosamente truccata*, sometimes with Pasquale and Ada.
The rione thickens with bad news, which Antonio internalises as if
*la disfunzione del suo corpo si stesse allargando a tutto il
rione*. Enzo's father, the fruttivendolo Nicola Scanno, dies of
pneumonia — Enzo gets a *licenza* but arrives too late. Carmen and
Pasquale's father, the ex-carpenter who'd been convicted of don
Achille's murder, dies in prison of a heart attack. Don Carlo
Resta, the *venditore di saponi e casalinghi*, is beaten to death
in his cellar — rumour says they shoved a file up his nose; the
Solaras take over the cellar and the stock *per pochissimi soldi*,
keeping the widow and the older son on to run it. Pasquale's
theory: Don Carlo owed *la madre dei Solara* gambling debts and
wouldn't pay *la strozzina*.

The worst comes one mild spring Sunday in their courtyard. They're
waiting for Carmela, who's gone up for a sweater. Carmela leans
out: *Pasquà, non trovo mamma: c'è la porta del cesso chiusa a
chiave dall'interno, ma lei non risponde.* Pasquale takes the stairs
two at a time. They knock; nothing. Antonio rips the handle off the
door. **Giuseppina Peluso** — once radiant, hard-working, who'd
welcomed the Stefano truce, who'd been proud her daughter worked at
Lila's salumeria — has unhooked the *lampadario* and used the
clothes-line wire to hang herself from the *crocco* in the ceiling.
Antonio falls apart worst of them all — *hai visto che aveva i
piedi scalzi e che le unghie erano lunghe e che su un piede c'era
lo smalto rosso fresco e sull'altro no?* For weeks he sees Giuseppina
in every dark corner. Elena drops some of her obligations to help
him through it. At the funeral she sees Lila for the first time
in months, holding the sobbing Carmen tightly; she and Stefano have
sent *una grande corona di fiori* with *le condoglianze dei coniugi
Carracci.*
