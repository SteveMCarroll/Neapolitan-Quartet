## Summary

In the spring of 1966 — five years after the events that open most of
this book — Lila gives Elena a metal box wrapped in too much string.
Inside are eight notebooks, and Lila is in a high state of agitation:
she can't keep them at home anymore because she's afraid her husband
will read them. She makes Elena swear not to open the box for any
reason. Elena swears. As soon as the train pulls out of the station,
she cuts the string and starts reading.

The notebooks are not a diary. They are a long, obsessive experiment
in disciplined writing — branch of a tree, the puddles, the pots and
the coffee pot, the workshop tools, the colours of everything at
different hours; isolated words in dialect and in Italian sometimes
circled without comment; Latin and Greek translation exercises; whole
passages in English about Enzo Scanno's fruit cart making the rounds
of the neighbourhood; arguments with Pasquale committed to paper;
notes on every film and every book. Even the entries Lila wrote at
ten or eleven read like nothing childish. Beneath their casual
naturalness, Elena can sense a relentless craft. So even the lovely
letter Lila had once sent her on Ischia, Elena realises bitterly, had
been *practiced*.

She makes herself stop. She fails. The notebooks pull her in the way
Lila herself always has — they have already captured the rione, the
Solaras, Stefano, Lila's first pair of shoes, her marriage at sixteen
— and Lila has also captured **Elena**: her looks, her words, her
loves, with a precision that humiliates and seduces in equal measure.
For days and weeks Elena studies the pages, memorising the parts that
exalt her and the parts that crush her.

One November evening, exasperated, she carries the box across the
Ponte Solferino in Turin. She sets it on the parapet and pushes it,
slowly, until it tips into the river — as if Lila herself were
plunging in, along with all her words, her cruelty, her habit of
swallowing everything and everyone she touches. With that the framing
device of book 2 is set: everything that follows is Elena's own
counter-account, told in part *against* Lila's silenced notebooks.
