## Summary

For once Lila feels herself entirely *dalla parte della ragione*, and the certainty calms her. She puts Gennaro to bed and waits at the kitchen table. Stefano comes in after midnight. She raises her eyes from her book and says she knows about Ada, knows how long it has been going on, doesn't care. *«Quello che tu hai fatto a me, io l'ho fatto a te»* she enunciates with a smile, and repeats — how many times has she said it now, two, three? — *Gennaro non è suo figlio*. He can do what he wants, sleep where and with whom he wants.

She doesn't know exactly what she expects: a confession; a thrashing; being thrown out; being made *sguattera* in her own home to a rival; the *protervia* of a man who feels he owns everything and has the money to buy what he doesn't. None of it comes. Stefano refuses to grant the conversation any words that would make it true. *Torvo*, but calm, he says Ada is nothing but his shop assistant; *qualsiasi chiacchiera circolasse… non aveva nessun fondamento*. Then he gets angry and shouts that if she repeats *quella brutta cosa* about the baby once more, *quant'era vero Dio*, he'll kill her: Gennaro is his image, everyone says so, end of subject.

Then — and this is the strangest part — he declares his love for her, the same formulas as ever, no variation. He'll love her forever, because she's his wife, because they married before a priest, because *niente poteva separarli*. When he tries to kiss her and she pushes him off, he lifts her bodily into the bedroom where the cot is and, while she begs in a tiny voice *«Si sveglia Rinuccio, ci vede, ci sente, per favore andiamo di là»*, he tears off her clothes and forces himself on her.
