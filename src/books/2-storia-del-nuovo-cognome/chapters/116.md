## Summary

Coming back to Naples is *come quando hai un ombrello difettoso che un colpo di vento ti chiude in testa all'improvviso*. She arrives in full summer. With her *laurea*, she can no longer hunt door‑to‑door for the little jobs of past summers; she has no money and is ashamed to ask her parents, *che si erano già sacrificati abbastanza*. She gets nervous. The streets, the ugly building fronts, the stradone, the *giardinetti* — once moving — now irritate her. *Se Pietro si trova un'altra, se non vinco il concorso, che cosa farò?* Her parents and brothers are *fierissimi* of her but don't really know why; she only complicates their crowded life and her habit of always being under a book makes them treat her as *un monumento inutile allo studio*. Her mother probes for facts about her *fidanzato* and is unimpressed — *insegnava ma non lo pagavano, pubblicava un libro ma non era famoso*. Worst of all, language itself becomes a sign of distance. Elena's simplified Italian sounds unnatural to her mother; her toned‑down Naples accent that didn't convince Pisa is now convincing *the rione* — she begins to be called *la pisana* behind her back.

She and Pietro exchange long letters that contain nothing concrete — no price of bread, no salary of a *usciere* or a *professore* — only book titles, articles, university unrest, and Pietro's amused report of *tematiche neoavanguardistiche* and a planned little book *con la carta appallottolata*. The last detail unsettles her: maybe it's his way of telling her her *quaderno*, her *regalo letterario*, struck him as *un prodotto attardato*. She asks after maestra Oliviero — the only person who might supply some satisfaction — but learns the sister has fetched her back to Potenza. Lonely, she even misses Lila and their *confrontarci turbolento*; she resolves not to seek her out and instead conducts a *cavillosa e sfaccendata indagine* on what's been said about her in the rione.

She fishes for Antonio — gone, said to be married to a beautiful platinum‑blonde German with two *gemelli*. She talks often to Alfonso at the piazza dei Martiri shop, now *un raffinatissimo hidalgo*, who has rented a flat at Ponte di Tappia, says he'll marry Marisa next year *senza troppo entusiasmo*, and runs the shop *a gonfie vele*. Marisa has lost her old vivacity and seems to weigh every sentence before speaking, lest she displease him; nobody mentions Nino. Pasquale is *muratore* across Naples and *provincia*, secretly seeing *la giovanissima figlia della merciaia*; Carmen is engaged to the forty‑something *benzinaio dello stradone*. Pinuccia is almost unrecognisable — *sciatta, nervosa, magrissima*, marked by the blows Rino still gives her in revenge for Stefano. The most surprising visit is to **Ada**, now mother of a *femmina* called Maria, beautiful and *serena*, working pregnant and after, *vera padrona delle due salumerie*. Of all the friends Ada speaks of Lila with the most warmth and the most precise information: *«Io ho fatto cose da pazza, lo riconosco. Ma Lina ed Enzo si sono comportati in un modo ancora più pazzo»* — they took nothing, left all the jewels, and *hanno scritto su un pezzo di carta dove andavano a stare, l'indirizzo preciso, numero, tutto, come per dire: venite a cercarci, fate quello che volete, chi se ne fotte*. Elena takes down the address. Ada's message: tell her it's not me keeping Stefano from the baby, *e dille di non fidarsi di nessuno*, especially of Michele Solara, *non si dimenticano di niente*.
