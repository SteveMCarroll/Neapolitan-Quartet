## Summary

Elena tries to catch up at school but the chaos at home — her
mother's shouting, the siblings' homework, her own exhaustion — and
Antonio's neediness keep her from concentrating. In a single day she
takes a 2 in chemistry, a 4 in art history, and a 3 in philosophy.
She bursts into tears in class. Alfonso tells her his sister-in-law
has been asking for her — *go, you'll study better there than at
home.* That afternoon Elena finally walks to Lila's new apartment.

Lila is well-dressed and well-coiffed, *as if she were the one
visiting.* Wedding gifts are still stacked in the hall; the new
furniture smells of varnish. Over coffee Elena asks how it's going
with Stefano. "Fine." Have you worked things out? "Yes, it's all
clear." Meaning? "Uno schifo" — disgusting. Did he beat you again?
"No, this is old stuff." Then? "It's the humiliation." And you?
"I do what he wants." Elena, fishing more carefully: *when you sleep
together, at least is it nice?* Lila grimaces and turns serious.
She describes Stefano without overt hatred — just a *tranquillo
disprezzo*, a calm contempt that flows over the whole man like
poisoned water into soil.

Elena tries to draw a comparison from her own life — *I'm
comfortable with Antonio even though I don't love him* — hoping Lila
will pick up the implied question (*does disgust come only after a
man takes you because he owns you?*). Lila changes tone, ironic:
*good for you that you're comfortable.* She leads Elena to a small
room overlooking the railway tracks: a desk, a chair, a cot, bare
walls. "Do you like it here? Then study." She closes the door behind
her. Elena hears Lila on the phone — *pronto, sono la signora
Carracci* — and opens her history book.
