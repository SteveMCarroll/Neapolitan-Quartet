## Summary

Stefano sleeps through the day. When he wakes and sees Lila is gone he does what he learned in childhood under don Achille: small movements, the half‑smile, *una distanza compassata*, holding terror and fury at arm's length. That evening, in a reckless mood, he goes under Ada's window and calls up to her. She's home — Melina is raving more than usual and Antonio is out at all hours for the Solaras — but Pasquale is there. Stefano comes up anyway, sits chatting politics with Pasquale and the salumeria with Ada, never mentions Lila, then goes home, shaves carefully and sleeps deeply, pretending she has gone to her parents.

The trouble starts the next morning. The shop assistant at piazza dei Martiri tells Michele that Lila hasn't come in; Michele rings Stefano; Stefano says she's ill. The illness drags on long enough that Nunzia comes by and finds the flat locked. When she comes back after the shops close, both families pile in. Stefano bursts into tears and says she's left him. Rino and Fernando turn on him for letting the *calzaturificio* die and for putting her in the Solara shop. Pinuccia screams that Lila has always been a *testa pazza* and that Stefano is the real victim. Alfonso suggests the police and the hospitals, and they shout him down — *non diventiamo la barzelletta del rione*. It is Maria, of all people, who quietly offers a way out: *«Forse è andata a stare un poco da Lenù»*. The idea catches; Nunzia leans on it gratefully. *«Sì, fa sempre così, appena ha un problema cerca Lenù»*. The story consolidates: Lila has run to Pisa to rest from Stefano and the Solaras. Only Alfonso wants to go check; Pinuccia shuts him down because he has to work, and Fernando seconds her: *«Lasciamola quieta»*.

The next day Stefano repeats the Pisa story to anyone who asks. Nunzia, still uneasy, sends Alfonso to Elena's mother for the address; Immacolata, hostile by nature or to protect her daughter's studies, gives him an incomplete one. Nunzia and Alfonso write Elena a careful letter c/o the University of Pisa; it arrives late, infuriates her at both Lila and Nino, and she does not answer. Meanwhile Ada now cleans Stefano's flat and cooks for him on top of everything else, which makes Pasquale furious — *«Non sei pagata per fare la serva» — «Meglio fare la serva che perdere tempo a discutere con te»*. Alfonso is hurried into the piazza dei Martiri shop to keep the Solaras quiet; he loves it. And Michele, foul‑tempered now that *la signora Carracci* has vanished, calls Antonio in and says: *«Trovamela»*. From where? — *«Dal figlio grande di Sarratore»*. Then the look reserved for the worthless: speak about this search to anyone, *ti faccio mettere dentro al manicomio di Aversa*. Antonio nods.
