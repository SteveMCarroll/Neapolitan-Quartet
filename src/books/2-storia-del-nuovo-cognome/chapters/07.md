## Summary

They reach Amalfi at dusk. Neither has stayed in a hotel before;
Stefano covers his unease with a swaggering tip to the porter Lila
follows up the staircase, and for the first time she sees her
husband as a stranger: short legs, long arms, white knuckles. The
fury of the drive gives way to a colder dread.

He tries to be tender — open the balcony, look at the sea, "let's
take a gilet from the suitcase" as if they had been living together
for years — and she answers by refusing to open the bags and walking
out into the corridor. They wander Amalfi. He's all banalities,
"isn't it lovely"; she shakes uncontrollably from nerves, not cold.
At a restaurant they barely eat but drink a lot. Looking across at
him in his suit, his red tongue in his open mouth, his fork dipping
into her plate, she has a single clear thought: *I don't know this
person. The man who courted me was a mask.* She palms a steak knife
under her napkin and considers slipping it into her purse; then she
sets it back down, less out of mercy than out of the dazed conviction
that her whole wedding has come undone in the head.

Back at the room she locks herself in the bathroom; Stefano threatens
to break down the door. She comes out in the nightgown she'd chosen
months ago, hating herself for the care she'd taken with it. *Non
ti voglio*, she says. He laughs, points at himself, *guarda cosa mi
succede solo a vederti*. When she keeps refusing he physically lifts
her and drops her on the bed. The chapter watches Stefano's familiar
mild face peel back to reveal **don Achille** underneath — *il padre
gli stava crepando la pelle* — and the scene becomes a rape. The
prose pulls outward at the end: by the time he tears into her, Lila
is "absent," gone to a place where the only thing she can still feel
is hatred — of his weight, his name, the surname pressed onto her.
