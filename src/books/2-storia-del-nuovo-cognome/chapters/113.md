## Summary

Lila barricades herself in Rinuccio's room and works out what to do. She will not go back to her parents — *il peso della sua vita le appartiene, non vuole ridiventare figlia*. She cannot count on Rino: he is *fuori di sé*, taking out Stefano on Pinuccia and even quarrelling with Maria, broke, in debt. She counts only on Enzo: she has trusted him and still trusts him, even though he hasn't shown up — and seems to have vanished from the rione altogether. *Pensa: me l'ha promesso che mi tirerà fuori di qui*. But she half hopes he won't make good on the promise — she doesn't want to bring him trouble. She isn't really afraid of a fight with Stefano (*il marito ha ormai rinunciato a lei*; he's a coward with a beast's strength). She is afraid of Michele Solara. *Non oggi, non domani, ma quando nemmeno ci penserò più mi comparirà davanti e se non mi piegherò me la farà pagare, e la farà pagare a chiunque mi abbia aiutato*. Better, then, to leave without involving anyone. She needs a job — *uno qualsiasi* — enough to feed the boy and put a roof over him.

The thought of her son drains her. *Cosa è finito nella testa di Rinuccio*. Images, words. She worries about the voices that have reached him without her control. Maybe he heard her voice while she carried him. *Chissà come gli si è impressa nella nervatura*. He has felt loved and rejected; he has felt her *agitazione*. *Come si protegge un figlio. Nutrendolo. Amandolo. Insegnandogli le cose. Facendo da filtro a ogni sensazione che possa menomarlo per sempre*. She has lost his real father, who knows nothing of him and will never love him. Stefano — *che non è suo padre e che tuttavia un po' lo ha amato* — has sold them both for the love of another woman and a *figlio più vero*.

What about the boy himself? Rinuccio already knows that when she goes into another room she has not vanished — *non mi perde*, she is still there. He moves between objects and ghosts of objects, between inside and outside; he eats by himself with spoon and fork; *manipola le cose e le forma, le trasforma*; he is past single words and into sentences. *In italiano. Non dice più lui, dice io*. He recognises the letters of the alphabet; he can put them together to write his name. He loves colours, *è allegro*. But there is all this fury around him. *Mi ha vista insultata e picchiata. Mi ha vista spaccare cose e insultare. In dialetto*. *Non posso più stare qui*.
