## Summary

A new obsession: stimulate the baby's intelligence. Lila has Alfonso ask the booksellers for what's needed and reads everything he brings home. Her notebooks describe a peculiar method: she pushes her eye down the lines even after meaning has slipped, lets her fingers turn pages on automatic, and finds afterwards that the words have somehow lodged in her head and brought ideas with them. Then she rereads, correcting and expanding the thoughts, until the book has nothing more to give and she goes looking for another.

Stefano comes home to no dinner and the baby playing with toys she has made herself. He erupts; she barely registers him. The flat feels inhabited only by her and the child, and when she does cook it's because she has got hungry, not because he has. After the long mutual *tolleranza* their marriage decays again. He yells he's had enough of her, of the baby, of everything. Once when she says *«Nemmeno io so che ci faccio qua, mi prendo il bambino e me ne vado»*, instead of telling her to leave he loses control as he hasn't in months and hits her in front of Gennaro, who watches dazed from his blanket on the floor. Bleeding from the nose, with Stefano still shouting, Lila turns to the baby and tells him in Italian — she now only speaks Italian to him — *«Papà gioca, ci stiamo divertendo»*.

She takes up her nephew, *Fernando detto Dino*, perhaps to have a second child to compare Gennaro against, perhaps out of scruple. Pinuccia, who treats Dino as the proof of her ruined life and screams at him *«La vuoi finire? Mi vuoi fare uscire pazza?»*, won't let Lila take him for *giochi misteriosi*. Then Rino steps in. He's in a wretched stretch — fighting his father (who wants to close the *calzaturificio*), the Solaras (who treat him as a petulant boy and deal directly with Stefano), Stefano himself (who stopped paying him and is, Rino believes, finalising a secret transfer of the shoe business to Marcello and Michele), and Pinuccia (who calls him *un pupazzetto* manoeuvred by everyone). Out of spite at all of them he begins bringing Dino over himself. He sits on Lila's floor in awe as his sullen, listless son turns alert with her: *«Che gli fai?» — «Li faccio giocare» — «Mio figlio giocava pure prima» — «Qui gioca e impara… ho letto che tutto quello che siamo si decide adesso, nei primi anni di vita» — «Allenalo, Lina, non ti stancare subito come fai di solito»*.

One evening Stefano comes home early and finds Rino on the kitchen floor. Beyond the usual sour face, he turns on the brother‑in‑law: this is his house, he doesn't want him hanging around all day, the *calzaturificio* is going under because Rino is a *sfaticato*, the Cerullos are *inaffidabili*, get out or I'll throw you out. Lila shouts at him to stop, Rino spits out everything he has been swallowing for months — *«Tu non sei nessuno, tu sei solo una merda, tuo padre sì che sapeva fare il delinquente, tu manco quello sai fare»*. With shocking strength Stefano seizes him at the waist like a ballet partner, lifts him off the ground despite their matching height and build, and hurls him against the wall, then drags him along the floor by the arm and pitches him down the stairs while Lila hangs on his back, begging. He comes back in for Dino, intending the same. Lila throws herself at his shoulders, claws his face: *«È un bambino, Ste', è un bambino»*. He stops. *«Mi sono rotto il cazzo di tutto, non ce la faccio più»*.
