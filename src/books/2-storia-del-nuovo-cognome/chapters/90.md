## Summary

Elena pauses to name the great underlying terror of Lila's life: that people, even more than things, lose their edges and spread shapelessly. Lila's notebooks record how she had watched it happen — to Rino, whom she loved more than anyone in her family, and to Stefano, the night her marriage began, his body threatening to swell, to ooze, to drag every piece of furniture and Lila herself into a sticky living flux. This *smarginarsi* is what she has been fighting on every page of her life: shapes that won't hold.

So when the door closes behind her on the rione and she crosses it as if inside *una scia bianca di vapore*, dragging her suitcase to the metro and out to the Campi Flegrei, she feels she has stepped from a soft, oozing space into something at last capable of containing her. She walks empty streets to a low‑grade *caseggiato popolare*, climbs to the second floor, and lets herself into the two rooms she has rented: dim, ill‑kept, mismatched furniture, a toilet with only a bowl and a sink. She has done all of this herself — Nino had exams to study for and an article to finish for *Il Mattino* and a longer essay to place with *Nord e Sud* after *Cronache meridionali* refused it. She has put down three months' rent. Once she's inside she is not depressed by any of it. *Piacere, sì, lei scriveva così*. The mould stain in the corner of the bedroom, the grey light at the window, the smell — none of it touches her. The old enchantment of erasure has taken her again: *basta con tutto ciò che era stata; basta* with the *stradone*, the shoes, the salumeria, the husband, the Solaras, piazza dei Martiri — *basta anche con me*. All she has kept of herself is Nino's mistress, who arrives that evening.

He's nervous. He embraces her, kisses her, looks around as if he doesn't know where he is, bolts every door and window as if expecting an *irruzione*. They make love, for the first time in a bed since Forio. Then he gets up to study and complains about the dim light; she gets up to help him revise the new *Mattino* article. They go to sleep at three in the morning, tangled together. Outside it's raining and the windows rattle, the flat is unfamiliar, and she feels *al sicuro*. *Com'era nuovo il corpo di Nino, lungo, sottile, così distante da quello di Stefano*. *Le parve di venire da un mondo d'ombre e di essere arrivata in un posto dove finalmente la vita era vera*. At dawn she puts her feet on the floor, runs to the toilet to throw up, and closes the door so he won't hear.
