## Summary

In those months Elena dreams of Lila often. In one dream Lila is in bed in a frilly green nightdress, hair plaited in ways she has never plaited it, holding a baby girl in pink and saying over and over *«Fatemi una foto ma solo a me, la bambina no»*. In another she greets Elena, calls in her daughter — *«Lenù, vieni a salutare la zia»* — and out walks a fat *gigantessa* much older than them, whom Lila orders Elena to undress, wash and re‑swaddle. Each morning Elena means to find a phone and ring Alfonso to ask whether the baby is here, whether Lila is happy, and each morning study or exams crowd it out. In August she skips Naples altogether, lies to her parents and goes to Versilia with Franco to a flat his family owns. She wears a *due pezzi* for the first time and feels *audace*.

It is Carmen who tells her, at Christmas, how brutal Lila's labour was. *«Ha rischiato di morire — il dottore alla fine le ha dovuto tagliare la pancia, se no il bambino non nasceva»*. A boy. *«È bellissimo»*. And Lila? *«S'è allargata»*. Stefano had wanted the baby named Achille after his father; Lila refused — for the first time in months their shouting echoed across the clinic and the nurses had to scold them. The child became Gennaro, *cioè Rino*, after Lila's brother. Elena listens, withdrawn behind her new distant manner. Carmen catches it: *«Parlo parlo, ma tu non dici nemmeno una parola, mi fai sentire come il telegiornale. Non te ne fotte più niente di noi?»*. *«Ti sei fatta bella, t'è cambiata persino la voce»*. — *«Avevo una brutta voce?»* — *«Avevi la voce che abbiamo noi… adesso ce l'hai di meno»*.

Elena stays in the rione ten days, 24 December 1964 to 3 January 1965, and never goes to see Lila or the child. She is afraid she'd recognise *qualcosa di Nino* in the baby's mouth or nose. At home her father watches her with embarrassed pride and stops asking what she studies for fear of not understanding the answers. Her mother moves with the old furious gait Elena once feared she would inherit; she resents the distance Elena has put between them, but for once won't let her do the dishes or the floors. Her brothers correct their own Italian and apologise; she leans into being the old Lenù until they relax. The old gang has fallen apart — Pasquale avoids Antonio for selling out to the Solaras; Antonio is at Solara errands all hours and has nothing to say; Ada is exhausted by Melina and the salumeria; Carmen hates everyone. The one who surprises her is Enzo, who has somehow, while nursing his mother Assunta through a terminal illness, passed his *perito industriale* by private exam. She drags him out for a walk; he barely speaks, his vocabulary pared back to nothing. When they part he produces a single sentence, apropos of nothing she has said: *«Comunque Lina è la madre migliore di tutto il rione»*. That *comunque* unsettles her. Walking beside her, he seems to have heard, as if she had declaimed it, the long silent indictment her body has been carrying.
