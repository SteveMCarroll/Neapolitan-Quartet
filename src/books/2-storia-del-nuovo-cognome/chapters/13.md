## Summary

At a crumbling, half-flooded liceo, classes run every other day and
homework piles up. A faction led by **Nino Sarratore** is demanding
a return to the full schedule. Elena keeps hoping that Galiani, who
huddles in the corridor with Nino, will call her over. She doesn't.
She hopes Nino will. He doesn't either. *I've lost the little
prestige I had,* she thinks bitterly. Then one morning Nino
materialises with a typed sheet: *Will you read this? Give it back at
the end of the day.*

She locks herself in the bathroom and reads. The leaflet is full of
numbers — building code, articles of the constitution, the building
plan — most of which mean nothing to her; she follows only the
demand to return to the normal schedule. Alfonso, glancing at it,
warns her off: *exams are coming, he just wants to make trouble for
you.* But Elena is electrified. She waits him out at the end of the
day, hands it back with a torrent of praise. He frowns — *Galiani
says it's no good* — crumples the sheet, throws it away. Then he
leans down and kisses her on the cheek.

Two ugly things happen in immediate succession. A girl, no older
than fifteen, very pretty in a soft, well-bred way, runs up to Nino;
he puts an arm around her shoulders and she lifts her face for a
kiss — a different kiss from the one he just gave Elena. And on the
corner Antonio is standing watching, having left work without telling
her, having been there for who-knows-how-long.
