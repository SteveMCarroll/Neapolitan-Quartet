## Summary

Lila's pregnancy lasts ten weeks total. The midwife scrapes her out
and she is back behind the salumeria counter the next day. She
settles into a long stretch of compressed life — pale, with acne and
hollow eyes, but slicing, weighing, taking in money, and out-earning
the old shop by Christmas. Her mother-in-law **Maria Carracci**
unexpectedly takes her side, gives her old red-gold earrings, and
defends her when Stefano and Pinuccia accuse her of *not wanting
children* — of being the kind of woman who *makes them slip away.*

For Elena it's the opposite of a slowdown: with Antonio drafted and
Nino in England, *qualcosa nella mia testa si fosse sciolto*. She
starts the second year of liceo at the top of the class.
**Professoressa Galiani**, having lost her star pupil Nino, makes
Elena her new project: sends her to a peace march that starts at
Resina and ends in Naples (Elena's brothers, dragged along, end up
throwing stones at the marchers); pushes books and newspapers at
her. There's no money for textbooks — la maestra Oliviero has gone
to live with a sister in Potenza. Elena arranges to share Alfonso's
books and study together at Lila's empty apartment.

Then Alfonso tells her one morning, irritated: *go by the salumeria,
Lila wants to see you, she made me swear not to tell you why.*
Elena goes. **Carmela Peluso** shows her a postcard from Enzo in
Piedmont; Lila has had one from Antonio too, doesn't show it. She
pulls Elena into the back room, reminds her — *remember our bet?
Remember you lost? Remember you have to be promoted with an 8
average?* — and points at two large parcels in butcher's paper:
**all her schoolbooks, brand new, perfumed with fresh ink, plus the
dictionaries Oliviero could never get her: Zingarelli, Rocci,
Calonghi-Georges.**
