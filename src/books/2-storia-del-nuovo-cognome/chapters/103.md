## Summary

That is roughly the state of things when Elena comes home for Easter. She has been at Pisa for two and a half years now, a *studentessa molto brillante*; coming home is a chore she does to keep her mother quiet. From the moment the train pulls in she's afraid that some accident — illness, a family crisis — will block her return. She has only been home a few hours when her mother delivers a brisk, malevolent report on the whole rione disaster and ends it, almost reluctantly, with a half‑pleasured: *«La tua amica si credeva di essere arrivata chissà dove, e invece oggi tu sei assai più brava e assai più bella di lei»*. Then hands her the note from Lila — already read — inviting her to lunch on Good Friday.

The note is only one of the day's requests. Pasquale calls her down to the courtyard *come se fossi discesa da un Olimpo* to lay out his theories on Woman and ask her opinion. Pinuccia does the same in the evening, raging at Rino and at Lila both. Ada does it the next morning, *bruciante di odi e di sensi di colpa*, asking Elena to relay to Lila that the heart obeys no master and that *io sono quella che a Stefano ha dato e dà tutto, ogni attenzione e sentimento che può volere un uomo, presto anche figli, e perciò lui è mio*. Elena handles each of them with calm advice in textbook Italian and quietly enjoys being treated *come una veggente, sterilizzata dallo studio*. To herself she thinks sarcastically that this is what her *esami di storia, filologia classica, glottologia* are good for: pacifying them for an afternoon. She doesn't tell any of them about the risks she has been taking in Pisa — Franco in her room, her in his, the Versilia summer they spent *come se fossimo sposati*. She is, she admits, *contenta di sé*.

The pleasure leaks away as lunch with Lila approaches. She fears Lila will rebuild the old hierarchy with a flick of the wrist, or point at Gennaro and show her Nino's bones. Neither happens. Rinuccio is a *bellissimo bambino bruno*, not yet Nino in any detail, with traces of Lila and even Stefano, *come se l'avessero generato in tre*. Lila herself is the most fragile Elena has ever seen her. She has dressed up — quick lipstick, a *rayon grigioperla* dress from her engagement days, heeled shoes — and at the door her eyes fill and her whole body shakes; Elena holds her tight to calm her. Her face is thinner, her cheekbones almost too large, her skin pale and opaque; the dress shows a swollen *ventre gonfio*. She bombards Elena with terminology from her disordered reading and parades Rinuccio's accomplishments. Elena notices a new tic — a sudden wide opening of the mouth that closes quickly to dam an emotion, often coupled with a flush in the eyes that the mouth's snap puts away. *Nel giro di una generazione*, Lila says, if each tiny child of the rione were truly worked with, *non ci sarebbero stati più i bravi e gli incapaci, i buoni e i cattivi*. Then she looks at Gennaro and bursts into tears. *«Mi ha rovinato i libri»* — she shows Elena books torn, snapped in two halves. Not the baby — Stefano. *«Ha preso l'abitudine di frugare tra le mie cose, non vuole che io abbia nemmeno un pensiero mio»*. She climbs on a chair and pulls down from the top of the bedroom wardrobe a metal box, and presses it on Elena. *«Qui c'è tutto quello che è successo con Nino, e tanti pensieri che mi sono passati per la testa in questi anni, e anche cose mie e tue che non ci siamo dette. Portatela via, ho paura che lui la trovi e si metta a leggere. Ma non voglio, non sono cose per lui, non sono cose per nessuno, nemmeno per te»*.
