## Summary

The two girls spend the last days of September shut in the Piazza
dei Martiri shop with three workmen, gluing black cardboard,
tracing red and blue outlines around the surviving fragments of the
wedding photo, painting in dense colour. Elena calls them "hours of
play, of invention, of freedom" — the closest she and Lila have
been since childhood. They forget Antonio, Nino, Stefano, the
Solaras, school, the pregnancy.

But as they work, Elena keeps returning to **Michele's word for what
they're doing: *scancellare*** — to erase. She begins to understand
that the strips and slashes aren't a design exercise: Lila has
seized this opportunity to **stage her own self-destruction as
public image**. The chapter's confession comes while Lila glues:
she has been working through what *Cerullo in Carracci* means as a
grammatical case. She started by reading it as *complemento di stato
in luogo* — Raffaella resides at Stefano's. Then, in the months
since the beating on the honeymoon, it has shifted into *complemento
di moto a luogo*: **Cerullo *moves into* Carracci, is absorbed by
him, dissolves inside his shape**. She has been overrun by a force
that is "still in motion." Elena finally sees the panel for what it
is — a portrait of a woman annulling herself.

When the workmen mount it on the wall, both girls fall briefly sad
and don't say so. Lila bursts into the first genuine, self-mocking
laugh Elena has heard from her in months. Elena stares at the top
of the panel, where Lila's head should be, and finds only **one
fiercely alive eye, ringed in midnight blue and red**.
