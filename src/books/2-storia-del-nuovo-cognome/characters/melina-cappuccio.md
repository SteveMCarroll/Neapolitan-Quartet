Antonio and Ada's mother. Widowed young when her husband died on a
building site, she had a brief and very public affair in book 1 with
the railwayman‑poet **Donato Sarratore**; when the Sarratores moved
out of the rione she broke down, throwing pots and an iron out of her
window — and very nearly killing the small Nino with it. She has
lived since on the edge of madness, in the apartment beneath the
Sarratores', cleaning staircases and being supported by her children.

In book 2 she is largely a half‑mute background figure — but she
returns to the foreground in the household scene in which she
accidentally brushes her *scopa* across her unmarried daughter Ada's
feet. The dialect superstition (a single woman so swept will never
marry) sets Ada off in a fury that effectively decides her future
with Stefano. By the end of the book Melina lives with Ada and
Stefano and her granddaughter Maria.
