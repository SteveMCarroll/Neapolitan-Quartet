Elena's classical *liceo* teacher of Latin and Italian, and her single
greatest intellectual patron of book 1's later half. A communist
*signora* of the Vomero, married to a doctor, mother of Armando and
Nadia. She is the first adult who treats Elena as a thinker and not
just as a diligent student — she lends her *l'Unità*, lends her
*Cronache Meridionali*, invites her to Sunday tea, and later to the
party at her flat where Elena meets her children and reconnects with
Nino.

In book 2 Galiani drifts out of Elena's daily orbit once Elena leaves
for the *Scuola Normale Superiore* in Pisa, but reappears, by
imitation, every time Elena needs to stage a particular kind of
educated voice — the way she handles a difficult dinner table, talks
about politics to the Airota family, or fields the unfriendly question
about *forza modernizzatrice* at her own Milan book launch.
