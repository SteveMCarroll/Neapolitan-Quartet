## Summary

Elena says she cannot truly narrate **Lila**'s pain. What happened to **Tina** was not death by illness, accident, or violence, but sudden vanishing. There was no body to hold, no funeral, no corpse that proved the child had become an inert thing. Elena imagines it like losing a limb that, without trauma, simply loses form and substance; but she admits she does not know the suffering well enough to imagine it.

In the ten years after Tina's disappearance, Elena sees Lila every day in the same building, yet never sees her cry or break down. After the first frantic searches through the rione, day and night, Lila seems to collapse from exhaustion. She sits for a long time by the kitchen window, where there is only a slice of railway and sky. Then she resumes ordinary life, but without any *rassegnazione* — resignation. She ages by shouting and quarrelling, spreading unease and fear.

At first Lila speaks of Tina constantly, as if clinging to the name could bring the child back. Later even mentioning the loss becomes impossible. If Elena tries, Lila drives her away. The only thing Lila seems to appreciate is a letter from **Pietro**, probably because he writes affectionately without mentioning Tina at all. By 1995, except in rare moments, Lila acts as if nothing has happened. When **Pinuccia** calls Tina a little angel watching over them, Lila says only: go away.
