## Summary

**Elena** and **Nino** return to Rome, swear endless promises, then separate: he goes to Naples, she to Florence. Elena expects disaster at home. Instead **Dede** and **Elsa** greet her with anxious joy and follow her through the house as if she might vanish again. **Adele** is controlled and kind; **Pietro**, pale and withdrawn, gives Elena a list of phone calls — **Lila**'s name appears four times — and leaves almost immediately without saying goodbye.

Adele slowly reveals her position. She wants Elena to *tornare in me* — come back to herself — and to Pietro, but she never shouts. She praises Elena's intelligence, takes interest in the French reviews and lecture plans, and avoids defending her son. When Elena insists that life with Pietro was especially hard, Adele warns that Milan gossip about Nino is ugly. Elena refuses to hear it, saying she has loved him for twenty years.

Adele accepts without protest when Elena leaves the girls to visit Nino in Naples, and then to go to France, asking only whether she will be home for Christmas. The girls react badly: Elsa begs to come, Dede tries contempt before breaking down. Their sobs follow Elena into the street. In Naples, Nino is waiting at the station and has taken a hotel room in Mergellina. Their joy returns, but at dinner he announces a troubling novelty: Lina has called and wants to see them.
