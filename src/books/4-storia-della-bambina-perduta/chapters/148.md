## Summary

The departures of **Dede** and **Elsa** depress Elena. Though the girls write and call affectionately, she feels they have chosen **Pietro** and the world he opens to them over her. **Lila** then suggests, in a tone Elena cannot read, that **Imma** should also be sent away to Rome to **Nino**, since the girl clearly wants to imitate her sisters. Elena hears this as another loss: if Imma leaves, she says, her life will have no meaning. Lila answers dryly, *dov'è scritto* — where is it written — that lives have to mean anything?

The question becomes entangled with politics. Imma, eleven years old, adores Nino and throws herself into his election campaign. Elena detests the Socialists more than ever and tells Nino that his class of politicians is worse than the Solara-style criminals of their childhood. When corruption scandals erupt and Nino asks, through Imma, for Elena's public support, she says yes to the child but then withdraws. Imma is furious when Elena also blocks her appearance in one of Nino's campaign spots.

Nino loses the election, and Imma sobs that it is Elena's fault. Soon the entire party system is overwhelmed. Lila, cheerful on the stairs, shows Elena a newspaper suggesting that even Guido Airota may be questioned about Italy's *marciume* — its rot.
