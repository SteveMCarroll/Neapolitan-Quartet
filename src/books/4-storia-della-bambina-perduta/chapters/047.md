## Summary

Lila's practiced movement between clarity and obscurity wounds **Elena** especially during one appointment with the gynecologist. It is November, but Naples still gives off heat as if summer had not ended. On the way, **Lila** feels ill, so the two women stop in a bar before going to the doctor, somewhat alarmed.

At the appointment Lila describes her discomfort with self-mocking exaggeration: the now-large *coso* — thing, the baby — inside her pulls, pushes, restrains, disturbs, and weakens her. The gynecologist, amused, reassures her that she will have a child like herself, lively and imaginative. Elena, still worried, asks whether everything is truly all right. The doctor says yes: nothing is wrong with the pregnancy. If Lila's symptoms have anything to do with anything, they have to do with her head.

Lila snaps back: what does the doctor know about her head? The answer lands heavily. The doctor says Lila's friend **Nino** has praised it highly. "Nino? Friend?" Silence follows. Outside, Elena struggles to stop Lila from changing doctors. Before leaving, Lila says with ferocity that Elena's lover is certainly not her friend, and in Lila's opinion he is not Elena's friend either.

Elena is pushed back to the center of her anxiety: Nino's unreliability. Lila has shown before that she knows things about him Elena does not. Is she now hinting at still more concrete facts? Elena asks, but Lila cuts off the conversation and goes away.
