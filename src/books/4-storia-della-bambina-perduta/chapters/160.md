## Summary

**Elena** holds her depression at bay and phones **Lila** less. She no longer hopes for Lila's pages; she fears them. If Lila were to send a manuscript, Elena knows she would admire it, publish it, and work to impose its value on the world — but she also believes that such a reversal would annihilate her. She has become, in Lila's teasing phrase, "Elena Greco, Raffaella Cerullo's brilliant friend," and she cannot bear the thought of losing even that identity.

For a while her ordinary life protects her from these thoughts. Then worse years arrive: her books sell less, she loses authority at the publishing house, her body changes, and she fears an old age that is poor and without *aura* — without prestige. In 2005 she goes to Naples and meets Lila, who is more nervous and talkative than before, excited that the world has come into the neighborhood through immigrants, new languages, unfamiliar cooking, and new forms of violence.

That visit is marked by the discovery of **Gigliola**'s corpse in the gardens. Elena first imagines she has been murdered; later it is known to be a heart attack. Seeing the huge, lifeless body, Elena feels no longer different from Gigliola. Lila, however, seems untouched by age. Elena's anxiety changes: the threat is not only Lila's possible masterpiece, but the fear that nothing of Elena herself will last.
