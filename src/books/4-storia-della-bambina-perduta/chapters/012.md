## Summary

Finding the house empty, **Elena** imagines disasters: perhaps **Dede** or **Elsa** is hurt, or **Pietro** has done something desperate and **Adele** has taken the girls to the hospital. At three in the morning she calls **Mariarosa**, assuming Adele would have warned her if something serious had happened. Mariarosa sleepily explains that Adele took the girls to Genoa two days earlier so Elena and Pietro could handle their crisis freely and the children could have peaceful Christmas holidays.

Relief immediately turns to fury. Pietro lied when Elena called from Rome: he knew there was no *cenone* — Christmas Eve dinner — waiting for her. Adele has taken Elena's daughters without permission. Elena asks Mariarosa whether she is ruining everything and deserves what is happening. Mariarosa answers gravely but supportively: Elena has the right to her own life and the duty to keep studying and writing, and Mariarosa will host her and the girls whenever needed.

Elena cannot sleep. She churns through anger, longing for **Nino**, and resentment that he will still spend the holidays with his family and **Albertino** while she is alone. In the morning Pietro returns unkempt and smelling of wine. He says he cannot care for the girls because Elena has her lover. Elena tries to negotiate custody, but when she mentions living in Naples, Pietro nearly hits her, then instead smashes a metal broom cabinet until he breaks his hand. After the hospital, they eat Christmas food together. Pietro then reveals he has called Elena's mother, who is already on the train to Florence.
