## Summary

In the same period **Elena**'s sister **Elisa** gives birth anxiously to a boy, **Silvio**, named for **Marcello**'s father. Because their mother **Immacolata** is unwell, Elena tries to help. Elisa is exhausted and frightened by the newborn's bloodied body, his crying, the umbilical wound, and even the fact that he is male. Elena instructs her, but both Marcello and Elisa soon seem irritated by her presence.

Then worse news arrives. On a hot morning shortly after the Bologna station bombing, **Peppe** calls: their mother has fainted in the bathroom. Elena finds her sweating, trembling, and in terrible abdominal pain. After tests, doctors diagnose what everyone calls *un brutto male* — "a bad illness," the evasive phrase for cancer — and finally describe it as inexorable.

**Vittorio** collapses emotionally, the brothers briefly bustle and then disappear into their vague jobs, leaving money, and Elisa stays home with the baby. Four months pregnant, Elena takes on the burden. She wants her mother to know that, despite all the torment between them, she loves her. She asks **Nino** and **Pietro** for medical contacts, takes Immacolata to specialists, stays by her through emergency surgery, and cares for her at home.

The illness changes Immacolata. Still harsh, dialectal, and demanding, she also becomes fragile, afraid, and unexpectedly tearful: over **Dede**, over laundry for her husband, over Elisa, over Elena's belly, even over **Maestra Oliviero** urging Elena toward middle school. Elena is shaken by this new softness.
