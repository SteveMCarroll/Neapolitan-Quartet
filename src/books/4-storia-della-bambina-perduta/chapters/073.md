## Summary

Elena no longer fully believes **Nino**'s recurring hostility toward **Lila**, because it alternates with moments that suggest the opposite. A few evenings earlier, he had been struggling with an essay on work and robotization at Fiat: he did not really know what a microprocessor or chip was. Elena suggested that he speak with **Enzo Scanno**, who was good with such things.

Nino asked distractedly who Enzo was. When Elena answered that he was Lina's man, Nino smiled and said he would rather talk to Lina, since she surely knew more. Then, as if remembering, he added with spite: was Scanno not the stupid son of the greengrocer? The tone stays with Elena. Enzo has founded an innovative small company in the old neighborhood, something almost miraculous. A serious scholar should admire him. Instead Nino reduces him, through the imperfect tense — *era* ("he was") — to the elementary-school boy who helped his mother in the shop and never shone.

Elena realizes that Nino has stripped Enzo of credit and given it all to Lila. If forced to dig inside himself, Nino would have to admit that his ideal of female intelligence, perhaps even his speeches about wasted women's intellects, has everything to do with Lila. If Elena and Nino's season of love is darkening, the season of Ischia will remain radiant for him. The man for whom she left **Pietro**, Elena thinks, is what he is because meeting Lila reshaped him.
