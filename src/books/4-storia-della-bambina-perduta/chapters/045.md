## Summary

Elena recognizes that she has a double identity. In the via Tasso apartment, **Nino** brings cultivated friends who respect her, especially for her second book. They debate the proletariat, socialists, Communists, governability, drugs, and the new disease they dismiss as a papal invention against sexual freedom. Elena feels like a woman with a small *aura* — an aura or prestige.

But she refuses to be trapped in Naples. She often takes **Dede** and **Elsa** to Florence, listens to **Pietro**, now openly Communist and politically estranged from his father, praise his party and report his book's academic success. Then she leaves the girls with him, goes to Milan, and tries to repair the damage **Adele** is doing at the publishing house by calling Elena unreliable. She charms everyone, helps publicity, and lies that her new book is well advanced.

Returning south, Elena slides back into chaotic Naples: traffic, endless bargaining, hostile queues, hospitals and laboratories for her mother. At via Tasso and around Italy she is a lady; in the neighborhood she loses refinement and, when wronged, shifts into dialect and filthy insults. Violence links the high and low worlds: killings across Italy, beatings and murders in the neighborhood. Though treated with courtesy, Elena feels like an outsider. **Carmen**, **Enzo**, **Lila**, and others seem to know secrets she does not. Talk of **Pasquale** makes Carmen and Lila stiffen. Encounters with **Antonio**, now strangely close to Lila and Enzo, deepen Elena's sense of an undisclosed pact.
