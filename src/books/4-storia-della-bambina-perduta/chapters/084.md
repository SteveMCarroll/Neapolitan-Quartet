## Summary

Within months, **Elena** and **Lila** become very close. They shop together and, on Sundays, go into the center with **Enzo** and the girls instead of lingering at the rione stalls. Enzo carries **Tina**, buys treats and balloons, and plays with **Dede**, **Elsa**, and **Imma**. Elena and Lila deliberately hang back, talking.

Their conversations are not like adolescence, but Elena enjoys them. Lila asks about things she has heard on television, and Elena speaks freely about postmodernism, publishing, feminism, famous intellectuals, and the world she has entered. Lila listens with a faintly ironic admiration, saying things like *quante cose sai* — how many things you know — while refusing to offer opinions of her own. Elena senses that Lila wants her nearby as a friend only if she keeps the aura of a writer, journalist, and television intellectual.

Elena also asks about Lila's life with Enzo, but Lila gives little away: she is "fairly" happy, loves him "fairly," wants no one else. Yet Lila's presence still stimulates Elena's mind, as if energy passes from her to others. In the rione, Elena sees that this force affects everyone. People stop Lila with respectful secrecy, asking advice. Though Elena is the famous writer whose new novel is being advertised, beside Lila she feels like a decoration proving Lila's power.
