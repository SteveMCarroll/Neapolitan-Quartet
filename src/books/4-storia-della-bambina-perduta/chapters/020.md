## Summary

Elena and **Nino** remain constantly in motion during those years. They want to be present everywhere — to observe, study, understand, reason, testify — but above all to love each other. Police sirens, roadblocks, helicopters, and murdered bodies become the calendar of their relationship: weeks, months, the first year and a half, all counted from the night Elena had gone to Nino's room in Florence. They tell themselves that this was when their *vita vera* — their "true life" — began, and its miraculous glow survives even amid daily horrors.

They are in Rome just after Aldo Moro's kidnapping. Nino is supposed to present a colleague's book on southern politics and geography, but the discussion turns almost entirely to Moro. Part of the audience erupts when Nino argues that Moro himself helped expose the state's worst face by identifying his corrupt party with the state in order to shield it from punishment. Even his claim that defending institutions means making them transparent and just does not calm the room. Elena sees him turn pale and pulls him away as soon as she can. They retreat into each other like into a shining armor.

Soon afterward Elena has her own bad evening in Ferrara, after Moro's body has been found. She calls the kidnappers assassins, but the far-left audience rejects the word: *assassini sono i fascisti* — "assassins are the fascists." Attacked and mocked, Elena freezes, feeling politically incapable and dragged back to her origins. Nino nearly fights a man in the audience, but again only their couple matters: dinner, wine, sex, and the conviction that no criticism can truly touch them.
