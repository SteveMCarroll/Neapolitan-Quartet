## Summary

In the last month of pregnancy, everything becomes hard for **Elena**. **Nino** rarely appears, and she takes his absence as proof that she is ugly and no longer desired. Her swollen body makes her feel like the bitter, frightening version of her mother she used to dread. That persecutory mother seems to act through her: Elena becomes irritable, shouts often, and treats phone calls from **Pietro**, publishers, and newspapers as conspiracies against her exhaustion.

She also worsens with **Dede** and especially **Elsa**, whose teacher calls her sly and violent. Elena sees herself and her daughters as a miserable trio of females and imagines Nino preferring **Eleonora**, Albertino, and Lidia. At night she even hopes the new baby will unexpectedly be a boy who resembles Nino and wins his love.

The earthquake's aftereffects unsettle her: tunnels and viaducts now terrify her, and she stops killing ants because **Alfonso** says they sense catastrophe first. Lila's hints about drugs also take hold. Elena notices syringes, wants to fight **Marcello** and her brothers, and blurts out to her sick mother that **Peppe** and **Gianni** are involved in drugs. She then lectures Peppe, who answers that without his money their mother would already be dead. Finally Elena confronts **Elisa**, telling her to tell Marcello not to ruin their brothers. Elisa throws her out, calling her arrogant and defending Marcello as the man to whom they owe everything.
