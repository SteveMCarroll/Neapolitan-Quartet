## Summary

**Elena** cannot imagine going home, though **Imma** is half-dressed in the cold. She buys diapers, cleans the child, wraps her in her coat, and phones **Lila** to ask if she can come to lunch with the girls. Lila says yes; **Enzo** will not mind. Elena admits that something is wrong and says only that what Lila predicted has happened.

She collects **Elsa** and **Dede** from school while hiding from the possibility that **Nino** may appear. The girls are excited by Imma's presence, but the scene quickly turns tense. Elsa complains that Imma is ruining Elena's coat, curses at Dede in dialect, and then rejects the baby when she becomes annoying. Elena loses control, shouting so fiercely that all three daughters are frightened.

As they cross the city, Elena's disgust hardens into rage. The obscene images of the bathroom keep returning; her daughters' chatter, Imma's *lallazioni* — babbling — and her role as mother become intolerable. She imagines Nino excusing himself with polished, cultured arguments while dismissing her pain as *filistea* — philistine, narrow-minded. By the time she reaches Lila's building, Elena hates Nino more than she has ever hated anyone.
