## Summary

Only **Nunzia** and **Fernando** seem truly wounded by **Rino**'s death. **Pinuccia** mourns just enough, then appears reborn. Two weeks later she asks Elena to take over Nunzia's work in the house for the same pay. She is less efficient but more talkative, and **Dede**, **Elsa**, and **Imma** like her. She flatters Elena's clothes, shoes, importance, and film prospects, then asks for castoff dresses and alters them skillfully to fit her.

Pinuccia often talks about Ischia and **Bruno Soccavo**, saying she has been widowed twice. She also complains that Rino was a real husband only briefly, immature even in bed, and then turns on **Lila**. In Pinuccia's version, Rino invented the Cerullo shoes, while Lila exploited him, tricked **Stefano**, stole money, allied with **Michele Solara**, and ruined everyone. Elena knows these are old resentments, but is struck when Lila herself begins preserving some of the same falsehoods.

After Rino's death, Lila attributes all the shoe venture's genius to him. She freezes his life at the moment when their father's shop might have become a real business, erasing everything later that he did and did to her. This method seems to help her with **Tina** too. Tina becomes an *ologramma* — a hologram — present and absent at once. Lila shows photos, plays recordings of the little voice, and speaks of Tina in the present: Tina has, Tina does, Tina says. She is not calmer; she still shouts at everyone. But she needs Imma less, and one evening tells Elena's girls they are daughters of a lady and should persuade their mother to take them away.
