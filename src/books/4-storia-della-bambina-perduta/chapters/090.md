## Summary

Elena stands frozen on the sidewalk with her suitcase and reads the *Panorama* article. It runs four pages and shows some of the ugliest places in the neighborhood. The only photo of Elena is the beautiful one with **Tina**, where the shabby apartment background gives both figures a special refinement.

The article does not review her novel as fiction. Instead, it uses the book to describe what it calls *il feudo dei fratelli Solara* — "the fief of the Solara brothers." The neighborhood is presented as a border territory, possibly connected to the new organized Camorra. It says little about **Marcello** and focuses on **Michele**, portraying him as enterprising, unscrupulous, and politically opportunistic. It lists both legal and illegal businesses: bars, leather goods, shoe stores, minimarkets, nightclubs, loan-sharking, old cigarette smuggling, receiving stolen goods, drugs, and post-earthquake construction schemes.

Elena breaks into a cold sweat. In Florence she had used childhood and adolescence from a distance, treating Naples almost like an invented landscape. After returning to the neighborhood and seeing Lila daily, she had developed a *smania di realtà* — a craving for reality — and made the setting too recognizable. The photographs now prove where the book comes from. The article even brings in old neighborhood murders, especially that of **Manuela Solara**, and speculates about **Pasquale Peluso**. Elena insists to herself that she wrote a novel, not a report on real crimes.
