## Summary

In the winter of 2002, **Elena** still feels, despite ups and downs, that she has made a life. **Dede** and **Elsa** return from the United States every year, **Imma** comes back from Paris, and the four women briefly re-form a family in Turin. At Christmas, with partners and grandchildren around them, Elena watches her daughters' young bodies and tells herself she has been lucky: they belong to other places and languages, and they have escaped the obstacles that shaped her. Even when they credit their security to their father, Elena insists privately that she, without privileges, is *il fondamento* — the foundation — of their privileges.

Then the mood shifts. The girls bring their men to the shelf of Elena's books and begin leafing through them. **Elsa**, especially, reads aloud with affectionate mockery, exposing the dated ideologies, emphatic tones, and old-fashioned vocabulary of Elena's work. What begins as *sfottò affettuoso* ("fond teasing") starts to feel like a demonstration that all those celebrated volumes have little value.

Afterward Elena rereads her own books and senses their fragility. At the same time, the imagined manuscript of **Lila** grows in her mind into a threatening masterpiece, a possible *pietra di paragone* ("touchstone") that could reveal Elena's entire career as a mere struggle to change social class.
