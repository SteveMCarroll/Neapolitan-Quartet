## Summary

After the meeting in Rome, **Elena** sees **Nino** more coldly than ever. He has treated her as an old friend before whom he can display his ascent from schoolboy to parliamentarian. Even his compliment — that the two of them have both risen high — feels false: his eyes seem to say that she is now only a petitioner, proof of what she lost by losing him.

Driving away with **Imma**, Elena thinks Nino would have behaved differently in front of **Lila**. Only with Lila, she realizes, had he ever truly risked his ambitions. His attachments to **Nadia**, **Eleonora**, Elena, and other women all fit a logic of *relazioni utili* ("useful connections"). Lila, by contrast, had no social advantage to offer. What fascinated him must have been the *gratuità dell'intelligenza* — the free, purposeless brilliance — that she seemed to squander without submitting it to careers, status, or respectability.

That thought leads Elena back to Nadia and **Pasquale**, and then to **Dede**. Dede has just passed her exams brilliantly, yet seems ready to throw away every advantage by declaring herself to **Rino**. When Elena asks Imma about Rino, Imma says Dede likes him, while she herself likes "Papà." Elena sees in the child's adoration the social light Nino kept by escaping Lila — the light Nadia lost with Pasquale, and Dede might lose with Rino.
