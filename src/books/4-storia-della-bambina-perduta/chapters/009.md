## Summary

**Lila** explains that **Antonio** returned from Germany for **Manuela Solara**'s funeral, thin and almost unrecognizable, then settled near **Melina**, **Stefano**, and **Ada** and brought his German wife and three children to the neighborhood. The news reconnects distant pieces of **Elena**'s past and lightens the morning. She asks **Nino** to agree to only a few minutes at Piazza dei Martiri.

As they walk, Lila takes possession of Elena with her old intimacy, while Nino trails behind, unhappy. Lila vividly describes Antonio's blond wife and children, then lists the others waiting: **Carmen**, **Enzo**, **Alfonso**, **Marisa**, and the children. Elena realizes that news of her separation and affair will spread through the neighborhood and reach her parents, but instead of fear she feels pleasure. She wants to be publicly seen with Nino, to replace the Elena-Pietro couple with Nino-Elena.

Lila links arms with her, but the old physical charge is gone. Elena thinks Lila is trying to revive adolescent loves and old passions, yet it all feels like a harmless museum. At the Solara shoe shop, everyone looks first at Nino but greets Elena as if she has come alone. Marisa scolds her brother for neglecting their parents; he mutters that he has his own troubles. Nino exchanges restrained nods with Antonio, Enzo, Alfonso, and Carmen, then smokes apart. For Elena, his very presence proves the new fact of her life; she wants only to take him away.
