## Summary

In Montpellier, **Elena** remembers almost nothing of the city itself; what remains is the feeling of crossing a boundary. Unlike her earlier trip to Paris with **Franco**, this journey with **Nino** makes Italy, Naples, and even the neighborhood seem like tiny fragments of the world. She feels her old limits break, and sees wifehood and motherhood as *angusto* — narrow, confining. From this new height, **Lila**'s refusal to leave Naples looks not like strength but mental smallness.

Elena and Nino live as lovers in separate hotel rooms that become one each night. He moves brilliantly through the academic conference, charming people in English and French, though he panics before his own speech and needs Elena's praise to recover. Afterward, an American academic invites him to a prestigious table, leaving Elena to talk with **Colombe** and **Augustin**, a French couple who have also left spouses and children.

Colombe's insistence that children are *elastici* — resilient — briefly reassures Elena, until her cold irritation with her own son unsettles her. The conversation returns Elena abruptly to **Dede**, **Elsa**, and **Pietro**. Sick with longing, she calls home near midnight. Pietro answers, says the girls are asleep, then wounds her: they no longer want to be her daughters, and neither he, the girls, nor **Adele** want to see her again.
