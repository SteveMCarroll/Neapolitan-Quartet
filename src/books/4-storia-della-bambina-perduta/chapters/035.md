## Summary

Elena answers Lila's question with an embarrassed little laugh: of course she will not have a child with **Nino**. But in the street she has to reassure **Elsa**, especially, while **Dede** stays darkly silent, that she will have no more children; her girls are the only ones.

For two days Elena has headaches and cannot sleep. With a few words placed *ad arte* — artfully, with intent — **Lila** has thrown disorder into an encounter Elena had thought beautiful. Elena tells herself Lila is incorrigible, always able to complicate her life. The disturbance is not only the anxiety she has stirred in Dede and Elsa. Lila has struck a hidden point in Elena: the urge toward motherhood she first felt years earlier when holding **Silvia**'s child **Mirko** at Mariarosa's house. That desire was not simply for a baby; it was for a specific baby, a child like Mirko, a child of Nino. **Pietro** and the births of Dede and Elsa had never stilled it. It had returned whenever Elena saw Mirko, and especially when Nino told her **Eleonora** was pregnant.

Elena interprets Lila's question as part of Lila's old game: pretending warmth, then nudging people — **Enzo**, **Carmen**, **Antonio**, **Alfonso**, **Michele**, **Gigliola** — just enough to unsettle and spoil them. Now, Elena fears, Lila wants to act on her and on Nino the same way. In via Tasso, alone or with others, the question keeps moving inside her until it becomes her own: will she have a child with Nino?
