## Summary

**Elena** cannot know what offended **Lila** in *Un'amicizia* ("A Friendship"): a detail, or the entire book. To Elena, its strength was that it was linear. Disguised as fiction, it summarized their two lives from the loss of the dolls to the loss of **Tina**. At first Elena thinks Lila was wounded by the ending, where Elena imagines that Lila, by showing off **Imma** to **Nino**, became distracted and lost Tina. What moves readers in fiction, Elena reflects, can become *un'infamia* — an infamy — to someone who hears the echo of real suffering.

Later Elena changes her mind. Perhaps the true offense was the way she used the dolls. She heightened the childhood trauma of their disappearance into the cellar and exploited the fact that one doll and the missing daughter shared a name. She pushed readers to connect the lost fake daughters of childhood with the lost real daughter of adulthood. Lila may have found that cynical and dishonest.

Elena admits these are only hypotheses. Sometimes she feels guilty and understands Lila; sometimes she detests her for cutting her off in old age, when they need closeness. She sees Lila's self-erasure as another punishment: Lila wants Elena to give what Lila could not give, while Elena has written for months to give Lila a form that will not *smarginarsi* — blur at the edges — and also to beat, calm, and console herself.
