## Summary

**Elena** rushes home with **Imma** behind her, calling for **Dede** and **Elsa**. She finds Dede on the bed, her eyes burned by tears, and first imagines that **Rino** has rejected her. Imma begins excitedly talking about her father, but Dede snaps at her in dialect, then explodes at Elena: Elena knows nothing, understands nothing, and thinks only of herself and the *stronzate* — the crap — she writes.

Dede throws Elena a sheet of squared paper. It is a letter from Elsa. Elsa tells Dede that feelings cannot be commanded: Rino has loved her for a long time, and she has gradually fallen in love with him. She knows she is causing pain, but renouncing him would not fix anything. Then she turns almost playfully to Elena, announcing that she is leaving school. Elena's *culto dello studio* ("cult of study") has always seemed foolish to her; books do not make people good, good people sometimes make good books. Rino is good though he has read none; her father is good and has written excellent ones. Elena is pointedly absent from that logic.

Elena becomes furious, blaming Dede for not seeing that Elsa meant to steal what mattered to her. She runs downstairs and tells **Lila** that Rino has taken Elsa. When Elena threatens the police because Elsa is underage, Lila first laughs incredulously, then dares her to go. Back upstairs, Elena forces Dede for information and discovers that Elsa has stolen her money and jewelry, including **Immacolata**'s bracelet.
