## Summary

Sundays are worst. **Lila** stays home while festive voices come from outside. Elena urges her to walk in the center or see the sea, but Lila refuses; when **Enzo** offers to go instead, she tells them all to leave her in peace so she can bathe and breathe. Elena walks with Enzo, her daughters, and sometimes **Gennaro**, now often called Rino. Enzo speaks in sparse, obscure phrases: without **Tina**, money has no purpose; stealing children is a sign of filthy times; after Tina was born a light bulb lit in his head, and now it has gone out.

Elena asks about Lila's physical health, but Enzo knows almost nothing about her body. He loves her like an idol, with prudence and reverence. On the rare Sundays when Lila does go out, she strides badly dressed and wild-haired, with fierce looks that make everyone avert their eyes. To the rione she is a terrible woman carrying disaster's power.

These walks inevitably cross the Solaras. **Marcello**, **Michele**, **Gigliola**, **Elisa**, and the children perform a Sunday ritual of church, stalls, and the local library. When Elena is alone, they are cordial; with Lila present, everyone pretends not to see anyone. One morning **Elsa** breaks the rule by greeting Michele's children, forcing a stop. Michele then mockingly praises Elena and her daughters as proof that study makes people good, unlike ignorant, bad Lina. He grabs Elena's wrist, and when she pulls away, her mother's bracelet breaks off in his hand. Michele promises to have it repaired. Lila says Elena defends herself even less than before: she will never see the bracelet again.
