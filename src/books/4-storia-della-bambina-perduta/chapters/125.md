## Summary

When Elena tells **Lila** about the package and shows her the shining bracelet, Lila warns her not to wear it and not to let her daughters wear it either. Lila has returned home badly weakened: one flight of stairs breaks her breath, she takes pills and gives herself injections, and she looks as if she has been in the realm of the dead. She speaks of the bracelet as if it came from there too.

The Solara murders and Lila's emergency hospitalization have merged in Elena's memory, their blood mixed with hers in the chaos of that Sunday. But whenever Elena tries to discuss the execution outside the church, Lila reacts with contempt: they were *gente di merda* ("shitty people"), and people like them die murdered. Elena tries to draw her into a sense of shared past — they knew the brothers as boys, Lila worked for Michele — but Lila refuses any softening. Behind her restrained words Elena senses a fiercer rage: if the Solaras took Tina, they deserved far worse; if they did not, they deserved death anyway.

Lila never says this aloud. Instead, the brothers' disappearance simply encourages her to walk more often through the neighborhood. During her long convalescence she passes the closed Solara pastry bar, its mourning sign fading, and notes with satisfaction that it is still shut. One day, at the ugly stripped corner, she remarks that when their relatives died the Solaras covered the neighborhood with crosses and Madonnas; now nobody mourns them. Then, abruptly, she says she no longer wants to work.
