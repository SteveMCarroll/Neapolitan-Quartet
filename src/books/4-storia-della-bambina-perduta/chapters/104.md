## Summary

The article's conciliatory outcome depresses **Lila**. She does not say so directly, but **Elena** sees disappointment on her face: Lila had overestimated the power of the alphabet, writing, and books. Elena thinks that in those weeks Lila finally ended her childhood faith in such things. Lila withdraws, leaves **Tina** with Elena more often, sometimes sends up **Gennaro**, and is no longer useful when Elena needs help.

When Elena asks about the children, Lila irritably tells her to call her mother. So **Nunzia** arrives: older, submissive, uncomfortable, but efficient. **Dede** and **Elsa** treat her with cruel superiority. Dede, entering adolescence and disgusted by her changing face, complains that the old woman spits when she talks and should not sleep in their house. Elsa is equally vicious in a colder way, saying Nunzia smells nicely of a corpse.

Only **Imma** attaches herself to Nunzia, because she imitates Tina even in affection. Nunzia is tender to her real granddaughter and brusque with the pretend one, until Elena tells her to be kinder. At the end of the week Nunzia awkwardly asks how much she will be paid. Elena realizes she had foolishly assumed the help was a family favor. Nunzia explains that her husband is ill, **Rino** has been fired, and they are *senza una lira* — without a penny. She and Lila, however, barely acknowledge each other, and Lila keeps worsening.
