## Summary

For Lila, going out and wandering becomes the answer to every tension. More and more often she leaves in the morning and returns at night, ignoring **Enzo**, who cannot manage the clients alone, **Rino**, and even promises she has made to Elena about watching the girls. She is unreliable now: one setback is enough for her to drop everything without considering the consequences.

**Carmen** claims that Lila takes refuge in the old Doganella cemetery, where she has chosen a child's grave as a place to think about Tina, who has no grave of her own. Carmen says the dead at least have a stone, dates of birth and death, while Tina will remain forever with only a birth date, no conclusion, no fixed point where her mother can sit and calm herself. Elena distrusts Carmen's taste for *fantasie mortuarie* ("deathly fantasies"). She imagines instead that Lila walks Naples to numb a pain that still poisons her, or that she has decided, in her extreme way, to devote herself to nothing and no one.

Elena fears Lila may snap at Enzo, Rino, Elena, the girls, or a stranger. Yet when Elena is busy and hears Lila leave, she often sighs with relief: Lila will not come upstairs to provoke, needle the girls, belittle **Imma**, or hurt Elena. Elena begins thinking insistently that it is time to leave. Staying in the neighborhood no longer makes sense for her, **Dede**, **Elsa**, or Imma. Lila herself now tells her to go, implying Elena's presence has been only an intellectual's performance and has helped neither Lila nor their birthplace.
