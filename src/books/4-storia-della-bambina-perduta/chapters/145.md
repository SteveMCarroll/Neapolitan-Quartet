## Summary

For a long time it is unclear what **Enzo** is accused of. **Lila** drops her hostility toward him and devotes herself to his defense, furious only that the state will not treat her as his wife because she never formalized their bond and never separated from **Stefano**. She spends heavily to support him through unofficial channels.

**Elena** asks **Nino** for help despite **Marisa**'s warning that he helps no one. He does try, perhaps to impress **Imma** or indirectly display power to Lila, but even he can offer only shifting rumors. **Nadia** has named Enzo, reviving his old ties to **Pasquale** and the workers-students collective. Investigators try to attach him to actions against NATO officers, to **Bruno Soccavo**'s murder, and even to the killing of the Solara brothers with **Antonio** and Pasquale.

Elena reflects that the political world of their youth is collapsing. Old labels — Marxist, Leninist, Maoist, *operaista* ("workerist") — now look obsolete or criminal. People like Nino and **Armando** adapt; Nadia washes her conscience through denunciations; Pasquale says nothing; Enzo speaks carefully, affirming his communist feelings while rejecting the charges.

Lila fights with intelligence, bad temper, and expensive lawyers. Eventually the case weakens, and after almost two years Enzo comes home. But the neighborhood has already branded him, and Lila too, as dangerous terrorists clever enough to escape justice. Their company, Basic Sight, cannot recover. In spring 1992 they sell it cheaply, separate as business partners and as a couple, and Enzo leaves for Milan, asking Elena to stay close to Lila.
