## Summary

The babies give **Elena** and **Lila** a new reason to be together. They phone, walk the newborns, and talk endlessly — supposedly about the girls, though the comparison of **Imma** and **Tina** becomes a new form of intimacy between them. They trade information about feeding, diapers, creams, and clothes in a virtuous competition. Lila buys for Imma whatever suits Tina, and Elena does the same when she can. One day Elena realizes Tina has the same name as her lost doll. Lila says she did not do it on purpose; Elena says the doll was beautiful, and Lila answers that her daughter is more beautiful.

Spring approaches, but **Immacolata** worsens. Elena asks **Nino** whether his medical contacts might secure a private room in the public hospital. He refuses favors and condemns a country where even a hospital bed seems to require a lodge or the Camorra. In the end Immacolata chooses the clinic's comforts, and **Marcello** astonishingly brings in a hospital specialist, who improves her care.

When the illness worsens again, Immacolata gathers strength for two contradictory acts. First she forces **Peppe** and **Gianni** to accept jobs Lila has found for them, threatening to persecute them from the dead. Then she makes Marcello swear he will marry **Elisa** before she dies. Having used both Lila's and Marcello's power, she becomes serenely happy. Soon after, she tells Elena she trusts her, falls asleep, and enters a coma. Elena is present with Imma when the death-rattle stops. Wearing the old bracelet her mother gave her, she feels that she and the baby are what remains alive and healthy of Immacolata.
