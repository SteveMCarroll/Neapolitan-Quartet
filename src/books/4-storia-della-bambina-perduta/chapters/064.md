## Summary

**Elisa** later tells Elena what happened in a tone of triumph: at nine, **Marcello** arrived at the hospital with a chief physician he had personally fetched, and **Immacolata** was moved by ambulance to a private clinic in Capodimonte. There, Elisa says, she is treated "like a queen"; relatives can stay as long as they want, and **Vittorio** even has a bed for the night. Elisa adds that they will pay, then warns Elena to tell **Nino** who he is dealing with and to tell **Lila** that Marcello will kill her if she insults Elisa again.

Elena tells Lila none of this. Instead she becomes more affectionate, phoning often to thank her and waiting for Lila's own delivery. Meanwhile the old and new obligations pile up. Elena is physically tied to **Imma**, misses **Dede** and **Elsa**, and has **Pietro** bring them back. Elsa first pretends to love the baby, then calls her ugly; Dede tries to show she is a better mother than Elena and nearly endangers the newborn.

Pietro offers to stay and help, but Nino's jealous phone calls force Elena to send him away. Nino then disappears into work and politics, leaving Elena alone. Visiting Immacolata is hardest: Elena brings Imma by taxi. The clinic's comforts truly help her mother, who enjoys the large room, private bathroom, and new granddaughter, then drifts into memories of childhood and death. Elena feels, perhaps for the first time, like her mother's favorite daughter.
