## Summary

The weeks grow complicated. **Elena** works to promote **Nino**'s essay collection, while **Pietro** brings her his own new book, dedicated embarrassingly to her as the woman who taught him to love with pain. At Pietro's celebration in Florence she faces her former in-laws' hostility and Nino's jealousy: he resents the dedication, Pietro's success, and any sign that Elena values another man's work.

Elena begins to see how exhausting her life with Nino is. He cannot bear Pietro's name or memories of **Franco**, yet he divides himself between Elena and **Eleonora** as if that were normal. When Elena runs into Nino with Eleonora and their children, she greets them pointedly and mentions her pregnancy; later Nino scolds her for being provocative. Elena realizes how *succube* — submissive — she has become, cooking, washing, listening, waiting, and trying not to inconvenience him.

To reassure her that she alone is his real wife, Nino takes Elena, **Dede**, and **Elsa** to lunch at his parents' house. His mother **Lidia** treats Elena like a daughter-in-law, and **Marisa** and **Alfonso** are there with their children. The tension is **Donato**. Old, shabby, obscene, and self-pitying, he tries to reclaim importance by praising Elena as a great writer and claiming his poems awakened her literary vocation. Elena kindly confirms part of it, making him glow. Nino later rebukes her for encouraging him. Elena watches Nino and wonders whether he too will age into his father's bitterness.
