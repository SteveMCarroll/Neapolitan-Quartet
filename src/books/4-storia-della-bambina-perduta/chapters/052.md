## Summary

**Lila** keeps talking, and older **Elena** marks this as the only time Lila ever tried to explain the inner feeling of the world in which she lived. Lila says she once thought these episodes were passing attacks, like a childhood illness. Now she understands that the terror has always been there, waiting in the gap between ordinary things.

She recalls earlier ruptures: the copper pot that seemed to split; the 1958 fireworks, when the colors felt sharp enough to cut **Rino** open; the night sky at Ischia, beautiful to others but to her like rotten egg and gelatinous darkness; the factory of **Bruno Soccavo**, where animal bones seemed to break under her fingers. Her whole life, she says, has been an effort to contain such moments. She has hidden behind men in sequence — **Marcello**, **Stefano**, **Michele**, **Nino**, **Enzo** — but protection is only a word.

Lila also folds other people into this vision. **Alfonso** made her anxious because his thread seemed ready to break; she says she found **Michele**'s outline and pulled, tangling him with Alfonso. The earthquake confirms what she feared: nothing holds. Even love does not hold, not for men or children. **Gennaro** makes her feel guilty; the unborn child is a cutting responsibility. She says **Maestra Oliviero** was right, she is bad, and begs Elena not to abandon her if she says cruel things.
