## Summary

In 1995 **Elena** leaves Naples for good, just when people are talking about the city's rebirth. She no longer believes in those resurrections: the new buildings around Scampia, Poggioreale, and the Arenaccia seem to her only *cipria della modernità* — "powder of modernity" — spread over a corrupt face. Time has accelerated into contracts, book deadlines, and publication dates. **Dede** and **Elsa** are already studying in Boston, close to **Pietro** at Harvard, and **Imma** wants to follow her sisters out of Italy.

Elena rents a house in Turin, on the Po, and both she and Imma improve. From there she writes about Naples more lucidly, stripping away any duty to defend it. But by the early 2000s her fame and income have faded; newspapers, television, and publishing no longer need her as they once did. Alone after Imma leaves for Paris, Elena feels old, bored, and professionally displaced.

She begins visiting Naples more often, though she avoids almost everyone except **Lila**, and sometimes avoids Lila too. In 2006, trapped by rain in a hotel, Elena writes a short rione story about **Tina**. It is published as *Un'amicizia* ("A Friendship") and succeeds brilliantly. Elena detests it: only two years earlier, after **Gigliola** was found dead, Lila had made her promise never to write about her. At a Naples presentation Elena fears Lila will appear and expose her, but the event goes well. Later she phones Lila repeatedly. Lila never answers again.
