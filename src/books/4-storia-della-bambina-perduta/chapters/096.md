## Summary

Elena returns home and does not go to **Lila**. She is sure Lila knows about the elections, the votes, and the Solaras lurking behind **Carmen**'s action, and that Lila tells her things only *con il contagocce* — with an eyedropper, bit by bit — for her own purposes. Instead Elena calls the publisher, reports the rumored lawsuit and **Antonio**'s warning, and says she is very worried. The director promises to ask the legal office to investigate, but adds that the controversy will help the book. Elena thinks it will not help her; she should never have returned to the neighborhood.

Days pass with no news from the publisher. Then the formal notice of the lawsuit arrives at her home like a stab. Carmen is demanding that Elena and the publisher withdraw the book from sale and pay enormous damages for injuring the memory of Carmen's mother, Giuseppina. Elena is overwhelmed by the document's legal force: its heading, stamps, tax seals, and official language make the law seem terrifying.

This time she runs to Lila. Lila mocks her: Elena wanted the law, and now the law has arrived. Her advice is to make a *bordello* — a racket, a public mess — by telling newspapers what is happening. Elena is afraid the wealthy Solaras can ruin her. Lila says Elena must hit their money: the more she writes about their filth, the more she damages their business. Elena suddenly understands that Lila has assigned her the magical power they once gave the author of *Little Women*. Perhaps this is why Lila wanted her back in the neighborhood. The next day the publisher calls cheerfully: he has written an article about the lawsuit for the *Corriere della Sera*.
