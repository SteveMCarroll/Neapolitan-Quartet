## Summary

Two unexpected opportunities arrive at once. **Nino**'s research institute is invited to New York for important work, and a small Boston publisher brings out Elena's booklet. After hesitation, arguments, and quarrels, the possibilities become a two-week trip to the United States.

The problem is **Dede** and **Elsa**. Elena is already juggling articles, translations, debates, notes for a new book, and childcare. Usual sitters are not enough; **Pietro** cannot take the girls; **Adele** is estranged, **Mariarosa** has disappeared, **Immacolata** is weakened, and **Elisa** is hostile. Pietro suggests **Lila**, reminding Elena that Lila once left **Gennaro** with her for months and is therefore *in obbligo* — under obligation. Elena hesitates between two ideas of Lila: someone who might torment the girls as spoiled dolls, and the only person who would devote herself completely to making them well. Need wins. Lila answers at once that Elena's daughters are more than her own daughters and she should bring them whenever she likes.

In May 1980 Elena leaves for America with Nino, guilty but thrilled. The trip is exhausting and expensive, yet exhilarating: she feels limitless, able to stretch herself across the world, and happier than she has ever been.

On return, Elena is sure she is pregnant. She had suspected it before leaving and secretly savoured the possibility. When she collects the girls, radiant, she nearly tells Lila, who greets her warmly and praises her beauty. Elena gives gifts to Lila, **Enzo**, and Gennaro and describes America in detail, omitting Nino. Just as Elena is about to confess, Lila says she has good news too: she is pregnant.
