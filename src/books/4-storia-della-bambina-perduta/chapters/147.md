## Summary

After **Elsa** leaves, **Rino** does not leave with her. To Elena's surprise, he remains in her apartment, terrified of falling back into the physical and moral poverty from which he believes she rescued him. He keeps the room that once belonged to **Dede** and Elsa and turns himself into Elena's helper: driver, porter, errand-runner, and general *factotum* — the person who does everything. When he needs money, he asks with affection and no embarrassment.

Sometimes Elena, irritated by his dependence, reminds him that he has duties toward his mother. Rino understands, disappears for a while, and then returns discouraged. **Lila**, he says, is never at home; or else the empty apartment depresses him; or she is sitting at the computer and does not even say hello. This last detail catches Elena's attention only weakly at first. Lila is writing? What could she be writing?

Elena is nearly fifty, professionally successful, publishing and selling a great deal, and reading and writing have become a job that weighs on her. Her first reaction is almost dismissive: in Lila's place she would be lying in the sun on a beach. Then she tells herself that if writing helps Lila, so much the better, and lets the thought go.
