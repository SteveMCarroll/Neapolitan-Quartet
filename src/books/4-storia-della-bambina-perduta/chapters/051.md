## Summary

**Elena** is terrified by the earthquake, but she is startled to see that **Lila** is even more terrified. In a few seconds Lila seems stripped of the calculating, tactical woman Elena knows and returned to older moments of panic: the New Year's Eve fireworks of 1958, or the time at Bruno Soccavo's factory when she thought she was dying and wanted Elena to take **Gennaro**. Elena, by contrast, still keeps a center: **Dede** and **Elsa** are safely in Florence with their father; she can hope **Nino**, her parents, **Elisa**, and her brothers are alive.

Lila cannot do this. She trembles, touches her pregnant belly, and feels **Enzo** and Gennaro as if they have lost all connection to her. Elena tries to anchor her by pointing out familiar people in the street — **Carmen**, **Antonio**, the Carracci circle, and **Marcello** driving away with Elisa and Elena's parents — but the familiar faces only frighten Lila more.

When Marcello's car mounts the curb and speeds off, Lila says the car and Marcello have *smarginato* — lost their margins. She explains that the outlines of things and people are fragile, like cotton thread, and can break, spill, and mix. For her, the composed world is a false covering over a terrifying true world where sensations dissolve into each other and nothing can finally be said to be simply itself.
