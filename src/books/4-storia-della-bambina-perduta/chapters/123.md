## Summary

Elena pauses to describe one of those historical moments when what has always seemed like a permanent background — a political party, a faith, a monument, even the ordinary people in daily life — suddenly collapses. That period feels exactly like that: strain piled on strain, fear on fear. She imagines herself like a figure in a novel or painting standing on a cliff or the prow of a ship before a storm that somehow does not touch her.

Her phone rings constantly. Because she lives in what she calls the Solara *feudo* ("fief"), she is drawn into an endless chain of spoken and written explanations. Her sister **Elisa**, newly widowed, becomes terrified and childlike, wanting Elena beside her day and night because she is convinced the killers will return for her and her son.

Above all, Elena must look after **Lila**. On that same Sunday Lila is torn away from the neighborhood, from **Gennaro**, **Enzo**, and work, and placed in doctors' hands. She is weak, hallucinating, and losing blood. They discover a fibroid uterus, operate, and remove it. Once in the hospital she wakes suddenly and cries that **Tina** has come out of her belly again and is now taking revenge on everyone, even Lila herself. For an instant she seems to believe Tina killed the Solara brothers.
