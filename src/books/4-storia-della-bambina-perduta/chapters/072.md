## Summary

At home **Nino**, **Dede**, and **Elsa** stage a comic ritual: they drag Elena to her desk, order her to work, and shut the door, scolding if she comes out. When Nino has time, he is good with the older girls. But he never truly bonds with **Imma**. He plays with her as a doll for Dede and Elsa, even taking the three girls to the Floridiana, but Elena senses he leaves the older girls to mother the baby while he charms the real mothers in the park.

Elena has grown used to Nino's seductive tic and to the way women brighten near him. What begins to disturb her is not simply possible betrayal, but the pattern of women helping him. After Imma's birth his career improves through female mediation: a newspaper column, a conference, a magazine board, an American foundation. Elena herself has helped him publish, and she remembers that even his school prestige began with **Professoressa Galiani**.

At dinners, Nino gives women intense attention while ignoring their husbands. He genuinely loves hearing women seek themselves, yet he keeps all his own time and freedom. Elena jokes that he wants to liberate other men's women but not his own; his answer echoes old conflicts with **Pietro**. Nino then hires a woman named Silvana, through his mother, to help with house and children, but treats the expense as permission not to worry. His praise of Elena's writing also loses force because he praises every woman. She no longer feels unique. When she asks whether he has ever met a "bitch," hoping he will say **Eleonora**, he answers seriously: **Lina**.
