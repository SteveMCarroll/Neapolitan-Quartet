## Summary

Elena is frightened and hurt. **Carmen**, of all people, has done this to her? In seconds the thrill of success collapses: guilt over neglecting Imma combines with the fear that legal action might take away her joy, prestige, and money. Elena tells **Lila** she wants to speak to Carmen immediately. Lila advises against it, but Elena senses Lila knows more than she has said and goes anyway.

At the gas station Carmen is absent, and her husband Roberto is embarrassed. He says only that Carmen has gone with the children to relatives in Giugliano. Elena checks Carmen's house, then, in the heat, goes looking for **Antonio**, sure he knows something. She finds him at the barber.

Antonio avoids her question at first, complaining about school and about his children being criticized for speaking German or dialect while not being taught Italian. Then he quietly says he wants to say goodbye: he is going back to Germany. Elena asks whether **Michele** is forcing him out. Antonio answers that Michele commands and he obeys. He speaks of recurring *esaurimento nervoso* — nervous breakdown — and of taking **Melina** with him because **Ada** already has too many troubles and because he wants to watch in his mother what he may become.

Finally he warns Elena to leave too. Lila believes the two women together are invincible, but they are not, and Antonio can no longer help. The Solaras are enraged because Lila has shifted votes toward the Communists. **Marcello** and Michele see Lila behind everything, especially Elena. The lawsuit exists, and Carmen's lawyers are the Solara lawyers.
