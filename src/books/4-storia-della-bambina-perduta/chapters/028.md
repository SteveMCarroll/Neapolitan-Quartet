## Summary

Looking back, Elena knows she reacted excessively to the news that **Eleonora** was seven months pregnant, but at the time it feels like the worst injury **Nino** could inflict. She thinks of **Lila**, of the look Lila and **Carmen** exchanged earlier, and wonders whether **Antonio** had discovered the pregnancy too. Did they know? Had Lila decided to ration Elena's pain?

Elena nearly collapses. With **Mariarosa** out walking with the girls, only **Franco** is home. Elena opens the door and, in a kind of *rantolo* ("death-rattle" or hoarse gasp), tells him to throw Nino out. Franco instead quiets Nino, steadies Elena, takes her to the kitchen, and listens. He admits Nino has lied for years and behaved abjectly, but asks why, if exposed, he came all the way from Naples to confess and beg. What if he truly loves Elena but can only love her like this?

Mariarosa returns with **Dede** and **Elsa**, who are quickly charmed by Nino. Franco urges Elena to take what is possible within the situation; Mariarosa urges her to imagine Eleonora abandoned with one child and another coming, and even to establish a relationship with her. Elena feels wounded and misunderstood. Watching Nino regain her daughters' sympathy, she pulls him outside for a long walk through Sant'Ambrogio. She says she must learn to do without him, but needs time. He says he cannot live without her. She sends him away, unable to answer when he asks what she will do.
