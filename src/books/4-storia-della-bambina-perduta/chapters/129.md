## Summary

Elena enters a period of constant strategizing. Her daughters need stability, and she must push their fathers to take more responsibility. **Nino** remains the hardest problem. He phones sometimes and coos at **Imma**, who answers in monosyllables. Then he predictably runs for office on the Socialist Party list and sends Elena a letter asking for votes, with a flyer showing his handsome photo and a biography. He has underlined the line saying he has three children — Albertino, Lidia, and Imma — and asks Elena to show it to the child.

Elena does not vote for him, but she shows Imma the flyer. When Nino is elected, Imma treasures both the flyer and his later triumphant letter as proof that her father exists. Elena is glad of the girl's pride but angry that Nino treats his daughter like a *coccarda* ("rosette"), something to use and put away.

**Pietro** has been more reliable with money and visits, but now he wants to move to the United States after breaking with Doriana. Elena fears **Dede** and **Elsa** will also be left fatherless. They do well in school but do not truly fit in, and they seem happy mainly with **Rino**. Elena thinks she must leave Naples — perhaps for Rome, perhaps Florence. The urgency sharpens when **Lila** comes upstairs in a quarrelsome state and asks if Elena told Dede to stop seeing Gennaro. Elena says she only warned Dede not to cling to him, but Lila forces the issue: does Elena think her son is not good enough? Dede, Lila says, is in love with him.
