## Summary

**Lila** increasingly speaks to **Elena** with spite, especially about Elena's daughters. She tells her **Dede** has had her period, using the vulgar word *marchese* (menstruation), and suggests Elena is never present enough to know how the girls talk or how cruelly they treat Nunzia. Elena feels Lila is trying to diminish her children and her as a mother, but one criticism troubles her: Lila says **Imma** has an eye tic and feels orphaned of a father, perhaps unsure she even has a mother.

Elena begins watching Imma and sees real suffering. Beside **Tina**'s brilliance, verbal ease, and ability to charm everyone, Imma seems to grey out. In a coloring game, Tina suggests proper colors; Imma cannot distinguish a suggestion from an order and gives in unhappily. Elena tries praising Imma more and restraining her own enthusiasm for Tina, but artificial praise cannot heal the wound.

Elena connects Imma's distress to fatherlessness. **Pietro** phones and visits Dede and **Elsa**, sometimes kindly including Imma, and Imma hovers, wanting to play at being his daughter. **Enzo** also tries to include her whenever he can. Still, things feel wrong. One day under Lila's office desk, Tina invents a rescue game, but Imma angrily says she has no prince and will not be saved. Elena is hurt by how suddenly play becomes confession.
