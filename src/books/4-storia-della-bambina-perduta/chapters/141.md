## Summary

Back in the neighborhood, **Elena** tells **Lila** what she has proposed to **Elsa** and **Rino**. The conversation is cold, almost contractual. Lila asks whether Elena will keep them at her house; Elena says yes. Lila says that if it suits Elena, it suits her too. They discuss expenses, each insisting she can pay, and settle the matter without warmth.

Lila asks how **Dede** has taken it. Elena says well: Dede is leaving in a couple of weeks to visit **Pietro**. Lila asks Elena to tell Dede to come say goodbye, then, when Elena doubts she will, to greet Pietro for her instead. Suddenly Elena feels a heavy grief and says that in a few days she has lost two daughters.

Lila immediately rejects the phrase. Elena has lost nothing, she says; if anything, she has acquired a son. Elena, however, accuses Lila of having pushed everything in this direction. Lila seems honestly disoriented and says she does not know what Elena means. Elena presses on: Lila always has to *sobillare, urtare, pungolare* — incite, jolt, prod. Lila asks whether Elena now wants to blame her for what Elena's daughters do.

Elena leaves, saying only that she is tired. Yet for days and weeks she cannot stop imagining that Lila cannot bear the fragile balances of Elena's life and therefore tries to break them. Since **Tina**'s disappearance, Elena thinks, this impulse has grown worse: one move, then another, and the consequences spread.
