## Summary

The article proves to be another leap in **Elena**'s public standing. Its circulation joins together the separate pieces of her reputation: novelist, writer on labor struggles, critic of women's condition, and now a voice against the degradation of Naples. Older readers, newer readers, and the much larger audience of her recent success merge. Her first two books are reprinted, the third keeps selling, and talk of a film becomes more concrete.

The article also brings trouble. Elena is summoned by the carabinieri and questioned by the finance police. Right-wing local papers smear her as divorced, feminist, communist, and a supporter of terrorists. Anonymous callers threaten her and her daughters in obscene dialect. Yet she is less shaken than she was after the *Panorama* article and **Carmen**'s complaint. Anxiety now feels almost inseparable from writing; she has legal backing, support in left-wing newspapers, crowded public events, and the conviction that she is right.

What most calms her is the Solaras' restraint. Her visibility makes **Marcello** and **Michele** invisible: they do not sue, do not attack, and greet her coldly but respectfully before the authorities. Investigations open and then stall. The real damage is familial. **Elisa**, little **Silvio**, and Elena's father cut her out in practice. Marcello remains polite, telling her his house is still open; when Elena says Elisa hung up on her, he smiles that Elisa is the mistress of the house.
