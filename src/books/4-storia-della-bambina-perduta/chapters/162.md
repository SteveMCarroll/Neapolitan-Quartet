## Summary

In Naples, during a rainy week, **Elena** writes the story she will call *Un'amicizia* ("A Friendship"). She knows she is violating an unwritten pact with **Lila**, who has forbidden her not only to write about her but also to use the people and events of the neighborhood. Still, Elena persuades herself that if the result is good, Lila may be grateful: perhaps Elena will have said in her name what Lila could not say even to herself.

Elena recognizes the presumption behind this. Writers act as if they have received an *investitura* — an authorization or calling — when in fact they have given it to themselves. She writes quickly a story she has imagined for years while both hoping and fearing that Lila was writing it: a story about herself, Lila, **Tina**, and **Imma**, because everything Elena attributes to Lila has always seemed more significant than anything that came from herself.

When the book is finished, Elena could call Lila and read it aloud, but fear stops her. The book appears and wins the strongest approval Elena has felt in years; it revives her career and her old sales. Yet Lila refuses every contact. Elena phones, emails, goes to the neighborhood, and speaks with **Rino**, but Lila never appears. Elena must accept that their friendship is over.
