## Summary

Elena says it takes more than two years of joys, torments, ugly surprises, and painful compromises to bring order back to her life. Publicly, however, she prospers. The short book written partly to impress **Nino** is translated into German and English; her earlier book reappears; she writes again for newspapers and magazines. At dinner with the Milan publisher, she learns that **Adele** tried to block the booklet near Christmas and may have limited Italian reviews. Its success, then, belongs to Elena's writing, not Adele's help. This strengthens her sense that dependence is over.

**Lila**, meanwhile, still treats Elena as an appendage of herself. She gets the Genoa number from **Pietro** and calls obsessively, whether Elena is there or not, annoying Adele. When she reaches Elena, she talks without stopping about **Enzo**, work, **Gennaro**, **Carmen**, and **Antonio**. She also forces contact with Nino, apparently through calls to **Eleonora** or to his Naples address, until he agrees to meet her and Carmen.

Nino reports that Lila first asked minute questions about Elena's public appearances — clothes, hair, makeup, shyness, improvisation — then let Carmen speak. Carmen wants Elena to contact **professoressa Galiani** safely about **Pasquale**, since **Nadia** is supposedly abroad. Carmen fears that rich people's children will escape while people like her brother will not. Nino advises Elena to leave both women alone because they are *scriteriate* ("reckless"). Elena is surprised to feel no jealousy over his meeting with Lila; she feels secure enough in Nino's love to tease him instead.
