## Summary

The book opens in Elena's older narrating voice, looking back. From October 1976 until she moved back to Naples in 1979, she deliberately kept her distance from Lila — and it was not easy, because Lila kept trying to force her way back in. Elena ignored her, tolerated her, endured her, all while unable to forget the contempt Lila had shown her.

What had wounded her was not only the insult. When Elena told her on the phone that she was leaving her husband for Nino, Lila had shouted *«sei una cretina»* — "you're an idiot" — something she had never done before. But the words that lodged deeper were about the children: *pensa al male che fai alle tue figlie* — "think of the harm you're doing to your daughters." At the time Elena brushed it off; over the years it gained weight, and she kept circling back to it.

Stung, Elena builds her defence. Lila, she insists, had never shown the slightest interest in **Dede** and **Elsa** — probably didn't even remember their names, cut her off whenever she mentioned something clever they'd done, barely glanced at them when they met at Marcello Solara's house. Only now, out of jealousy because Elena has taken Nino, has Lila suddenly remembered the girls in order to brand her a bad mother. And who is Lila to talk? Elena throws Lila's own record back at her: did she worry about **Gennaro** when she left Stefano, parked the boy with a neighbour for her factory job, shipped him off to Elena as if to be rid of him? Elena admits her own faults — *avevo le mie colpe* — but she is, she tells herself, without doubt more of a mother than Lila ever was.
