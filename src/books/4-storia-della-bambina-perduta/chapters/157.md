## Summary

In Turin everything is ready: Elena has found a house near Ponte Isabella and moved most of her and **Imma's** belongings there. As the train leaves Naples, Imma sits opposite her, melancholy for the first time about what she is leaving. Elena, exhausted, watches the city's outskirts and Vesuvio recede. Suddenly she becomes certain that **Lila**, in writing about Naples, will also write about **Tina**, and that the effort to express an unsayable pain will make the text extraordinary.

That certainty never fades. During Elena's Turin years, while she directs a small but promising publishing house and feels more powerful than **Adele** had once seemed to her, it becomes a wish: Lila will call and say she has a manuscript, a *scartafaccio* or *zibaldone* — a rough bundle of papers or miscellany — for Elena to read and help shape. Elena imagines editing it, perhaps even rewriting it, because Lila's brilliance, memory, and hidden reading are joined to insufficient schooling and no narrative craft. Still, Elena never imagines the text could be sentimental or banal; she is sure it would be dignified.

She questions **Rino**, who often appears at her house for weeks at a time, and discreetly asks Lila whether her passion for Naples and her notes continue. Lila always denies everything: what passion, what notes? She calls herself an old madwoman like **Melina**, and Elena lets the matter drop.
