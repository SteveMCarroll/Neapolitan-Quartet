## Summary

Elena leaves her daughters to manage and eats with **Lila**, though she feels cold to the bone. They keep talking about physical resemblances. Elena also mentions the magazine piece she is writing, telling Lila that talking with her helps her think. Lila is pleased: when she knows she is useful, she feels better. But she soon slides into twisted arguments about having raised a son of **Nino**, just as Elena has raised Imma.

When **Elsa** calls from upstairs that Elena must come back, Elena tells Lila to rest and to call if she needs anything. She then works late into the night. As often happens, even a half-sentence from Lila activates Elena's mind. She writes elegantly about her hip and her mother, proud now to admit that Lila's pushes help her connect distant things. Elena thinks this is adulthood: she can make room for Lila inside herself and give her a durable form. Lila, by contrast, does not want to be herself; this, Elena thinks, lies beneath the illness she calls *smarginatura* — dissolving margins.

The next morning **Dede**'s fever is gone but **Imma** is coughing. Elena finds Lila half-undone, makeup melted, convinced **Pinuccia** has poisoned her. There is blood-soaked toilet paper near the sink. Lila says her belly is splitting and refuses to be examined. Elena realizes this may be real bodily pain, not just old terror. She makes chamomile and runs to the pharmacy, hoping **Gino**'s father will advise her. On the stradone she hears five shots — *pah, pah* — then sirens. Across the street, **Carmen** tells her in dialect: they have killed both Solaras.
