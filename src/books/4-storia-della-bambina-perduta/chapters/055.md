## Summary

**Lila**'s silent accusation turns out to be mostly right. **Enzo** and **Gennaro** come back to the neighborhood that evening, exhausted and shaken like war survivors, and their only concern is Lila. **Nino**, by contrast, reappears several days later as if returning from a holiday. He says he lost his head, gathered his children, and fled.

Elena catches on the phrase "his children." He had rescued the children he has with **Eleonora**, but what about the child Elena is carrying? He explains, in his easy way, that he took refuge with the children, Eleonora, and her parents in a family villa at Minturno. Elena becomes sullen and keeps him away for days. She also worries about her parents, learning from **Marcello**, back alone in the neighborhood, that he has taken them, **Elisa**, and Silvio to safety in Gaeta.

Elena returns alone to the cold apartment in via Tasso. She checks the walls for cracks, is afraid to sleep, and is grateful that **Pietro** and Doriana keep **Dede** and **Elsa** longer. At Christmas she reconciles with Nino and retrieves the girls from Florence, but life resumes like an endless convalescence. Lila, too, seems altered: aggressive as ever, yet with a new uncertainty, hands often spread over her belly. When Elena says the world has returned to its place — *al suo posto* — Lila mocks her: what place?
