## Summary

Until Lila's warning, Elena had barely noticed that **Dede**, unlike **Elsa**, had never displayed a crush. She had explained it as shyness about not feeling beautiful, or as moral severity. Dede condemns frivolity in everyone, especially herself and Elena. But now Elena watches her closely and realizes Dede's protectiveness toward **Rino** is not childish affection or adolescent defense of the humiliated. It is an intense, exclusive bond that has lasted since early childhood.

This frightens Elena. She compares it to her own long love for **Nino**, but with a worse object: Nino had been brilliant and became handsome and successful, while Rino seems insecure, uncultivated, charm-lacking, futureless, and physically more like don Achille than **Stefano**. Elena decides to question Dede directly. Dede answers with complete frankness: yes, she is in love with Rino; she has felt it forever; if he does not return it, her life has no meaning. After her exams she plans to tell him. If he wants her, they will leave, perhaps for Bologna, because it is a place of freedom.

Elena warns that neither she nor **Pietro** would allow it, but Dede says she will simply go. Elena is shaken but tries to behave as if the plan were reasonable. She fears the real confrontation will be with **Lila**, who has lost Tina and has only Rino left. Rino is doing better around Elena's daughters and even working with **Enzo**; taking them away might unbalance him again. Elena becomes more affectionate to him, while he repairs shutters and taps upstairs, but Lila calls him down imperiously whenever he stays too long.
