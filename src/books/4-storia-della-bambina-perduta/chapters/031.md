## Summary

From via Tasso, the old neighbourhood looks like a pale, distant *pietraia* — a stony wasteland — at the foot of Vesuvius. Elena wants it to stay distant, but within days she gives in. She dresses **Dede**, **Elsa**, and herself carefully and announces a visit to her parents and siblings.

The visit is also, secretly, about **Lila**. Elena has not decided how to handle her; chance feels easier than planning. Still, if they meet, she wants Lila to see that she and the girls are elegant, steady, and unharmed. At her parents' apartment, **Immacolata** opens the door looking shockingly thin and aged. She refuses Elena's embrace but is affectionate with the girls, making sugar candies with them and pointedly ignoring Elena.

When they are alone, Elena says she has moved to via Tasso and written another book. Her mother answers with contempt: what does she care? Seeing Elena makes her stomach turn, so she drinks lemon, water, and bicarbonate. Elena worries about her health, but Immacolata blames Elena for making her *schiattare in corpo* — burst inside. She orders Elena never to show herself there again if she has come to Naples without her husband.

Elena tries to defend separation and her work, even comparing herself to pregnant, unmarried **Elisa**. Immacolata says Elisa has become a respectable signora, while educated Elena has ended up with Sarratore's son. Worst, she praises Lina: beautiful, generous, owner of a business with **Enzo**, commanding even **Michele Solara**. Elena leaves bitterly, understanding that her mother's deepest accusation is that she is worth less than Lila.
