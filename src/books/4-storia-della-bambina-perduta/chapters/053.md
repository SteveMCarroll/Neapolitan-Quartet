## Summary

After **Lila**'s long confession, **Elena** keeps answering yes and telling her to rest. She holds Lila close until she falls asleep, then stays awake watching her, as Lila had once asked her to do. Small aftershocks still seem to pass through the parked cars, and people cry out in fear. Elena feels her own unborn child move like a sloshing inside her belly, then touches Lila's belly and feels that child moving too.

Under the influence of Lila's disturbed words, Elena imagines everything in motion: fire under the earth's crust, stars, planets, universes, light inside darkness. Yet she realizes that fear cannot take root in her in the same way. Even lava and molten matter arrange themselves in her mind as ordered phrases and harmonious images. She knows how to give herself weight — *darmi peso*, to make herself matter — whatever happens.

This contrast makes Elena proud, calm, and tender. Everything that has happened to her — study, books, **Franco**, **Pietro**, the girls, **Nino**, the earthquake — will pass, but some version of Elena will remain fixed, like the point of a compass while the pencil circles around it. Lila, Elena thinks, cannot feel stable in that way. However much she dominates others, she experiences herself as a flow that she is always struggling to contain. When that flow prevails, Lila loses Lila and becomes nothing.
