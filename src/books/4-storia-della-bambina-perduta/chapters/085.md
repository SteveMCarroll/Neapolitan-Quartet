## Summary

Elena knows many people wonder why a writer who looks rich and famous in the newspapers has moved into a miserable apartment in a decaying area. **Dede** and **Elsa** do not understand it either: one reports an old man urinating in their doorway, the other a stabbing near the gardens. Elena worries that while she works on literature, the rione may damage her daughters. She calms herself by insisting the stay is temporary, only until the novel is finished.

The book is improving because Elena is watching **Lila**. Lila's voice, dialect, generosity, cruelty, and even Basic Sight — locally pronounced *basissìt* — seem rooted in the neighborhood's poverty and violence. In Lila's office, Elena observes the whole background: **Rino** consumed by drugs, **Ada** embittered against **Marisa**, **Alfonso** injured and unsettling, **Carmen** treating Lila like an oracle, **Antonio** hovering silently, and endless rumors about **Stefano**, **Pasquale**, the Solaras, drugs, murders, and **Gennaro**.

Waiting one day with **Imma** and **Tina**, Elena hears Lila curse a client over unpaid money. Shaken, she asks who Lila really is in such an environment. Lila says she pays attention and moves things to make them go her way. Elena argues for *la legge* — the law — but Lila laughs: where people do not fear the law, you must make them fear you. When justice failed after an accountant stole from them, she asked Antonio; the money came back.
