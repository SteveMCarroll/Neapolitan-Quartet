## Summary

After **Rino**'s death, **Lila** appears briefly improved: her narrowed alertness relaxes and her face softens. Soon, however, she ages abruptly, with disordered wrinkles, a bent back, and a swollen belly. **Carmen** says **Tina** has become *incistata* — cysted inside her — and they need to remove her. Elena agrees that they must somehow make the child's story flow again.

But Lila keeps everything about Tina frozen. Elena suspects something moves only in secret, with **Antonio** and **Enzo**. When Antonio suddenly leaves with his blond family and elderly **Melina**, Lila loses even his mysterious reports. She is left to attack Enzo and **Gennaro**, or to wait in distraction. Elena visits daily, trying to restore intimacy. Lila says she never liked computer work; she only made herself like it because Enzo did. When Elena urges her to leave grief behind, Lila corrects her: they must leave *la rabbia* — rage — not pain.

Lila constantly turns Elena's attempts back toward **Dede**, **Elsa**, and **Imma**, accusing Elena of neglect. Elena accepts some of this, but keeps trying to push Lila toward Tina. She notices Lila's gray pallor and menstrual bleeding, but Lila deflects. Elena wonders if language is the barrier: Lila uses Italian as armor, while Elena's dialect is now artificial. Elena longs for Lila to burst out in the true Neapolitan of childhood and confess unbearable possibilities — Tina alive elsewhere, or dead and killing Lila from within. But Lila never does. Elena suspects that what she wants to hear may be only her own feelings, and that Lila contains something even more unspeakable.
