**Nino** arrives in Book 4 as the grand passion for whom **Elena** breaks her family. He offers glamour, political talk, travel, and the illusion that their love stands outside ordinary damage. Yet he never truly frees himself from his wife Eleonora, expects Elena to carry the domestic burden, and treats their daughter **Imma** as proof of himself when useful.

The illusion collapses when Elena finds him with Silvana in the bathroom mirror and recognizes in him the sordid continuity of his father **Donato**. Nino's public career still rises — he campaigns, is elected, cultivates useful relations — but his private life grows smaller and less credible. By the epilogue he is swollen, loud, and self-celebrating, a man Elena can hardly bear to see: brilliant enough to have dazzled her, never steady enough to deserve the life she risked for him.
