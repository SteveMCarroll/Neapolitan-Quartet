**Donato Sarratore** is no longer the polished railwayman-poet of Elena's girlhood. In book 4 he appears as an old, shabby, obscene presence at the Sarratore lunch where **Nino** tries to reassure Elena that she is his true wife. Donato praises Elena as a great writer and claims his own poems awakened her vocation; Elena indulges him just enough to make him glow, then hears Nino rebuke her for giving him rope.

Donato's real force is memory. After Elena sees Nino's sordidness clearly, Nino's face calls up Donato touching her on Ischia. Father and son fuse into one moral pattern. In the epilogue Elena notes that Donato has died, a funeral she missed while abroad.
